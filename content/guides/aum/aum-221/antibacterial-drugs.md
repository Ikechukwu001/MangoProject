## What are antibiotics?

**Antibiotics** (antibacterial drugs) are medicines that **kill or stop the growth of bacteria**. The term first meant substances made by microbes, such as penicillin. Today it includes semi-synthetic and synthetic drugs.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bactericidal | **Kills** bacteria (for example penicillins, aminoglycosides) |
| Bacteriostatic | **Stops bacteria multiplying** so the body's defences can clear them (for example tetracyclines, macrolides) |
| Broad-spectrum | Works against **many** types of bacteria |
| Narrow-spectrum | Works against **few** types of bacteria |

Antibiotics have **no effect on viruses**. Colds, flu, most sore throats and most coughs are viral, so antibiotics do not help.

## Main groups

| Group | Examples | Main uses | Cautions and common side effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penicillins | Amoxicillin, ampicillin, flucloxacillin, benzylpenicillin | Chest, ear and throat infections, urinary infections, skin infections | **Allergy** (rash, swelling, anaphylaxis). Diarrhoea. Always ask about penicillin allergy |
| Cephalosporins | Cefuroxime, cefixime, ceftriaxone | Many infections, including more serious ones | Some patients allergic to penicillin also react to these. Diarrhoea |
| Macrolides | Erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin | Chest and throat infections, and a choice for patients allergic to penicillin | Nausea, stomach upset, interactions with other drugs |
| Tetracyclines | Doxycycline, tetracycline | Chest infections, acne, some tropical infections | **Not in pregnancy or in children** (teeth). Avoid milk, antacids and iron at the same time. Sun sensitivity. Take sitting upright with water |
| Aminoglycosides | Gentamicin, amikacin | Serious infections, **by injection** | Damage to **hearing and kidneys**. Blood levels monitored |
| Quinolones | Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin | Urinary, gut and some chest infections | Tendon problems, nausea, dizziness. Avoid antacids, iron and dairy at the same time. Not for young children unless specialist advice |
| Sulfonamides and co-trimoxazole | Co-trimoxazole (sulfamethoxazole with trimethoprim) | Urinary and chest infections, some other infections | Rash, allergy to sulfa drugs. Drink plenty of fluids. Avoid in pregnancy, as advised |
| Nitroimidazoles | Metronidazole, tinidazole | Anaerobic infections, amoebiasis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis | **Avoid alcohol.** Metallic taste, nausea |
| Chloramphenicol | Chloramphenicol | Rare serious infections, eye drops and ointment | Can harm **bone marrow**, and cause "grey baby syndrome" in newborns. Oral and injection use is limited |
| Lincosamides | Clindamycin | Bone, skin and anaerobic infections | Can cause severe **diarrhoea** (colitis) |

**Note.** Medicines for **tuberculosis** (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol) are a special group. TB treatment is long, is given through the national programme, and **must be completed**.

## How antibiotics act (simple view)

- **Penicillins and cephalosporins** damage the **cell wall** of bacteria, so the bacteria burst.
- **Tetracyclines, macrolides and aminoglycosides** block bacterial **protein making**.
- **Quinolones** block bacterial **DNA copying**.
- **Sulfonamides** block bacterial **folic acid** production.

Human cells do not have these targets, so the drugs can be fairly safe for us, but they may still cause side effects.

## Antibiotic resistance

**Resistance** is the ability of bacteria to **survive an antibiotic** that used to kill them. It is one of the greatest threats to health in the world.

### Causes

- Using antibiotics for **viral infections**.
- **Not finishing** the course, or taking too small a dose.
- **Taking leftover or shared** antibiotics, or buying them without a prescription.
- **Using a broad-spectrum** antibiotic when a narrow one would do.
- Use in **animals and farming**.
- Poor **infection control** and hygiene.

### How a technician helps

- **Do not supply** antibiotics without a valid prescription where the law requires one.
- **Explain** that antibiotics do not work for colds and flu.
- **Counsel** on finishing the course and on timing.
- **Encourage** hygiene, handwashing and **vaccination**.
- **Report** unusual requests to the pharmacist.

## Counselling for every antibiotic

1. **Finish the whole course**, even if you feel better.
2. **Take at the right times**, spaced evenly through the day.
3. **Follow food advice**: some need food, some need an empty stomach.
4. **Do not share** antibiotics, and **do not save** leftovers.
5. Know the **common side effects**, and **stop and seek help** if there is a rash, swelling of the face, or difficulty breathing.
6. **Diarrhoea** that is severe or bloody needs medical review.
7. If you have a **penicillin allergy**, tell every health worker.
8. Women taking the oral contraceptive pill should ask the pharmacist about **extra precautions** when vomiting or severe diarrhoea occurs, or when taking rifampicin, which weakens the pill.
9. Keep suspensions as the label says, usually in a **refrigerator**, and discard when the discard date passes.
