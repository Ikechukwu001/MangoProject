## Disorders of muscle, bone and joint

| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| Muscle spasm and strain | Painful tight muscles, from injury or back pain |
| Osteoarthritis | Wear of joint cartilage, with pain and stiffness, common in older age |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | A long-term disease in which the body's defences attack the joints, causing swelling, pain and stiffness |
| Gout | Painful, swollen joints (often the big toe) caused by uric acid crystals |
| Osteoporosis | Thin, weak bones that break easily, common after the menopause |

## Muscle relaxants

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Baclofen | For muscle spasticity (for example in spinal injury or multiple sclerosis). Drowsiness, weakness. Do not stop suddenly |
| Methocarbamol, orphenadrine | For painful muscle spasm. Drowsiness, dry mouth |
| Diazepam | A sedative that also relaxes muscle. Dependence risk. Short use only |
| Tizanidine | Spasticity. Drowsiness, low blood pressure |

**Counselling:** these cause **drowsiness**, so **avoid driving and alcohol**. Rest, heat and physiotherapy also help.

## Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)

**NSAIDs** reduce **pain, swelling and inflammation**, and also lower fever. They block **cyclo-oxygenase (COX)** enzymes, which make prostaglandins.

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | Lowest stomach risk of the common NSAIDs at usual doses |
| Diclofenac | Tablets, injection, gel and suppositories. Some heart risk with long use |
| Naproxen | Longer acting |
| Piroxicam | Long-acting. Higher stomach risk |
| Celecoxib, etoricoxib (COX-2 inhibitors) | Less stomach irritation, but heart cautions |
| Aspirin | Also used in low dose to prevent clots. Not for children under 16 |

### Side effects and cautions

- **Stomach irritation, ulcers and bleeding**: take **with or after food**. Report **black stools** or **vomiting blood**.
- **Kidney harm**, particularly in the elderly, those who are dehydrated, and those on water tablets.
- **Raise blood pressure** and may worsen **heart failure**.
- Can **trigger asthma** in sensitive patients.
- **Avoid** in the **last 3 months of pregnancy**, and in **active ulcers**.
- **Do not combine** two NSAIDs.
- Use the **lowest effective dose for the shortest time**. Topical gels give less effect on the rest of the body.

## Disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs) and steroids

For **rheumatoid arthritis**, NSAIDs relieve symptoms but do **not** slow the disease. **DMARDs** slow joint damage.

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Methotrexate | Taken **once a week**, not daily, because of the risk of fatal overdose. Needs blood tests. Folic acid is often added. Not in pregnancy |
| Sulfasalazine | Gut upset, rash. Needs blood tests |
| Hydroxychloroquine | Eye checks needed |
| Corticosteroids (prednisolone) | Reduce inflammation quickly. Long use causes side effects. Do not stop suddenly |

DMARDs are started and monitored by a **doctor**. The pharmacist checks that the patient understands the **weekly dose** of methotrexate.

## Gout

| Aim | Medicines |
|---|---|
| Treat an acute attack | **NSAIDs** (for example naproxen or diclofenac), **colchicine**, or a short steroid course |
| Prevent attacks | **Allopurinol** (lowers uric acid), or others such as febuxostat |

- **Colchicine:** diarrhoea and nausea are common. Do not exceed the stated dose.
- **Allopurinol:** **do not start during an acute attack**. Start after it settles. Drink plenty of fluids. Report rash.
- **Lifestyle:** reduce alcohol (especially beer), red meat and organ meats, maintain a healthy weight, and drink water.
- **Aspirin** is avoided because it raises uric acid.

## Osteoporosis

| Medicine | Notes |
|---|---|
| Calcium and vitamin D | The base of treatment and prevention |
| Bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate) | Slow bone loss. Taken **weekly** or daily, **on an empty stomach with a full glass of plain water**, then the patient must **stay upright for at least 30 minutes** and wait before food. Can irritate the gullet |
| Others | Hormone-related and injectable drugs, started by specialists |

Also advise **weight-bearing exercise**, **stopping smoking**, **less alcohol**, and **preventing falls**.

## Topical treatments

- **NSAID gels** (diclofenac, ibuprofen, piroxicam) are applied to the sore area.
- **Rubefacients** such as methyl salicylate and capsaicin produce warmth.
- **Do not apply** to broken skin, and **wash hands** after use. Do not use with heat pads or tight bandages.

## When to refer

- A **hot, red, swollen joint** with fever
- Pain after a **fall** or injury
- **Back pain** with leg weakness, numbness or loss of bladder control
- Pain that lasts **more than a few weeks**
