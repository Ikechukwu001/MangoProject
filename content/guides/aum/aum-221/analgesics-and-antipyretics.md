## Definitions

- An **analgesic** is a medicine that **relieves pain**.
- An **antipyretic** is a medicine that **lowers fever**.
- Many drugs do both, for example paracetamol, ibuprofen and aspirin.

Pain and fever are **symptoms**. The cause should be found if they persist.

## Paracetamol

- **Pain relief and fever reduction**, with **little anti-inflammatory effect**.
- Gentle on the **stomach**, so often **first choice**, including in **children** and **pregnancy** (at the usual dose).
- Available as **tablets, syrup, suppositories** and injection.

### Safety

- **Overdose** can cause **severe liver damage**, which may not show for 1 to 2 days. Even a moderate overdose can be dangerous, and the risk is greater in **children**, in **low body weight**, in **liver disease**, with **regular alcohol**, and in **malnutrition**.
- The **adult daily maximum is usually 4 g (eight 500 mg tablets) in 24 hours**. Doses for children are **by weight**. Use the limit on the label and the guidance from the pharmacist.
- **Do not take more than one product containing paracetamol** at once. Many cold and flu remedies contain it.
- Overdose is a **medical emergency**, even if the patient feels well. The antidote is **acetylcysteine**, which works best when started early.

## NSAIDs

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | Pain, fever, inflammation. Often the first NSAID chosen |
| Diclofenac | Stronger. Tablets, gel, suppositories, injection |
| Naproxen | Longer acting |
| Mefenamic acid | Period pain, and other pain |
| Aspirin | Pain and fever in adults. Low dose to prevent clots |

For side effects and cautions, see the lesson on drugs for muscle, bone and joint disease. In brief: **take with food**, avoid in **active ulcers**, **kidney disease**, **late pregnancy**, and in **asthma** that worsens with NSAIDs, and **do not combine** NSAIDs.

### Aspirin

- Used for pain and fever in adults, and in **low doses** to **help prevent heart attacks and strokes**.
- **Do not give to children under 16**, or to anyone with a viral illness, because of **Reye's syndrome**.
- **Do not use** in **dengue-like illness** or when bleeding is possible.
- Avoid in **gout**, in the **last 3 months of pregnancy**, and with **anticoagulants** unless advised.

## Opioid analgesics

Opioids act on **opioid receptors in the brain and spinal cord**. They are covered in more detail in AUM 222.

| Strength | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weak opioids | Codeine, tramadol, dihydrocodeine | Moderate pain. Often combined with paracetamol |
| Strong opioids | Morphine, pethidine, fentanyl, oxycodone | Severe pain, such as after surgery and in cancer |

**Side effects:** **drowsiness**, **constipation**, nausea, itching and, in overdose, **slow breathing**. **Tolerance and dependence** can develop. These are **controlled drugs**, supplied only on a valid prescription, with exact records and secure storage. The antidote for opioid overdose is **naloxone**.

## The WHO analgesic ladder

| Step | Pain | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mild | **Non-opioids**: paracetamol or an NSAID |
| 2 | Moderate, or pain not relieved by step 1 | **Weak opioid** (for example codeine) **with** a non-opioid |
| 3 | Severe | **Strong opioid** (for example morphine) **with** a non-opioid |

Adjuvants (such as medicines for nerve pain) may be added at any step. Doses are given **regularly**, **by mouth** when possible, and **adjusted for the individual**.

## Managing fever

Fever is a **sign**, usually of infection, and is not itself an illness. The aim is **comfort**.

- **Paracetamol** or **ibuprofen** to lower temperature and relieve discomfort.
- **Drink plenty of fluids.**
- **Light clothing**, and a cool, ventilated room. **Tepid sponging** is optional. Do not use cold water or alcohol.
- **Treat the cause**: in a malaria area, **test for malaria**.
- **Do not give aspirin** to children.

### Refer if

- A **baby under 3 months** has a fever.
- **Fever lasts more than 3 days**, or goes above 39 °C in a child.
- There are **convulsions, stiff neck, rash that does not fade, difficulty breathing, severe headache, drowsiness**, or **signs of dehydration**.
- The patient is **pregnant**, **elderly** or has a **long-term illness**.

## Counselling summary

| Medicine | Key advice |
|---|---|
| Paracetamol | Do not exceed the daily limit. Check other products for paracetamol |
| Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs | Take with food. Avoid in ulcer, kidney disease and late pregnancy |
| Aspirin | Not for children under 16 |
| Codeine and other opioids | Drowsiness. No alcohol, no driving. Constipation. Keep secure |
| All | Do not use for more than a few days without advice |
