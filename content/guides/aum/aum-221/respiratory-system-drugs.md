## Types of cough

| Type | Description | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Productive (chesty) cough | Brings up phlegm | Help clear mucus: fluids, expectorants or mucolytics |
| Dry (unproductive) cough | No phlegm, often irritating | A cough suppressant may help |

**Refer** a patient who has a cough lasting more than **2 to 3 weeks**, coughing **blood**, **weight loss**, **night sweats** (think of tuberculosis), **breathlessness**, **chest pain**, or a **fever lasting several days**. Children under a certain age and pregnant women need the pharmacist's advice before any cough product.

## Expectorants and mucolytics

| Group | Examples | How they work |
|---|---|---|
| Expectorants | Guaifenesin, ammonium chloride | Said to increase the amount of fluid in the airways, making mucus easier to cough up. Evidence of benefit is limited |
| Mucolytics | Carbocisteine, acetylcysteine, bromhexine, ambroxol | Break up or thin the mucus |

**Advice:** drink plenty of fluids, and avoid giving cough products to very young children unless the pharmacist agrees.

## Cough suppressants (antitussives)

- Examples: **dextromethorphan**, **codeine**, **pholcodine**.
- They act on the **cough centre in the brain** and reduce the urge to cough.
- Used for **dry, troublesome coughs**, **not productive coughs**.
- **Side effects:** drowsiness, constipation (codeine), and dependence with misuse.
- **Not for young children.** Codeine can be dangerous in children and in some breast-feeding mothers.

A **simple linctus** soothes the throat and may help a dry cough.

## Antihistamines

**Antihistamines** block histamine and relieve **allergic rhinitis (hay fever), itching, rash and hives**.

| Type | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sedating (first generation) | Chlorpheniramine, promethazine | Cause **drowsiness**. Do not drive. Avoid alcohol |
| Non-sedating (second generation) | Loratadine, cetirizine | Cause less drowsiness |

Also used for **nausea, travel sickness** (promethazine) and as **sedatives**. **Caution** in glaucoma and prostate enlargement.

## Nasal decongestants

- **Topical** (drops, sprays): **xylometazoline**, oxymetazoline. Fast, but cause **rebound congestion** if used more than **5 to 7 days**.
- **Oral**: **pseudoephedrine**. May raise **blood pressure** and heart rate. Not for patients with high blood pressure or heart disease.

## Bronchodilators and asthma

**Asthma** is a condition in which the **airways become inflamed and narrow**, causing **wheeze, cough, chest tightness and breathlessness**.

| Group | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABA) | **Salbutamol**, terbutaline | **Relievers**: open the airways within minutes, for quick relief |
| Long-acting beta-2 agonists (LABA) | Salmeterol, formoterol | Used regularly **with an inhaled steroid**, not alone |
| Anticholinergics | Ipratropium | Open airways. Used in asthma and COPD |
| Xanthines | Theophylline, aminophylline | Open airways. **Narrow safety margin**, many interactions |

**Side effects of beta-2 agonists:** **tremor**, **fast heartbeat**, headache.

### Relievers and preventers

| | Reliever | Preventer |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Salbutamol | Beclometasone, budesonide (inhaled steroids) |
| Use | When symptoms occur | **Every day**, even when well |
| Effect | Quick opening of airways | Reduces inflammation over days to weeks |
| Warning sign | Using the reliever **more than usual** means asthma is poorly controlled: **see a doctor** | Do not stop suddenly |

## Corticosteroids

- **Inhaled** (beclometasone, budesonide, fluticasone): **preventers**, with few effects on the body.
  - **Rinse the mouth** and spit after use. This lowers **oral thrush** and **hoarseness**.
- **Oral** (prednisolone) and **injectable** (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone): for **severe attacks**, used for short courses.
  - Long courses cause weight gain, raised blood sugar and blood pressure, weak bones and infection risk. **Do not stop suddenly** after long use.

## Inhaler technique

1. **Shake** the inhaler (for aerosol types).
2. **Breathe out** fully.
3. **Seal the lips** around the mouthpiece.
4. **Press** the canister as you **breathe in slowly and deeply**.
5. **Hold the breath** for about 10 seconds.
6. **Wait** about a minute before the next puff.
7. Use a **spacer** for children and anyone who finds it hard to coordinate.
8. **Keep the inhaler clean**, and **check the dose counter**.

## When to refer

- A **severe asthma attack**: difficulty speaking, blue lips, no relief from the reliever. This is an **emergency**.
- **Frequent** use of a reliever.
- **Cough or wheeze in a young child.**
- Any **cough lasting more than 2 to 3 weeks.**
