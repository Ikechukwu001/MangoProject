## Antacids

**Antacids** are **weak bases** that **neutralise stomach acid**. They relieve **heartburn, indigestion and acid reflux**, with quick but short action (an hour or two).

| Antacid | Notes |
|---|---|
| Aluminium hydroxide | Tends to cause **constipation** |
| Magnesium hydroxide, magnesium trisilicate | Tend to cause **diarrhoea** |
| Calcium carbonate | Fast, but may cause constipation and "acid rebound" |
| Sodium bicarbonate | Very fast, but adds sodium, and gas. Not for long use |
| Mixtures (for example aluminium and magnesium) | Balance the effects on the bowel |

Often combined with **simeticone** (for wind) or **alginates** (a raft that stops reflux).

**Counselling**

- Take **after meals and at bedtime**, or when symptoms occur.
- **Chew** tablets well, or shake liquids.
- Antacids **reduce the absorption** of many drugs (such as tetracyclines, ciprofloxacin and iron), so **take other medicines at least 2 hours apart**.
- Care in **kidney disease**, **heart failure** (sodium) and **pregnancy**: ask the pharmacist.
- Symptoms that **persist**, or are accompanied by **weight loss, vomiting, black stools or difficulty swallowing**, need a doctor.

## Antispasmodics

**Antispasmodics** **relax the smooth muscle of the gut** and relieve **cramps and colic**.

- Examples: **hyoscine butylbromide**, **dicycloverine**, **mebeverine**.
- Many are **anticholinergic**: they block acetylcholine.
- **Side effects:** **dry mouth**, **blurred vision**, **constipation**, **difficulty passing urine**, fast pulse.
- **Cautions:** **glaucoma**, enlarged prostate, and bowel obstruction.
- Warn about **driving** if vision is blurred.

## Ulcer-healing drugs

A peptic ulcer forms when acid and pepsin damage the lining of the stomach or duodenum. Common causes are the germ ***Helicobacter pylori***, **NSAIDs** and smoking.

| Group | Examples | How they work |
|---|---|---|
| Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) | Omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole | Block the "pump" that makes acid. The **most effective** acid reducers |
| H2-receptor blockers | Famotidine, cimetidine (ranitidine has been withdrawn in many countries) | Block histamine receptors on acid-producing cells |
| Mucosal protectants | Sucralfate, bismuth | Form a protective layer over the ulcer |
| Prostaglandin analogues | Misoprostol | Protect the lining. **Must not be used in pregnancy** |
| *H. pylori* eradication | A PPI with two antibiotics, such as amoxicillin with clarithromycin or metronidazole | Kills the germ, so the ulcer does not return |

**Counselling**

- **PPIs** are best taken **before food**, usually in the morning. Swallow whole.
- Complete the full **antibiotic course** in eradication therapy.
- Avoid **smoking**, **alcohol** and **NSAIDs** where possible.
- Report **black stools, vomiting blood or severe pain** at once.

## Antidiarrhoeals

**Diarrhoea** is the passing of **loose or watery stools** three or more times a day. The main danger is **loss of water and salts (dehydration)**, especially in children.

### Treatment

1. **Oral rehydration salts (ORS)** to replace fluid and salts. Mixed with the correct amount of **clean water**, and sipped often.
2. **Zinc** for **10 to 14 days** in children, as advised, which shortens the illness and lowers the risk of another attack.
3. **Continue feeding**, and breast-feeding for babies.
4. **Antibiotics** only for **bloody diarrhoea (dysentery)**, cholera or other proven causes, as prescribed.
5. **Refer** for danger signs.

### Medicines to reduce the diarrhoea

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Loperamide | Slows gut movement. For **adults** with simple diarrhoea. **Not for children**, and not for **bloody diarrhoea, high fever or suspected infection**. Not in pregnancy without advice |
| Kaolin and similar | Bulk and absorb, but of little proven value |
| Codeine and similar opioids | Slow the gut, but can cause dependence. Not first-line |

### Danger signs: refer at once

- Blood in the stool
- High fever
- Signs of dehydration: sunken eyes, dry mouth, very little urine, drowsiness, a skin pinch that goes back slowly
- Vomiting that prevents drinking
- A baby under 6 months
- Diarrhoea lasting more than a few days
