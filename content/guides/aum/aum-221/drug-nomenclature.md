## One medicine, several names

The same medicine can have **several names**. It helps to know each type.

| Type of name | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical name | Describes the **chemical structure**. Long and technical | N-acetyl-para-aminophenol |
| Generic (approved, non-proprietary) name | The **official name of the active drug**, the same everywhere | Paracetamol |
| Brand (proprietary, trade) name | The name **chosen by a manufacturer** for its product, protected as a trademark | Panadol |

A **proprietary name** is a name that belongs to a company. A **proprietary medicine** is a product sold under such a name.

Examples:

| Generic name | Some brand names |
|---|---|
| Paracetamol | Panadol, and many local brands |
| Artemether-lumefantrine | Coartem, and many other brands |
| Salbutamol | Ventolin |
| Metronidazole | Flagyl |
| Diclofenac | Voltaren |
| Amoxicillin-clavulanic acid | Augmentin |

Some generic names differ between countries. For example, **paracetamol** is called **acetaminophen** in the USA. Pharmacopoeias and international bodies work to standardise names.

## Generic and branded products

| | Generic product | Branded product |
|---|---|---|
| Name on label | The generic name | The brand name (usually with the generic name in small print) |
| Active drug | Same | Same |
| Price | Often lower | Often higher |
| Source | Many manufacturers | One manufacturer (originator) or others |

A product sold under the generic name contains the same active drug as the brand, and it must meet **quality standards**. Differences in **price, appearance, taste** and **inactive ingredients** may exist.

## Why generic names are preferred

- They are **standard**, so a doctor, a pharmacist and a nurse all mean the same drug.
- They **show what the drug is**, and often show its **class**. For example, drugs ending in **-cillin** are penicillins and drugs ending in **-azole** are often antifungals or antiprotozoals.
- They allow **choice of a cheaper equivalent**, reducing cost.
- They **avoid duplication** when patients use several brands.
- They are used in **essential medicines lists** and **treatment guidelines**.

## Problems with names

| Problem | Example | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate therapy | A patient takes a paracetamol tablet and a cold remedy that also contains paracetamol | Always check the **active ingredients**, and tell patients to do the same |
| Look-alike, sound-alike names | Two different drugs with similar names, such as those that differ by a few letters | Read labels carefully, use the generic name, and ask when in doubt |
| Different brands for different strengths | The same brand name used for a different strength or form | Always check strength and form |
| Counterfeit products | A fake product that carries a real brand name | Buy from approved suppliers, and report suspect products |

## Practical rules for a technician

1. **Check the generic name** on the label of every product.
2. When a patient asks for a brand, **find the generic name** and the strength.
3. Ask the patient about **other medicines** they use, including over-the-counter products.
4. Do not **substitute** one product for another without the pharmacist's approval.
5. Write the **generic name** in your records, along with the brand if supplied.
