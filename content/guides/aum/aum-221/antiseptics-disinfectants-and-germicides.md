## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antiseptic | A substance applied to **living tissue** (skin, wounds, mucous membrane) to **reduce or stop the growth of germs** |
| Disinfectant | A substance used on **non-living objects** and surfaces to **destroy germs**. Too strong for living tissue |
| Germicide | A substance that **kills germs**. Includes both antiseptics and disinfectants |
| Bactericide, fungicide, virucide | Kills bacteria, fungi or viruses |
| Bacteriostatic | Stops bacteria from multiplying without killing them |
| Sterilisation | **Destroys all living microorganisms**, including spores |
| Preservative | Added to a product to stop germs growing in it |

### Comparison with antibiotics

| | Antiseptic or disinfectant | Antibiotic |
|---|---|---|
| Used on | Skin, wounds, surfaces, equipment | Inside the body |
| Action | Local | Reaches the infection through the blood |
| Selectivity | Often harm human cells too | Selectively harms bacteria |
| Taken by mouth | **No** | Yes |

## Antiseptics

| Antiseptic | Uses and notes |
|---|---|
| Chlorhexidine | Skin and hand washing, wounds, mouthwash, umbilical cord care. Long action. Avoid contact with eyes and ears |
| Povidone-iodine | Skin before surgery, and wounds. Stains skin. Avoid in iodine allergy and in some thyroid disease. Avoid in newborns unless advised |
| Hydrogen peroxide | Cleans wounds and acts as a mouthwash. Fizzes. Weak, and delays healing if overused |
| Alcohol (ethanol, isopropanol) 70% | Hand rubs, skin before injection. Dries the skin. Flammable. 70% works better than 95% |
| Cetrimide | Cleaning of skin and wounds. Often in combination with chlorhexidine |
| Chloroxylenol | Dilute household antiseptics for skin and cuts |
| Gentian violet | Mouth and skin fungal infections, stains |
| Potassium permanganate (dilute) | Soaks for weeping skin conditions and ulcers. Stains. Use only well diluted |

## Disinfectants

| Disinfectant | Uses and notes |
|---|---|
| Chlorine compounds (sodium hypochlorite, bleach, bleaching powder) | Surfaces, spills of blood, floors, water treatment. Corrodes metals. Irritating. Do not mix with acids or other cleaners |
| Phenols and cresols | Floors and drains. Strong smell. Toxic. Do not use on skin or near infants |
| Glutaraldehyde | Cold disinfection of instruments such as endoscopes. Irritant. Use in a ventilated area |
| Formaldehyde | Disinfecting rooms and equipment. Irritant. Harmful vapour |
| Alcohol 70% | Small surfaces and equipment |
| Quaternary ammonium compounds | Surfaces and equipment. Inactivated by soap |

## Factors affecting the action

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Concentration | Too weak: not effective. Too strong: wasteful and may damage tissue |
| Contact time | The germs must be exposed long enough |
| Temperature | Higher temperature usually increases effect |
| Organic matter | Blood, pus and dirt protect germs, so **clean first** |
| pH | Some agents work best at a certain pH |
| Type of germ | Spores, TB bacteria and some viruses are harder to kill |
| Dilution errors | A common cause of failure |
| Age of dilution | Some, such as diluted bleach, lose strength with time, so make fresh each day |

## Dilution

```
strength wanted × volume wanted = strength of stock × volume of stock
```

### Worked example

*Commercial bleach contains 5% sodium hypochlorite. Make 1 litre (1 000 mL) of a 0.05% solution.*

1. 0.05 × 1 000 = 5 × V
2. V = 50 ÷ 5 = **10 mL** of bleach
3. Add water to **1 000 mL**

*Make 1 litre of 0.5% solution for blood spills.*

1. 0.5 × 1 000 = 5 × V
2. V = 500 ÷ 5 = **100 mL** of bleach, made up to 1 000 mL

Use the strengths in your **local infection-control guidelines**.

## Safety rules

- **Read the label** and use the **stated dilution**. Do not guess.
- **Wear gloves** and eye protection when handling concentrates.
- **Never mix** different disinfectants, especially **bleach with acids or ammonia**, which gives toxic gas.
- **Label** every container, and **never** put in drink bottles.
- Keep **out of the reach of children**.
- **Do not use** disinfectants on skin or wounds.
- **Do not swallow.** If swallowed, **seek help at once**, and do not induce vomiting unless advised.
- **Store** in a cool, well-ventilated place away from light and flammables.
- **Clean surfaces first**, then disinfect.
- **Make fresh** dilutions as needed, and discard unused ones as advised.

## First aid use of antiseptics

For a small cut or graze:

1. **Wash** hands, then **clean** the wound with clean water.
2. **Apply** a suitable antiseptic.
3. **Cover** with a clean dressing.
4. **Seek help** for deep, dirty or bleeding wounds, animal bites, and wounds in people with diabetes or no tetanus protection.
