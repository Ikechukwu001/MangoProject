## Dose and dosage form

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | The **amount of a medicine** given at one time | 500 mg of paracetamol |
| Dosage regimen | The dose, **how often**, and **for how long** | 500 mg three times a day for 5 days |
| Dosage form | The **physical form** of the medicine | Tablet, capsule, syrup, injection, cream |
| Therapeutic effect | The **wanted** effect that the medicine is given for | Pain relief from paracetamol |

Other dose terms you may meet:

- **Minimum effective dose:** the smallest dose that gives the wanted effect.
- **Maximum (toxic) dose:** the highest dose that can be given without harm. Above it, toxicity appears.
- **Loading dose:** a larger first dose to reach an effective level quickly.
- **Maintenance dose:** the regular dose that keeps the level steady.
- **Lethal dose:** a dose that can cause death.

## Side effects and adverse reactions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Side effect | An effect **other than the wanted one**, which occurs at normal doses. May be mild, such as drowsiness, or serious |
| Adverse drug reaction (ADR) | Any **harmful and unwanted** response to a medicine at normal doses |
| Toxic effect | Harm from **too much** drug, or from a drug that builds up |

Examples:

- **Drowsiness** with some antihistamines
- **Stomach irritation** with NSAIDs such as ibuprofen
- **Dry cough** with some blood pressure medicines
- **Nausea** with many antibiotics

Patients should be told the **common** side effects and **what to do** about them. **Serious** reactions must be reported to the pharmacist or doctor at once. Reporting adverse reactions is part of **pharmacovigilance**.

## Contraindication

A **contraindication** is a **condition or situation in which a medicine should not be used**, because it may cause harm.

| Medicine | Contraindication (example) |
|---|---|
| Aspirin and NSAIDs | Active stomach ulcer |
| Penicillins | Known penicillin allergy |
| Many medicines | Pregnancy, when a drug may harm the baby |
| Metformin | Severe kidney disease |
| Medicines that cause drowsiness | Driving, for the patient's safety (a caution) |

- An **absolute** contraindication means the medicine must **never** be used.
- A **relative** contraindication means the medicine may be used **with great care**, if the benefit outweighs the risk, and the doctor decides.

A **precaution** or **caution** is a lesser warning: the medicine may be used but needs care.

## Sensitivity, hypersensitivity, allergy and idiosyncrasy

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sensitivity | How **responsive** a person is to a drug. Some people respond to small doses |
| Hypersensitivity | An **exaggerated** reaction to a drug, often involving the **immune system** |
| Allergy | A **hypersensitivity reaction by the immune system**, such as a rash, itching, swelling or difficulty breathing |
| Anaphylaxis | A **severe, life-threatening** allergic reaction needing emergency treatment |
| Idiosyncrasy | An **unusual reaction** to a drug that is not expected from its action, often due to **inherited differences** |

### Allergy warning signs

- Skin rash, hives or itching
- Swelling of the face, lips or tongue
- Wheezing or difficulty breathing
- Dizziness or collapse

If these occur, the patient needs **urgent medical help**. Always **ask about allergies** before dispensing, and record them. **Penicillin allergy** is a common example.

A **side effect** is **not the same as an allergy**. For example, nausea from an antibiotic is a side effect. A rash with swelling suggests an allergy.

## Compliance (adherence)

**Compliance**, also called **adherence**, is **how closely a patient follows the treatment plan**: taking the right dose, at the right time, for the right length of time.

**Poor compliance** causes:

- **treatment failure** and relapse;
- **resistance**, for example to antibiotics and antimalarials;
- **waste** of money and medicines.

Reasons for poor compliance include **forgetting**, **side effects**, **cost**, **complex regimens**, **not understanding**, and **feeling better**.

**How you can help**

- Give **clear, simple** instructions and labels.
- **Explain** why the course must be finished.
- Use **reminders**, pill boxes and simple schedules.
- Ask the patient to **repeat** the instructions.
- Refer concerns about **side effects** or **cost** to the pharmacist.
