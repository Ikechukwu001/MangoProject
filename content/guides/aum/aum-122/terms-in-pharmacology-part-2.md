## When drugs act together

When two or more drugs are taken together, their effects may combine in different ways. Using numbers as a memory aid:

| Term | Meaning | Memory aid | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Additive effect | The combined effect **equals the sum** of the separate effects | 1 + 1 = 2 | Two painkillers of different kinds taken together, as advised |
| Synergism | The combined effect is **greater than the sum** | 1 + 1 = 3 | Sulfamethoxazole with trimethoprim (co-trimoxazole) |
| Potentiation | One drug, with little or no effect alone, **increases the effect of another** | 0 + 1 = 2 | Probenecid raises the blood level of penicillin, so penicillin acts for longer |
| Antagonism | One drug **reduces or blocks** the effect of another | 1 + 1 = 0 | Naloxone blocking morphine |

Some interactions are **useful** and some are **dangerous**. The pharmacist checks every prescription for them, and a technician reports anything unusual, such as a patient on **several medicines** or buying **over-the-counter products** that may interact.

### Types of antagonism

- **Pharmacological**: both drugs act on the same receptor, as with naloxone and morphine.
- **Physiological**: the drugs have opposite effects through different routes.
- **Chemical**: the drugs react with each other, as in using an antidote to bind a poison.

## Cumulative effect

A drug has a **cumulative effect** when it **builds up in the body**, because each new dose is given before the earlier one has been removed. Eventually it may reach **toxic levels**. This is a risk with drugs that have a **long half-life** and in patients with **poor kidney or liver function**.

## Tolerance

**Tolerance** means that, **after repeated use, a larger dose is needed to produce the same effect**. The body adapts to the drug. It is common with **opioid painkillers**, **sedatives** and **alcohol**.

- Tolerance can develop **quickly**, in a few doses, as with some drugs (this is called **tachyphylaxis**), or **slowly**, over weeks or months.
- Tolerance can be **dangerous**, since patients may take more than is safe.
- **Cross-tolerance** occurs between similar drugs.

## Dependence and addiction

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical dependence | The body **has adapted** to the drug, and stopping it causes **withdrawal symptoms**, such as shaking, sweating, pain or anxiety |
| Psychological dependence | A **strong mental need** or craving for the drug, to feel normal or to cope |
| Addiction | **Compulsive drug seeking and use**, despite harm to health, work, family or finances |
| Withdrawal | The **unpleasant symptoms** when a drug that the body depends on is stopped suddenly |

Drugs that may cause dependence include **opioids**, **sedatives (benzodiazepines)**, **stimulants** and **alcohol**. Addiction is a health problem, and people with addiction need **help and treatment**, not blame. See the lesson on dependence in AUM 321.

## Abuse and misuse

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Drug misuse | Using a **medicine wrongly**, not as directed | Taking more paracetamol than advised, sharing antibiotics, stopping a course early |
| Drug abuse | Using a drug for **non-medical reasons**, or in **excess**, in a way that harms the user or others | Taking a strong painkiller or a sedative to get high |

A pharmacy technician should **notice warning signs**, such as repeated requests for the same medicine, and **tell the pharmacist**. See the lessons on ethical dispensing in BDP 332.

## Other terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chemotherapy | The use of drugs to **kill or stop the growth of germs, parasites or cancer cells**, with little harm to the patient. Often used for infections and cancer |
| Therapeutics | The branch of medicine that deals with the **treatment of disease** |
| Placebo | An **inactive substance** given in place of an active drug, often to test a new medicine, and which may help because of the patient's belief |
| Drug interaction | A change in the effect of a drug because of **another drug, food or herb** |
| Antidote | A substance that **counteracts a poison** or overdose |

## Quick comparison to remember

| If you see… | Think of… |
|---|---|
| Needs more drug for the same effect | **Tolerance** |
| Cannot stop without symptoms | **Dependence** |
| Compulsive use despite harm | **Addiction** |
| Drug blocks another | **Antagonism** |
| Combined effect larger than the sum | **Synergism** |
| Drug with no effect alone boosts another | **Potentiation** |
| Builds up in the body | **Cumulative effect** |
