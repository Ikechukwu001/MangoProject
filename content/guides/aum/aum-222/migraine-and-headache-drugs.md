## Types of headache

| Type | Description | Usual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Tension headache | A dull, tight, band-like ache on both sides. The commonest type | Paracetamol or an NSAID, rest, fluids, stress control |
| Migraine | Throbbing pain, often **one-sided**, lasting hours to days, with **nausea or vomiting** and **sensitivity to light and sound**. Some people have an **aura** (flashing lights, blurred vision) first | Early treatment as below |
| Cluster headache | Severe pain around one eye, in clusters, with a watering eye | Specialist treatment |
| Secondary headache | Caused by another illness, such as malaria, high blood pressure, infection or eye problems | Treat the cause |

In Nigeria, fever with headache may be **malaria**, so test before treating as migraine or tension headache.

## Treating an acute migraine

1. **Take treatment early**, at the first sign, in a **quiet, dark room**.
2. **Simple analgesics**: paracetamol, ibuprofen, diclofenac or aspirin (adults).
3. **Anti-sickness drug**: metoclopramide or domperidone, which also helps the gut absorb the painkiller. Short use only.
4. **Triptans** for moderate to severe attacks, or when simple painkillers fail.
5. **Ergotamine**: an older drug, used rarely.

### Triptans

- Examples: **sumatriptan**, rizatriptan, zolmitriptan.
- Act on **serotonin receptors**, narrowing blood vessels in the head and reducing the signal for pain.
- Taken **at the start of the headache**, not during the aura.
- **Do not take more than the stated number of doses** in 24 hours.
- **Side effects:** tingling, flushing, tightness in the chest or throat, drowsiness.
- **Not for** patients with **heart disease**, **uncontrolled high blood pressure**, previous stroke, or **liver disease** (some). Not within 24 hours of ergotamine.

### Ergotamine

- Narrows blood vessels strongly. **Not in pregnancy**, heart disease or high blood pressure. Interacts with some antibiotics and antifungals. Risk of serious side effects if used too often.

## Preventing migraine

For patients with **frequent or severe** attacks, doctors may prescribe a **daily preventive drug**:

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Propranolol (beta-blocker) | Avoid in asthma. Tiredness, cold hands |
| Amitriptyline | Drowsiness, dry mouth |
| Topiramate, valproate | Antiepileptics. **Valproate is not suitable for women who may become pregnant** |
| Others | Specialist drugs |

Prevention works over **weeks**, so the patient must keep taking it.

## Medication-overuse headache

Taking **painkillers on too many days each month** (usually more than **10 to 15 days**, depending on the drug) can cause **medication-overuse headache**, a daily or near-daily headache that gets worse with more medicine.

- Limit simple painkillers to **no more than 2 to 3 days a week**.
- The patient may need **medical help** to stop the overused drug.
- Keep a **headache diary** of days, triggers and treatments.

## Lifestyle and triggers

- **Regular meals**, sleep and fluids.
- Avoid known **triggers**, which may include some foods, alcohol, strong smells, stress, and bright light.
- **Exercise** regularly and manage stress.
- **Women:** migraine linked to periods, and combined oral contraceptives may need review, particularly when there is an **aura**.

## When to refer urgently

- A **sudden, severe ("worst ever")** headache.
- Headache with **fever and stiff neck**, or a **rash**.
- Headache after a **head injury**.
- Headache with **weakness, numbness, confusion, speech or vision changes**, or **fits**.
- A **new** headache in a person **over 50**, or in a person with cancer or HIV.
- A headache that is **worse on waking**, or **keeps getting worse**.
- Headache in **pregnancy** with high blood pressure.
- A headache lasting **more than a few days** without relief.
- Children with **frequent headaches**.
