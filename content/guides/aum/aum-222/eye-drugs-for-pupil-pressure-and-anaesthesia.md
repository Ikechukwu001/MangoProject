## The pupil and the eye muscles

The **pupil** is the opening in the iris that lets light enter. It widens in dim light and narrows in bright light. The **ciliary muscle** changes the shape of the lens to focus on near objects.

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Mydriatic | **Widens** the pupil | Tropicamide, phenylephrine, atropine, cyclopentolate |
| Cycloplegic | **Relaxes the focusing muscle**, so near focus is lost | Atropine, cyclopentolate, homatropine, tropicamide |
| Miotic | **Narrows** the pupil | Pilocarpine, carbachol |

## Mydriatics and cycloplegics

Most drugs here are **antimuscarinic**: they block acetylcholine in the eye. **Phenylephrine** widens the pupil by a different route.

| Drug | Duration | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Tropicamide | Short (hours) | Eye examination |
| Cyclopentolate | Medium (about a day) | Eye examination in children, measuring glasses |
| Atropine | Long (days to over a week) | Inflammation of the iris and uvea, and to treat pain. Used for amblyopia and myopia control in some settings |
| Homatropine | Medium to long | Iris inflammation |
| Phenylephrine | Short | Widening pupil for examination or surgery |

### Side effects and cautions

- **Blurred near vision and glare**: warn the patient not to drive or use machines until it clears, and to wear dark glasses.
- **Dry mouth, flushing, fast pulse**, drowsiness, from drops reaching the bloodstream. **Press the inner corner of the eye** after instilling to reduce this.
- **Young children and the elderly** are more sensitive. Excess atropine can cause fever and confusion.
- **Narrow-angle glaucoma**: mydriatics can bring on an attack and must **not** be used. Warning signs include a **red painful eye, headache, halos around lights, and reduced vision**, which need an **emergency** visit.
- Phenylephrine can raise blood pressure, so lower strengths are used in children and heart patients.

## Glaucoma

**Glaucoma** is a group of diseases in which **eye pressure rises** and damages the **optic nerve**, causing loss of side vision and eventually blindness. It is **a leading cause of blindness in Nigeria**, and often has **no early symptoms**, so regular eye tests, especially in people over 40 or with a family history, are important. It is **managed for life**.

- **Open-angle glaucoma** develops slowly.
- **Narrow-angle (angle-closure) glaucoma** can develop suddenly, as an emergency.

### Drugs that lower eye pressure

| Group | Examples | How they act | Main points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beta-blocker drops | Timolol, betaxolol | Reduce fluid made in the eye | Avoid in asthma, slow heart rate, heart block. Can cause tiredness and slow pulse |
| Prostaglandin analogues | Latanoprost, travoprost, bimatoprost | Help fluid drain | Used once daily at night. Darkening of the iris, longer eyelashes, redness. Latanoprost is stored in the fridge before opening |
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors | Dorzolamide drops, acetazolamide tablets | Reduce fluid made | Stinging, bitter taste. Tablets cause tingling, increased urine, and are used in emergencies. Avoid in sulfonamide allergy |
| Alpha agonists | Brimonidine | Both effects | Dry mouth, tiredness, allergy |
| Miotics | Pilocarpine | Constrict the pupil and help drainage | Blurred vision in dim light, headache, brow ache |
| Combinations | Timolol with dorzolamide, and others | Two actions in one bottle | Fewer drops, more convenience |
| Osmotic agents | Mannitol | In **emergency** lowering of pressure | Hospital use |

**Counselling for glaucoma**

- Use drops **every day, as prescribed**, even if the eye feels fine.
- Glaucoma drugs **control** the disease and do not cure it. **Damage already done cannot be reversed.**
- **Space** different drops by about **5 minutes**.
- **Do not stop** without the doctor's advice.
- Keep **follow-up appointments** and eye pressure checks.
- Tell the pharmacist about any **heart or lung disease** before using beta-blocker drops.
- **Family members** should have their eyes checked.

## Local anaesthetic eye drops

Examples: **tetracaine (amethocaine)**, **proxymetacaine**, **oxybuprocaine**.

- Used by **eye specialists and trained clinicians** to numb the surface of the eye for **examination**, **measuring eye pressure**, **removing foreign bodies** and minor procedures.
- They act within **seconds** and last **15 to 30 minutes**.
- The patient should **not rub the eye** while it is numb, since injury may go unnoticed.
- **Repeated use at home causes serious corneal damage** and delayed healing. They are **not given to patients to take home**. Do not supply them on request.
- Some may sting at first.

## Other eye preparations

| Preparation | Use |
|---|---|
| Artificial tears (hypromellose, carbomer) | Dry eye, with preservative-free forms for frequent use |
| Decongestant drops (naphazoline, tetrahydrozoline) | Short-term relief of redness. Prolonged use causes rebound redness. Avoid in glaucoma |
| Fluorescein drops or strips | Staining the cornea to look for damage |
| Eye lotions and washes (sterile saline) | Rinsing out foreign matter. Used once, then thrown away |

## Safety summary

- **Eye products are sterile**: wash hands, avoid touching the tip, and discard after 28 days.
- **Check the label** for the right eye drop: several look alike (for example mydriatic and glaucoma drops). A mix-up can be harmful.
- **Never use steroid, anaesthetic or mydriatic drops** without a prescription or the pharmacist's decision.
- Warn about **driving** after any drops that blur vision.
- **Refer** any painful eye, sudden loss of vision, halos around lights, or eye injury at once.
