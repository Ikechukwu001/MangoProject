## Why worms matter

**Helminths** (worms) are parasites that live in humans. They cause **anaemia, poor growth in children, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, weight loss and, in some cases, blindness or swelling of the limbs**. They are very common where sanitation and clean water are poor. **Anthelmintics** are drugs that kill or expel them.

## Common worms and their drugs

| Worm | Disease or sign | How it spreads | Usual drugs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roundworm (*Ascaris*) | Abdominal pain, worms in stool or vomit, malnutrition | Eggs swallowed from soil, dirty hands or food | Albendazole, mebendazole, pyrantel, levamisole, piperazine |
| Hookworm | Anaemia, tiredness, itchy rash on feet | Larvae enter through bare skin from soil | Albendazole, mebendazole, pyrantel |
| Whipworm (*Trichuris*) | Diarrhoea, rectal prolapse in heavy infection | Eggs swallowed | Albendazole, mebendazole |
| Threadworm (pinworm, *Enterobius*) | Itching around the anus, worse at night | Eggs spread by hands, bedding and dust | Mebendazole, albendazole, pyrantel |
| Strongyloides | Skin rash, diarrhoea. Can be severe in weak immunity | Larvae through skin | Ivermectin, albendazole |
| Tapeworm (*Taenia*) | Segments in stool, abdominal discomfort | Undercooked beef or pork | Praziquantel, albendazole |
| Schistosoma (bilharzia) | Blood in urine or stool | Larvae in freshwater snails' water | Praziquantel |
| Onchocerca (river blindness) | Itchy skin, nodules, loss of vision | Blackfly bites near rivers | Ivermectin |
| Filarial worms (lymphatic filariasis) | Swollen limbs and scrotum | Mosquito bites | Diethylcarbamazine, albendazole, ivermectin (mass treatment) |
| Guinea worm | Painful blister, worm emerging | Drinking water with infected water fleas | No drug cures it. Worm is removed carefully and wound cared for. Prevention is by filtering water. Nigeria has worked hard to eliminate it |

Treatment choices and schedules depend on national guidelines, so follow the pharmacist's instructions.

## How the drugs work

| Drug | Action |
|---|---|
| Albendazole, mebendazole | Block the worm's uptake of glucose, so it runs out of energy and dies |
| Pyrantel, levamisole | Paralyse the worm so it is passed out in the stool |
| Piperazine | Paralyses roundworms |
| Praziquantel | Raises calcium in the worm's muscles, which causes spasm and paralysis, and damages its outer layer |
| Ivermectin | Paralyses worms and microfilariae through nerve and muscle effects |
| Diethylcarbamazine | Kills microfilariae and makes them easier for the immune system to destroy |

## Side effects

- **Mild stomach upset**: nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, headache and dizziness. Common and short-lived.
- **Worm expulsion**: worms may appear in the stool or vomit.
- **Praziquantel**: dizziness, drowsiness, abdominal pain. Do not drive afterwards.
- **Ivermectin and diethylcarbamazine in onchocerciasis or filariasis**: itching, rash, fever, swollen glands and body pains caused by the **death of the parasites** (Mazzotti-type reaction). Treatment is supervised in programmes.
- **Albendazole** used long term: liver effects and low blood counts, so tests may be done.

## Cautions

- **Pregnancy**: many are avoided in the **first trimester**. Ask the doctor or pharmacist before giving. Mass treatment programmes follow their own guidance.
- **Breast-feeding**: ask before supplying.
- **Young children**: some drugs are not used under a certain age or weight. Use the age and weight dose on the label.
- **Liver disease** and **very poor health**: refer.
- **Loa loa** infection, which occurs in parts of Africa, can make ivermectin or diethylcarbamazine **dangerous**. Treatment programmes screen for it.
- Check for **other medicines** that may interact.

## Mass treatment (deworming) programmes

Governments and partners give **single-dose treatments to whole communities or school children** to control worms. Common examples are **albendazole** for intestinal worms, **praziquantel** for schistosomiasis, and **ivermectin** for onchocerciasis and filariasis. These programmes work best with **health education, clean water, sanitation and shoes**.

## Preventing reinfection

- **Wash hands** with soap before eating and after using the toilet.
- Use a **latrine**, and never defecate in open places.
- Drink **clean or boiled** water. Filter water in guinea worm areas.
- **Wash and peel** raw vegetables and fruit.
- **Cook meat** well, especially beef and pork.
- **Wear shoes**, particularly on soil.
- Avoid **freshwater** where bilharzia is known.
- **Keep nails short**. For threadworm, wash bedding and underwear at a high temperature, and treat all household members.
- **Control mosquitoes and blackflies** with nets and repellents.
- **Deworm** children regularly as advised by health workers.

## Counselling

- Take the medicine **as directed** with or without food as the label says. Some, such as albendazole for tissue infections, are taken **with a fatty meal**.
- **Chew** chewable tablets, and swallow others with water.
- Do not be alarmed if **worms are passed**.
- **Treat the family** where advised, and **repeat** the dose if the pharmacist says so (for example, threadworm after two weeks).
- **Return** to the doctor if symptoms persist, or if there is blood in the stool or urine, severe weight loss or severe anaemia.
