## What are opioids?

**Opioids** are drugs that act on **opioid receptors** in the brain, spinal cord and gut. They **relieve pain**, and they also cause **drowsiness, a feeling of wellbeing, slow breathing and constipation**. The word **narcotic** is also used, but it is a loose term.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Weak opioids | Codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol |
| Strong opioids | Morphine, pethidine, fentanyl, oxycodone, diamorphine |
| Opioids used to treat dependence | Methadone, buprenorphine |
| Opioid antagonist (reverses) | Naloxone, naltrexone |

## Uses

- **Severe pain**: after surgery or injury, heart attack, cancer and palliative care. See the WHO ladder in the lesson on analgesics.
- **Cough suppression**: codeine and pholcodine (not for children).
- **Diarrhoea**: loperamide, codeine.
- **Treatment of opioid dependence**: methadone, buprenorphine, under supervision.

## Side effects

| Effect | Notes |
|---|---|
| Drowsiness and confusion | Do not drive or use machines. Avoid alcohol |
| Nausea and vomiting | Common at the start |
| Constipation | Very common, persists, and usually needs a **laxative** |
| Slow, shallow breathing (respiratory depression) | The most dangerous effect, worse in overdose |
| Itching, sweating, dry mouth | Fairly common |
| Pinpoint pupils | A sign of opioid effect |
| Low blood pressure and dizziness | Especially when standing up |

**Cautions:** elderly patients, **children** (codeine is not suitable for children under 12, and some people break it down very quickly to morphine), **breast-feeding**, **breathing problems**, **liver or kidney disease**, **head injury**, and use with other **sedatives or alcohol**.

**Tramadol** also lowers the seizure threshold and interacts with antidepressants. It is widely misused in Nigeria, so supply is done with care and the pharmacist is told about repeated or large requests.

## Overdose

| Sign | Notes |
|---|---|
| Deep sleep or coma | Cannot be woken |
| Very small (pinpoint) pupils | May be wide late on |
| Slow, shallow breathing | Blue lips may follow |

This is an **emergency**.

1. **Call for help** and an ambulance.
2. Keep the airway open and **turn the person on their side** (recovery position).
3. **Naloxone** is the antidote. It must be given by trained staff. Its effect is **short**, so the patient may sink back into sleep when it wears off and needs **monitoring**.

## Tolerance, dependence and addiction

- **Tolerance**: after regular use, **more drug is needed** for the same effect.
- **Physical dependence**: the body adapts, and **stopping suddenly** causes withdrawal: restlessness, sweating, runny nose, stomach cramps, diarrhoea, aches and anxiety. Doses are **reduced gradually**.
- **Addiction**: compulsive use despite harm. It is a health problem that needs treatment and support.
- Dependence is less likely when opioids are used **for a short time for pain**, as prescribed.

## Control of opioids

Opioids are **controlled drugs**.

- Supplied **only on a valid prescription** that meets legal requirements.
- Stored in a **locked cupboard**, with **limited access**.
- Recorded in a **register**: every receipt and supply, with a running balance, checked regularly.
- **Never** supplied for others, or without checking the patient and prescription.
- **Report** forged prescriptions, repeated requests and thefts to the pharmacist. See the lessons on ethical dispensing.

## Counselling

- Take **exactly as prescribed**. Do not take extra doses.
- **Drowsiness**: avoid driving and alcohol.
- Expect **constipation**: eat fibre, drink water, and use the laxative prescribed.
- **Never share** the medicine, and keep it **locked and out of reach of children**.
- **Do not stop suddenly** after regular use. Ask the doctor or pharmacist.
- Return **unused** medicine to the pharmacy for safe disposal.
- Ask for help if breathing becomes slow, the patient is hard to wake, or confusion is severe.
