## Obesity

**Obesity** means having excess body fat that harms health. A common measure is the **body mass index (BMI)**, which is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared.

```
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
```

*Example.* A person weighing 90 kg and 1.70 m tall: 1.70² = 2.89, and 90 ÷ 2.89 = **31.1**.

| BMI | Usual description |
|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 to 24.9 | Healthy range |
| 25 to 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 and above | Obese |

Obesity raises the risk of **type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, joint problems, sleep apnoea** and some cancers.

## Treatment comes first from lifestyle

- **Healthier eating**: fewer sugary drinks and fried foods, smaller portions, more vegetables and fibre.
- **Regular physical activity**, such as brisk walking.
- **Sleep, stress control** and support from family.
- A realistic target: **slow, steady loss**, which is easier to keep.

**Medicines** are considered only when lifestyle measures have not worked enough, usually for people with a high BMI or with health problems caused by weight. They are **prescribed and reviewed by a doctor**, and used for a limited time.

## Drugs for weight loss

| Drug or group | How it works | Main problems |
|---|---|---|
| Orlistat | Blocks the gut enzyme that digests fat, so part of the fat passes out | Oily, loose stools, urgency, wind. Low-fat diet needed. May reduce absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), so a supplement may be advised. Rarely, liver problems |
| Amphetamine-like appetite suppressants (for example phentermine, diethylpropion) | Stimulate the brain, which reduces appetite | Fast heart rate, high blood pressure, anxiety, sleeplessness, dependence. Many have been withdrawn in several countries |
| Sibutramine | Acts on brain chemicals | **Withdrawn** in many countries because of heart and stroke risk |
| Injectable hormone-based drugs (such as semaglutide, liraglutide) | Mimic gut hormones, slow stomach emptying and reduce hunger | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, gall bladder and pancreas problems. Prescribed by a doctor, costly, and often regained when stopped |

Availability and legal status differ between countries and change over time, so ask the pharmacist what is registered and allowed for use in Nigeria.

## Dangerous products

Many **slimming teas, capsules, injections and "detox" products** are sold in markets and online. Some contain **hidden drugs** such as:

- stimulants (such as sibutramine),
- strong **laxatives** or **diuretics**, which cause loss of fluid and salts,
- **thyroid hormones**, **steroids**.

These can cause **heart problems, dehydration, kidney damage, dependence and even death**. A product being "natural" does not make it safe.

## Misuse of other drugs

Some people misuse **laxatives, diuretics, thyroid tablets or diabetes drugs** to lose weight. This is dangerous. Anyone who restricts food, vomits or purges, or fears weight gain, may need **medical and emotional support**. Treat them kindly and tell the pharmacist.

## Advice for patients

- **Talk to a doctor** first, especially with diabetes, heart disease, high blood pressure, or pregnancy.
- Avoid **unregistered** slimming products, and **never share** them.
- Combine any medicine with **diet and activity**.
- **Do not expect quick results**. A loss of about 0.5 to 1 kg a week is considered safe.
- Report **palpitations, chest pain, severe stomach pain, dark urine, or yellow eyes** at once.
- Regular **weighing and blood pressure checks** help to track progress.

## The technician's role

- **Do not sell** prescription-only slimming drugs without a prescription.
- Offer **general healthy-eating advice** and refer the patient to the pharmacist.
- Check the **registration number** (such as NAFDAC number) on any product before stocking it, and report suspicious products.
