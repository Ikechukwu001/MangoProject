## Depression

**Depression** is a **medical illness**, not weakness or laziness. Common symptoms lasting **two weeks or more**:

- **Low mood** and **loss of interest or pleasure**
- **Sleep** and **appetite** changes
- **Tiredness**, poor concentration
- **Guilt**, worthlessness, **hopeless thoughts**
- In severe cases, **thoughts of self-harm**

It is common and **treatable**, with **talking therapies**, **antidepressants**, or both. Support from family and the community matters too.

## Groups of antidepressants

| Group | Examples | Main side effects and notes |
|---|---|---|
| SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) | Sertraline, fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, paroxetine | Nausea, headache, sleep changes, sexual problems, restlessness at the start. **First choice** for most patients. Increased bleeding risk with NSAIDs and aspirin |
| Tricyclics (TCAs) | Amitriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine | Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, drowsiness, fast pulse, low blood pressure on standing. **Dangerous in overdose**, so supplies are limited. Also used for nerve pain and migraine prevention |
| SNRIs | Venlafaxine, duloxetine | Nausea, raised blood pressure, sweating |
| MAOIs | Phenelzine, moclobemide | Many **food and drug interactions**. Strict diet for older types. Specialist use |
| Others | Mirtazapine, trazodone | Sedation, weight gain (mirtazapine) |

### How they work

Antidepressants raise the activity of brain chemicals such as **serotonin** and **noradrenaline**. **Benefit builds slowly over 2 to 4 weeks**, though side effects may appear in days.

## Using antidepressants

- **Start low and go up** under supervision.
- **Continue for at least 6 months** after recovery, often longer, to prevent relapse.
- **Do not stop suddenly.** The dose is reduced slowly, to avoid **discontinuation symptoms**: dizziness, "electric shock" feelings, nausea, anxiety, and sleep problems.
- They are **not addictive** in the way that sedatives and opioids are, but they should not be stopped abruptly.
- **Take** at the time advised. Sedating ones are usually taken at night.

## Interactions and serotonin syndrome

**Serotonin syndrome** is a dangerous reaction when too much serotonin builds up, often from **combining** drugs that raise it. Signs: **agitation, sweating, tremor, fast heartbeat, high temperature, muscle stiffness, diarrhoea, and confusion**. It needs **urgent medical care**.

Risky combinations include SSRIs with:

- **MAOIs**
- **Tramadol**, pethidine, and some other painkillers
- **Triptans** (migraine drugs)
- **St John's wort** (a herbal remedy)
- Some **cough and cold** medicines (dextromethorphan)

Always **ask about other medicines and herbal products**, and refer to the pharmacist. Alcohol worsens drowsiness and should be avoided.

## Safety and support

- **Early in treatment**, and after dose changes, some patients, especially **young people**, may feel **more anxious, agitated, or have thoughts of self-harm**. Tell the patient and family to **watch for this** and to **contact the doctor or go for help at once** if it happens.
- **Treatment should be supervised** by a doctor, with regular review.
- **Limit supply** if the pharmacist or prescriber advises it, and **store medicines safely**, away from children.
- Treat the patient **with respect and without stigma**. Do not discuss their illness with others.
- If someone is in **crisis** or talks about ending their life, **do not leave them alone**, **tell the pharmacist** immediately, and help them reach emergency services or a trusted person.

## Counselling summary

| Point | Message |
|---|---|
| Delay | It takes weeks, so keep taking it |
| Side effects | Many ease after the first weeks. Report severe ones |
| Stopping | Never stop suddenly |
| Alcohol | Avoid |
| Driving | Take care if drowsy |
| Other medicines and herbs | Tell the pharmacist before adding any |
| Worsening mood or self-harm thoughts | Get help at once |

## Mood stabilisers

For **bipolar disorder** (alternating depression and high mood), **lithium**, **valproate** and **carbamazepine** are used. **Lithium** has a **narrow safety margin** and needs **regular blood tests**. Patients should keep **fluid and salt intake steady**, and report vomiting, diarrhoea, tremor and confusion. NSAIDs and water tablets can raise lithium levels, so always check with the pharmacist.
