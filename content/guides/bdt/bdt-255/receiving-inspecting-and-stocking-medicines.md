## The steps

The curriculum lists four steps for stocking medicines:

1. **Receive** the medicines.
2. **Keep a record** of the medicines received.
3. **Complete a ledger form** for each item, adding the new quantity received to the quantity already in stock.
4. **Stack** the medicines on shelves, or in refrigerators, as suitable for each.

## Step 1: Receiving a delivery

Receive deliveries in a **clean, secure** area, at a set time when possible, with the **person in charge of the store** present. Have the **order** (such as the local purchase order, LPO) and the **packing note** or **delivery note** ready.

### What to check

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| Correct supplier and delivery | The name and address on the delivery agree with the order |
| Quantity | Count the cartons, then the units in a sample or in all of them. Compare with the packing note and the order |
| Quality | The right medicine, form and strength. Look at the labels and the colour, and look for broken seals, damaged tablets or leaking bottles |
| Damage | Look at each carton for crushing, wetness or tears |
| Expiry | Check the expiry date and the batch number. Reject or note anything with too short a remaining shelf life, as the policy says |
| Storage needs | Identify items that must be kept cold or locked up |

If something is **short, wrong or damaged**, write it on the delivery note **before signing**, and make a **damage report**.

### A damage report shows

- the date and the supplier;
- the **item, batch number and quantity** damaged;
- the **kind of damage** and how it was found;
- the **action** taken, such as returned, replaced or written off;
- the names and **signatures** of the people involved.

## Step 2: Recording the receipt

Enter the delivery in the **receipt records**: the date, the supplier, the item, the batch number, the expiry date, the quantity and the receipt voucher number. Keep the **delivery note**, and file it with the order.

## Step 3: The ledger

Each medicine has a **ledger entry** (and often a bin or tally card on the shelf). The new quantity is **added** to what was already in stock.

```
new balance = previous balance + quantity received
```

**Example.** *The ledger shows 450 tablets. A delivery of 300 arrives.*

- New balance = 450 + 300 = **750 tablets**

After you stack the stock, **count** it and make sure the shelf agrees with the ledger.

## Step 4: Stacking

- **Cold-chain** items such as vaccines and insulin go **straight into the refrigerator**.
- **Dangerous and controlled drugs** go into the **locked cupboard** at once, and are entered in their own register.
- Ordinary stock goes on **shelves or pallets**, **off the floor** and **away from the wall**, so air can pass and cleaning is easy.
- Put **new stock behind or beneath** older stock, so that stock with the **earliest expiry date is used first**. See the lesson on storage and FEFO.
- **Label** shelves clearly, and keep each medicine in its own place.
- Keep **large and small** packs in order, with the labels facing outward.

## Handling rules

- Never accept a delivery you have not checked.
- Never leave a delivery unattended on the floor, outside or in the sun.
- Make sure only **authorised** people are in the store during a delivery.
- Open cartons carefully, and keep damaged packs separate and labelled until they are dealt with.
