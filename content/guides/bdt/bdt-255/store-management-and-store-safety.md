## Definitions

- A **store** is a place where supplies are **received, kept and issued**.
- **Ownership of the store** rests with the organisation, such as the hospital or the government. The store keeper does not own the stock. He or she is **accountable** for it.
- A **store keeper** is the person given the job of **receiving, storing, issuing and recording** supplies, and keeping the store safe.

## Responsibilities of the store keeper

- Receive deliveries and **check** them.
- Keep the stock **safe** and in good condition, in the right place and conditions.
- **Issue** supplies only on an approved requisition.
- Keep accurate **records**: bin cards, ledgers and vouchers.
- Check stock regularly, using **FEFO** and expiry checks.
- Report **stock levels, shortages, damage and losses** promptly.
- Control **keys** and who enters the store.
- Keep the store **clean, tidy and safe**.
- Help with **stock verification** by auditors and inspectors.

## Organising the store

A well-organised store saves time, and makes errors and loss easier to spot.

- **Group items** logically, for example tablets, liquids, creams, injections, and supplies.
- Use **labelled shelves** and keep each item in the **same place**.
- Keep **aisles clear** and wide enough to work and to escape.
- Keep stock **off the floor** and away from walls.
- Separate **cold-chain**, **controlled drugs**, **flammables**, **expired stock** and **damaged stock**.
- Put **fast-moving items** near the door, and **heavy items** low down.
- Keep a **plan** of the store, and a **list of stock** by location.

## Sources of supply

| Source | How supplies are requested |
|---|---|
| Higher stores (for example a central or state store) | Requisition form, with approval |
| Local purchase | A **local purchase order (LPO)** sent to an approved supplier |
| Government supply | Issues under the supply programme, with vouchers |
| Donations from NGOs, voluntary organisations and others | Receipt with a record of the donor, the items and their expiry dates |

Whatever the source, the supplies must be **received, checked and recorded** in the same way.

## Store safety

| Hazard | Measures |
|---|---|
| Burglary and theft | Strong doors and locks, window bars, good lighting, a limited number of keys, a security guard if possible, an alarm |
| Pilferage (small, repeated theft) | Regular counts, checks of records against stock, two people for counting, and no bags in the store |
| Fire | No smoking, extinguishers in working order, staff trained to use them, clear exits, no overloaded electrical sockets, flammables stored apart |
| Chemical hazards | Labels, safe storage of corrosives and flammables, spill kits, gloves and eye protection, and ventilation |
| Natural disasters (flood, storm) | Store above flood level, a sound roof, stock off the floor, and a plan to move stock |
| Pests (rats, insects) | A clean store, proper waste disposal, sealed packs, and regular pest control |
| Heat and damp | Ventilation, shading, and keeping the roof and walls in good repair |
| Loss of power | A generator or back-up for refrigerators, and a plan to save vaccines |

## Security of records

The records are as important as the stock.

- Keep **ledgers and vouchers locked** away when not in use.
- Make **copies or back-ups**, for example a photograph or digital copy, kept in another place.
- Control **who can write in or change** the records, and never use correction fluid or erase an entry. Cross out, initial and rewrite.
- Back up any **computer records** regularly, and protect them with a password.

## Good habits

- Limit store entry to **authorised persons**, and keep a visitors' record.
- **Count** and **inspect** the store regularly.
- Report any loss, damage or break-in **immediately**.
- Know the **emergency plan** and where the extinguishers and first-aid box are.
