## Why ordering needs method

If a unit orders too little, it runs out and patients go without. If it orders too much, money is tied up and medicines expire. **A good order is based on data**, worked out in steps, and written on a form that can be checked.

## The steps for ordering

1. **Collect data** for estimating the medicines needed.
2. **Decide the ordering interval**, which is how often to order.
3. **List the common medicines** that are needed.
4. **Calculate the amount** of each medicine needed in the ordering interval, based on **local and seasonal use**.
5. **Complete the medicines requisition form.**
6. **Calculate the cost** of the medicines ordered.

## Step 1: Data collection

The data comes from **records**, not from memory:

- **issue records** or **sales records** for the last few months;
- the **number of patients** treated, and the illnesses seen;
- the **stock on hand** and **any stock already on order**;
- **expiry dates**, so that stock about to expire is counted properly;
- information on **seasons and outbreaks**.

**Average monthly consumption (AMC)** is the average quantity used each month. Leave out months when the medicine was out of stock, since use would have been higher.

## Step 2: The ordering interval

The **ordering interval** is the time between orders, for example every month or every three months.

| | Short interval | Long interval |
|---|---|---|
| Money tied up in stock | Less | More |
| Risk of expiry | Lower | Higher |
| Number of orders and trips | More | Fewer |

It depends on how far the supplier is, how reliable transport is, the money available and the storage space.

## Step 3: The list of medicines

Order from the **essential medicines list** for the level of care, and choose the medicines for the illnesses that are common locally. See the lesson on setting up a drug revolving fund for more on this.

## Step 4: Calculating the amount

```
order quantity = AMC × (ordering interval + lead time + safety stock)
                 − stock on hand − stock already on order
```

All three time periods are in **months**. The **lead time** is how long the supplier takes to deliver. The **safety stock** is an extra cushion for delays or a sudden rise in use.

### Worked example

*AMC = 300 tablets. Interval 2 months, lead time 1 month, safety stock 1 month. Stock on hand 400.*

1. Months of cover = 2 + 1 + 1 = **4**
2. Needed = 300 × 4 = **1 200**
3. Order = 1 200 − 400 = **800 tablets**

### Seasonal and local use

Use is not always the same every month. **Adjust** the average when you know that demand will change:

- malaria cases rise in the **rainy season**;
- cough and cold medicines rise in the **dry (harmattan) season**;
- **vaccines** are needed in larger amounts before an expected outbreak, such as measles vaccine before a measles outbreak;
- a **new clinic day** or a **new population** changes the number of patients.

**Example.** *Normal use is 100 doses a month. A 50% rise is expected.*

- Expected use = 100 + (100 × 50 ÷ 100) = 100 + 50 = **150 doses** a month
- Use this figure in place of the AMC for the period of the outbreak.

## Step 5: The requisition form

A **requisition form** is the written request for medicines. It usually shows:

- the **name of the unit** and the date;
- the **name, form and strength** of each medicine, in the same way as on the supply list;
- the **unit of issue** (tablet, bottle, pack);
- the **stock on hand**, the **average monthly consumption** and the **quantity requested**;
- the **signature** of the person who made the request, and of the person who approves it.

Write clearly, so that the supplier sends exactly what you need. A form with missing or unclear details is returned or leads to the wrong supply.

## Step 6: Costing the order

```
cost of an item = quantity × unit cost
cost of the order = total of all the items
```

**Example.** *500 capsules at ₦20 each and 200 tablets at ₦5 each.*

| Item | Quantity | Unit cost | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capsules | 500 | ₦20 | ₦10 000 |
| Tablets | 200 | ₦5 | ₦1 000 |
| **Total** | | | **₦11 000** |

Use the **current** price. Compare the total with the money available before sending the order. All amounts in these lessons are made up for practice.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Result | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Guessing the quantity | Stock-outs or overstock | Use the records |
| Forgetting stock on hand | Over-ordering | Subtract it every time |
| Ignoring stock already on order | Double ordering | Always check pending orders |
| Ignoring the lead time | Running out before delivery | Add the lead time to the cover |
| Not allowing for seasons | Shortages in busy periods | Adjust for known seasonal use |
