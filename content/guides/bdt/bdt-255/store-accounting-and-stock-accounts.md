## Why stores keep accounts

A store holds goods that cost money. Store accounting answers: **What is the stock worth? Has any been lost? Who owes us, and whom do we owe?** The records are checked by **auditors** (internal and external) as part of **stock verification**.

## Valuing stock

```
value of an item = quantity × unit cost
value of the stock = total of all the items
```

**Example.** *200 packs at ₦50 and 100 packs at ₦80.*

| Item | Quantity | Unit cost | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Batch 1 | 200 | ₦50 | ₦10 000 |
| Batch 2 | 100 | ₦80 | ₦8 000 |
| **Total** | 300 | | **₦18 000** |

## Methods of pricing stock

When the same item is bought at different prices, the store needs a rule for the unit cost.

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| First in, first out (FIFO) | Issues are priced at the cost of the **oldest** stock first |
| Weighted average cost | The average cost of all the stock, weighted by quantity |
| Replacement cost | The cost to buy the item **today** |

### Worked example: weighted average

*100 units were bought at ₦40, and 200 units at ₦46.*

1. Total cost = (100 × 40) + (200 × 46) = 4 000 + 9 200 = ₦13 200
2. Total units = 100 + 200 = 300
3. Average cost = 13 200 ÷ 300 = **₦44 per unit**

A revolving fund uses **replacement cost** for pricing, so that sales can restock the shelf.

## Deficiencies and surpluses

At stock-taking, the **physical count** is compared with the **ledger**.

- A **deficiency** is when the count is **less** than the ledger.
- A **surplus** is when the count is **more** than the ledger.

**Example.** *The ledger shows 450 units. The count is 440. The unit cost is ₦44.*

- Deficiency = 450 − 440 = **10 units**
- Value = 10 × 44 = **₦440**

Steps:

1. **Recount** to confirm.
2. **Look for the cause**: a wrong entry, a missing voucher, an issue not recorded, or loss.
3. **Report** it to the person responsible.
4. **Correct the record** with an approved entry, and **never change it secretly**.

A surplus should be investigated in the same way, because it may mean a receipt or issue was wrongly recorded.

## Tallying records and vouchers

The **issue voucher (IV)** and **receipt voucher (RV)** are the documents behind every entry. To "tally with the cards", check that:

- the total received on the vouchers equals the receipts on the bin card and ledger;
- the total issued on the vouchers equals the issues on the card and ledger;
- the opening balance + receipts − issues equals the closing balance and the shelf count.

## The main accounts

| Account | What it records |
|---|---|
| Capital account | The money or goods put into the business or fund by the owners, or as seed capital |
| Petty cash account | Small cash payments, kept on an imprest system, with a voucher for each |
| Sales journal | A list of sales in the order they happen, with date, customer, item and amount |
| Sales ledger | An account for each customer showing what they have bought, paid and owe |
| Control account | A summary account whose total must agree with the total of the individual accounts, as a check |
| Bills book | A record of bills payable and bills receivable |

### Credit transactions

A **credit sale** means the customer takes the goods now and **pays later**. The amount is entered in the sales ledger as owed, and when the customer pays, it is cleared.

- **Bills receivable** are amounts **owed to the pharmacy**, such as credit sales.
- **Bills payable** are amounts **the pharmacy owes**, such as an unpaid supplier invoice.

Check that bills are paid on time, and that those owing money are **reminded**. Too much credit starves the business of cash.

### Joint ventures and partnerships

In a **joint venture**, two or more people or groups work together on a particular project and share its profit or loss, with accounts for the project. In a **partnership**, two or more people run a business together, and a partnership account shows each partner's capital and share of profit. A pharmacy owned by partners keeps these accounts.

## Good practice

- Record every transaction **on the day** it happens.
- Support every entry with a **voucher or receipt**.
- Keep **cash and stock separate** from personal property.
- Have records **checked** by a second person.
- Never remove or alter a document without approval.
