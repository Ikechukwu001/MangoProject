## The steps

The curriculum lists five steps for issuing medicines:

1. **File** the requisition from the requesting unit.
2. **Evaluate** the requisition.
3. **Issue** the medicines.
4. **Balance the ledger** for each item, by subtracting the quantity issued from the previous balance.
5. **Make sure the receiving unit signs** the receipt voucher.

## Step 1: The requisition

A **requisition** is the written request from a ward, clinic or unit for medicines from the store. It must be **complete, legible, signed** and **approved** by the authorising officer. File it with a serial number, so that every issue can be traced to a request.

Do not issue on a **verbal request** alone, except in a real emergency, and then record it properly afterwards.

## Step 2: Evaluating the requisition

Before issuing, check:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Authority | Is it signed and approved by the right person? |
| Correct items | Are the names, forms and strengths clear and on the unit's list? |
| Quantity | Is it reasonable compared with the unit's past use and stock on hand? |
| Stock available | Do we have enough, and in what batches? |
| Schedule | Is it within the distribution schedule, or outside it? |

### Worked example

*A clinic asks for 1 000 tablets. It uses about 300 a month, has 400 in stock, and the interval is 1 month.*

- Needed for the month = 300. It already has 400. So it needs **no more** for this interval, or only a small top-up for safety stock.

The request is far too large. **Query it** with the unit and the authorising officer. Do not just issue it, or the stock will be wasted, or the store will run short for other units.

If the store has less than the whole request, issue a **fair share** by need, and record what is owed (dues-out).

### Issues outside the schedule

Most units receive supplies on a **distribution schedule**, which sets a regular date and quantity. If a unit asks for more, or at another time, ask:

- Why is more needed (an outbreak, a rise in patients, a loss)?
- What is the unit's current stock and use?
- Is the **authorising officer's approval** given in writing?

The quantity outside the schedule is decided from the **real need** and the **stock available**, and is recorded.

## Step 3: Issuing

1. Take the medicines using **FEFO**, so stock with the earliest expiry date goes first.
2. **Count** the quantity twice, or have a second person check it.
3. Check the **expiry date, batch number and condition** of every item.
4. **Pack** the medicines safely. Keep cold-chain items cold, and keep controlled drugs secure.
5. Complete the **issue voucher**: the unit, the date, the items, the batch numbers and the quantities.

## Step 4: Balancing the ledger

Subtract the quantity issued from the previous balance, on both the **bin card** and the **ledger**.

```
new balance = previous balance − quantity issued
```

**Example.** *Ledger balance 750 tablets. Issue 200 to Ward A.*

- New balance = 750 − 200 = **550 tablets**

Do this **at the time of issue**, not at the end of the day, so the record is always right.

## Step 5: The signed voucher

The **receiving unit signs the receipt (issue) voucher**, after checking the items against the voucher. This proves the unit received the medicines.

Often the voucher has several copies:

| Copy | Kept by |
|---|---|
| Original | The store, filed in order |
| Duplicate | The receiving unit |
| Triplicate | Sent with the goods and returned signed, or sent to accounts |

Different services call these forms by different names, such as the **issue voucher (IV)** and **receipt voucher (RV)**.

## Checking for proper balancing

Regularly check that:

- the **bin card balance** equals the **ledger balance**;
- both equal the **physical count**;
- every issue in the ledger has an **approved requisition** and a **signed voucher**;
- the totals of **receipts less issues** equal the **closing balance**.

**Example.** *Opening balance 500. Receipts 300. Issues 200 + 150 = 350.*

- Closing balance = 500 + 300 − 350 = **450**

Any difference must be traced and explained.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Result | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Issuing without an approved requisition | No proof, and open to misuse | Always file an approved requisition first |
| Not checking the quantity against need | Waste, or shortages elsewhere | Compare with past use and stock |
| Not using FEFO | Expired stock | Issue the earliest expiry first |
| Forgetting to update the ledger | Records wrong, stock-outs | Update at the time of issue |
| No signature on the voucher | No proof of receipt | Do not release the goods without a signature |
