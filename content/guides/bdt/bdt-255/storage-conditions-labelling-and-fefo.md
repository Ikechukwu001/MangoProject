## What damages medicines?

| Factor | What it does | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature and heat | Speeds up breakdown. Melts ointments, creams and suppositories | Store cool, and away from direct heat. Use a refrigerator when the label says so |
| Light | Breaks down light-sensitive drugs | Keep in amber or opaque containers, and away from windows |
| Moisture | Makes tablets crumble, powders cake and germs grow | Keep dry, and keep containers closed |
| Air | Oxidises some drugs and lets in moisture | Keep tightly closed |
| Germs and pests | Spoil liquids and creams. Rats and insects damage packs | Keep the store clean and pest-proof |

Read the **storage instructions** on each product. If the label gives a temperature, follow it exactly.

## Storing different preparations

| Preparation | Storage |
|---|---|
| Mixtures and other liquids | Tightly closed, cool place, away from light. Do not freeze unless stated. Water-based products have a short life |
| Galenicals (tinctures, extracts, spirits) | Tightly closed, away from light and heat. Many contain alcohol and are **flammable**, so keep them away from fire |
| Ointments and creams | In a cool place, because heat makes them soften or separate. Keep the tubes and jars closed |
| Tablets and capsules | Dry, cool, in tight containers. Capsules soften in damp heat |
| Suppositories and pessaries | In a cool place, or a refrigerator if stated, because they melt |
| Vaccines and insulin | In a refrigerator at **2 to 8 °C**, with a thermometer, and a record of the temperature |
| Controlled drugs | In a **locked cupboard**, with a register and one person responsible for the key |
| Flammable and corrosive chemicals | Separately, in a safe place, labelled with the hazard |

Keep **medicines apart from food, drinks and cleaning materials**, and do not store anything on the floor.

## Labelling for all purposes

Every container, and every shelf, should be labelled. A label shows:

- the **name** of the medicine (approved name) and its **form and strength**;
- the **batch number** and the **expiry date**;
- **storage instructions**, such as "Store below 25 °C" or "Keep in a refrigerator";
- **warnings**, such as "Flammable" or "For external use only".

Items dispensed to patients have their own labels, as covered in the dispensing lessons. When medicines are moved into a smaller container, the new container must carry **all of the label details**. Never use an unlabelled bottle.

## Expiry dates and FEFO

An **expiry date** is the date after which the manufacturer does not guarantee the medicine. **Do not issue or use medicine after its expiry date.**

**FEFO (first expiry, first out)** means that the stock that **expires first is issued first**, whatever the date it was received.

This is different from **FIFO (first in, first out)**, which goes by the date of arrival. FEFO is better, because a newer delivery can have an earlier expiry date.

### Worked example

*Three batches of the same tablet are in the store.*

| Batch | Received | Expiry |
|---|---|---|
| A | January | March |
| B | April | February |
| C | May | August |

Order of issue under FEFO: **B** (February), then **A** (March), then **C** (August).

Under FIFO the order would have been A, B, C. Batch B would then sit on the shelf until it expired.

## Good practice for expiry dates

- **Check expiry dates** on receipt and **at regular intervals**, for example each month.
- Put the **earliest-expiry stock at the front** of the shelf.
- Mark or list items that will expire within a few months, and **use them first or arrange a transfer** to a busy unit.
- **Remove expired medicines** at once, record them, and keep them apart until they are disposed of as the rules require.
- Write expired stock off in the ledger, with the approval of the person responsible.

## Storage checks

| Check | How often |
|---|---|
| Store temperature and humidity | Daily, with a thermometer |
| Refrigerator temperature | Twice a day, with a written record |
| Expiry dates | Monthly |
| Cleanliness, pests and leaks | Regularly |
| Stock count against the ledger | As the policy sets, and at least yearly |
