## What is inventory?

**Inventory** is the **stock of goods** held by a store or pharmacy, and the **list that records it**: item, quantity, batch, expiry date and value. Managing inventory means holding **enough stock to meet need**, without **tying up too much money** or letting stock expire.

## Stock-taking

A **stock-take** (or stock verification) is a **physical count** of the stock, compared with the records. It shows whether stock is **lost, damaged, expired or wrongly recorded**. Auditors, both internal and external, carry out checks too.

### Types

| Type | How it works | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous (perpetual) | A few items are counted each day or week, so all are covered over the year | Less disruption, problems found sooner | Needs a plan and good records |
| Periodic | All stock is counted on a set date, for example yearly | The whole store is checked at one time | The store may have to close, and errors are found late |

### Steps for a stock-take

1. **Plan**: choose the date, the team and the areas. Tell the staff.
2. **Stop movement**: finish all issues and receipts, and post the records up to date.
3. **Count**: two people count each item. One counts and one records.
4. **Record** the count on count sheets, with the batch and expiry date.
5. **Compare** with the ledger and bin cards.
6. **Recount** any differences.
7. **Investigate** differences that remain.
8. **Correct** the records with approved entries, and report to the person responsible.
9. **Remove** expired or damaged stock and record it.
10. **Value** the stock and sign off the report.

### Differences

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Count is less than the record | Deficiency | Investigate: missing entry, loss, theft |
| Count is more than the record | Surplus | Investigate: missed receipt, wrong issue |

**Example.** *The ledger shows 450 units. The count is 440. The unit cost is ₦44.*

- Deficiency = 10 units. Value = 10 × 44 = **₦440**

## Valuing stock

```
value of stock = quantity × unit cost, added up for all items
```

See the lesson on store accounting for the methods of pricing stock.

## ABC analysis

**ABC analysis** ranks items by the **money value** of their yearly use. It helps to put effort where the money is.

- **A items**: a few items that account for most of the value. Check them **closely and often**.
- **B items**: a middle group. Check them regularly.
- **C items**: many items that use little money. Check them less often.

### Worked example

*Yearly use value of six items:*

| Item | Value | Share | Cumulative share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₦600 000 | 60% | 60% |
| 2 | ₦250 000 | 25% | 85% |
| 3 | ₦70 000 | 7% | 92% |
| 4 | ₦40 000 | 4% | 96% |
| 5 | ₦25 000 | 2.5% | 98.5% |
| 6 | ₦15 000 | 1.5% | 100% |
| **Total** | **₦1 000 000** | | |

The total is 600 000 + 250 000 + 70 000 + 40 000 + 25 000 + 15 000 = ₦1 000 000.

- **A**: items 1 and 2, which make up 85% of the money.
- **B**: items 3 and 4.
- **C**: items 5 and 6.

The exact cut-off points vary, so use the rule your textbook or workplace follows.

## VEN analysis

**VEN analysis** ranks items by **how important they are for health**, not by cost.

| Category | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vital (V) | Life-saving, or serious illness without them. Never be out of stock | Emergency medicines, essential vaccines |
| Essential (E) | Effective against less severe but significant illness | Common antibiotics, many analgesics |
| Non-essential (N) | Minor illness, or of doubtful value | Items used for minor or self-limiting complaints |

Use VEN to decide what to **protect first** when money is short. ABC says where the money goes. VEN says what matters most for patients. The two can be combined.

## Stock turnover

**Stock turnover** shows how many times the stock is sold and replaced in a year.

```
stock turnover = cost of goods sold ÷ average stock value
months of stock held = 12 ÷ turnover
```

### Worked example

*Cost of goods sold in a year = ₦600 000. Average stock value = ₦100 000.*

1. Turnover = 600 000 ÷ 100 000 = **6 times a year**
2. Months of stock held = 12 ÷ 6 = **2 months**

- **High turnover**: money is not tied up, and there is less expiry. Too high may mean frequent stock-outs.
- **Low turnover**: money is tied up, and expiry is likely. Check for slow-moving items.

## Good practice

- Keep the inventory **up to date**, and keep it in an **organised** way.
- Do **regular counts**, and have a second person do the checking.
- Use **FEFO**, and remove expired stock.
- Act on **slow-moving** and **overstocked** items.
- Use records to **order the right amounts**.

All amounts are made up for practice.
