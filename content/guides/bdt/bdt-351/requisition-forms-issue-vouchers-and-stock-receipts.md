## How the documents fit together

```
Requisition  →  Issue voucher  →  Stock receipt
(asks for stock)  (stock leaves the store)  (stock arrives at the unit)
```

When a ward asks the store for stock, it sends a **requisition**. The store issues the stock with an **issue voucher**. The ward checks and signs, and records the stock in on a **receipt**. Each step is recorded in the **ledger** and on the **bin card**.

## Requisition form

A **requisition form** is a written **request for stock**.

| Heading | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Serial number and date | For filing and tracing |
| From (requesting unit) and To (store or supplier) | Who asks and who supplies |
| Item description | Name, form, strength |
| Unit of issue | Tablet, bottle, pack |
| Stock on hand, and average monthly use | To justify the amount |
| Quantity requested | What the unit wants |
| Quantity approved or issued | Filled in by the store |
| Signatures | Of the person asking, and the authorising officer |

**Rules**

- Write clearly, using the names on the supply list.
- Ask only for quantities that match need.
- The form is **signed** before it is sent.
- The store **keeps** it, as authority for the issue.

## Stock issue voucher

A **stock issue voucher** records **stock leaving the store**.

| Heading | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Voucher number and date | For tracing |
| Issued to | The receiving unit |
| Requisition reference | Links it to the request |
| Item, batch number and expiry date | Exactly what was issued |
| Quantity issued | Counted and checked |
| Unit cost and value (if used) | For accounts |
| Issued by, checked by, received by | Three signatures, where possible |

**Copies:** the original stays in the store, a copy goes with the stock to the unit, and another goes to accounts if needed.

The **receiving unit must sign**. A voucher without a signature is not proof that the stock was received.

## Stock receipt

A **stock receipt** (also called a **receipt voucher** or **goods received note**) records **stock arriving**, whether from a supplier or from another store.

| Heading | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Receipt number and date | For tracing |
| Received from | Supplier or issuing store |
| Order or LPO number, and delivery note number | Links it to the order and the delivery |
| Item, batch number and expiry date | What came |
| Quantity ordered, delivered and accepted | To show any shortage or rejection |
| Condition | Good, damaged, short |
| Received by and checked by | Signatures |

**Rule:** record what **actually arrived**, not what the delivery note says.

### Worked example: checking a delivery

*The LPO was for 200 tablets. The delivery note says 200. A count finds 180, and 5 of them are damaged.*

| | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ordered | 200 |
| Delivered | 180 |
| Damaged (rejected) | 5 |
| Accepted | 180 − 5 = **175** |
| Shortage | 200 − 180 = **20** |

The receipt shows **175 accepted**. The shortage of 20 and the 5 damaged are reported to the supplier, with a damage report. The ledger is increased by 175, not 200.

## Linking the documents to the ledger

For every item, the ledger and the bin card should show:

- **receipts** from the stock receipts;
- **issues** from the issue vouchers.

**Example.** *Opening balance 400. Receipts 175. Issues of 100 and 50.*

- Closing balance = 400 + 175 − 100 − 50 = **425**

If a ledger entry has no document, it is a problem that must be found and corrected.

## Filing and handling

- Number the documents **in order**, and never skip or remove a number.
- File them **by date and number**, in a safe place.
- Keep spoiled forms, marked "cancelled", in the book.
- Keep them for as long as the rules require, for audit.
- Make sure each person **keeps their own copy**.

## Common faults

| Fault | Risk |
|---|---|
| Issue without a signed requisition | Stock can disappear without authority |
| No receiving signature | No proof of delivery |
| Receipt copies the delivery note without counting | Shortages are missed |
| Missing numbers | Possible removed or hidden documents |
| Quantities changed without initials | Possible fraud |
