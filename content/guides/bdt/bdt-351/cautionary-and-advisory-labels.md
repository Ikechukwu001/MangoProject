## Where this fits

The lessons on labels and labelling and on dispensing in BDT 251 cover what a label is, its functions and its qualities. This lesson goes deeper into the **kinds of label** and the **instructions for particular classes of medicines**.

## Categories of label

| Category | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| General label | Identifies the patient, the medicine and how to take it | "Mr A. Obi. Amoxicillin 500 mg capsules. Take ONE capsule THREE times a day" |
| Cautionary label | Warns about a danger or something to avoid | "Avoid alcohol", "May cause drowsiness" |
| Advisory label | Gives advice on how to get the best from the medicine | "Take with or after food", "Complete the course" |
| Special instruction | Extra instructions for a particular medicine or group | "Dissolve under the tongue", "Store in a refrigerator" |

## Information on the general label

- The **patient's name** (and age for children).
- The **name and strength** of the medicine, and the **form**.
- The **quantity** supplied.
- The **directions**: dose, how often, when, and for how long.
- The **date** of dispensing.
- The **name and address** of the pharmacy, and the dispenser's or pharmacist's details.
- **Storage** instructions and the **discard or expiry date**, as needed.
- "**Keep out of the reach of children**."

The label should be **clear, accurate, legible and in plain words**. Write "Take ONE tablet THREE times a day" rather than "1 t.d.s."

## Common cautionary and advisory labels

The wording below is a guide. Use the wording that your school's reference book or your pharmacy has approved.

| Label | Suitable for |
|---|---|
| Complete the prescribed course | Antibiotics, antimalarials |
| Take with or after food | Anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen and diclofenac, prednisolone |
| Take on an empty stomach, about half an hour before food | Some medicines, such as rifampicin |
| Avoid alcohol | Metronidazole, and medicines that cause drowsiness |
| May cause drowsiness. If affected, do not drive or use machinery | Many antihistamines, strong painkillers, sedatives |
| Do not take milk, antacids or iron preparations at the same time | Tetracyclines such as doxycycline, and ciprofloxacin |
| Swallow whole. Do not chew or crush | Modified-release tablets |
| Dissolve under the tongue | Glyceryl trinitrate tablets |
| Do not stop taking this medicine unless your doctor tells you to | Steroids, antiepileptics, antihypertensives |
| Protect your skin from sunlight | Doxycycline and similar medicines |
| Shake the bottle well before use | Suspensions |
| For external use only | Creams, lotions, liniments |
| Keep in a refrigerator | Insulin, some eye drops, some liquids |
| Space the doses evenly through the day | Antibiotics |

## Special instructions by class of medicine

| Class | Key instructions |
|---|---|
| Antibiotics | Finish the course. Space the doses. Do not share with others. Some need food and some need an empty stomach |
| Antimalarials | Finish the course. Take with food if the label says so. Seek help if the fever continues |
| Painkillers (analgesics) | Do not exceed the stated dose. Do not take another medicine that contains the same drug, for example paracetamol in a cold remedy |
| Anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) | Take with food. Report black stools or stomach pain |
| Antihypertensives | Take daily, even when you feel well. Do not stop suddenly. Rise slowly from lying down if you feel dizzy |
| Antidiabetic medicines | Take as directed with meals. Know the signs of low blood sugar. Do not skip meals |
| Insulin | Store in a refrigerator. Rotate injection sites. Use the correct syringe or pen |
| Steroids | Take in the morning, with food. Do not stop suddenly |
| Iron tablets | May turn stools dark. Take as directed, and keep out of reach of children |
| Eye and ear drops | Wash hands first. Do not touch the dropper to the eye or ear. Many are discarded 4 weeks after opening, as the label says |
| Inhalers | Teach the technique. Rinse the mouth after steroid inhalers |

## Working out the quantity from the directions

**Example.** *Amoxicillin 500 mg capsules. One capsule three times a day for 7 days.*

- 1 × 3 × 7 = **21 capsules**

**Example.** *Suspension, 5 mL twice a day for 5 days.*

- 5 × 2 × 5 = **50 mL**

The quantity on the label must match the quantity supplied.

## Good practice

- Choose the **most important** labels. Too many labels confuse the patient.
- Put the label on the **bottle, not the lid**, and keep it clean.
- Use **plain language** and, where needed, the local language or **pictograms**.
- **Counsel** the patient out loud, as well as using labels. Ask them to repeat the main points back.
- **Check every label** against the prescription before the medicine leaves the counter.

## Common label errors

| Error | Risk |
|---|---|
| Wrong patient or wrong medicine | Serious harm |
| Wrong dose or frequency | Under- or overdose |
| Missing warning | Patient is unaware of a danger |
| Smudged or unreadable label | Wrong use |
| Different quantity from the label | Course too short or too long |
