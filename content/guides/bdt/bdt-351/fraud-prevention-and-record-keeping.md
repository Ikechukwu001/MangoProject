## What is fraud?

**Fraud** is **dishonest deception** to gain something unfairly, such as money, stock or medicines. A pharmacy is at risk because it holds **stock that has value and can be sold**, handles **cash**, and receives **prescriptions** that can be copied or altered.

## Where fraud happens

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Prescriptions | Forged prescriptions, altered quantities or doses, stolen prescription pads, the same prescription used at several pharmacies, or a repeat prescription used again and again |
| Stock | Theft, issuing without a record, false counts, "ghost" issues to units that did not receive anything, and selling stock for personal gain |
| Cash | Money taken from sales, sales not recorded, receipts not issued, and false expenses |
| Records | Altered entries, missing pages, and forged signatures on vouchers |
| Suppliers | Short deliveries, goods of the wrong quality, or false invoices |

## Techniques to prevent fraud

### Prescriptions

- **Check** the prescription for signs of alteration, such as different handwriting or ink, or changes to the dose or quantity.
- Check the **prescriber's name, signature and registration details**, and that the date is recent.
- Be careful with **new patients** asking for strong painkillers, sedatives or other medicines that can be abused.
- **Record** the prescription, and mark it when it has been dispensed, so it cannot be used again.
- **Phone the prescriber** if you are unsure.
- Keep **controlled drugs** in a locked cupboard, with a register that is checked.

### Stock

- **Count** stock regularly, and compare it with the ledger.
- Use **bin cards** and **issue vouchers** for every movement.
- **Lock** the store, and keep the keys with an authorised person.
- Do not allow bags into the store.
- Carry out **surprise checks**.

### Cash

- Issue a **numbered receipt** for every sale, and keep the copy.
- **Count** the cash with a second person.
- **Bank** the money regularly and intact.
- **Check** the bank statement against the cash book.
- Never mix **personal money** with the pharmacy's money.

### Separation of duties

The most important rule: **no single person should control all the steps** of a transaction. For example, one person receives the stock, another records it, another issues it, and another checks the count. A loss or a false entry is then more likely to be seen.

Other helpful steps:

- **Rotate duties** now and then.
- Have all voucher entries **signed**.
- **Audit** the records by someone who does not keep them.
- Create a culture of **honesty**, where staff know that checks are routine and that they can raise concerns safely.

## What to do if fraud is suspected

1. **Do not** accuse anyone or act alone.
2. **Keep** the evidence, such as the prescription or the record.
3. **Tell** the pharmacist or manager at once.
4. **Write down** what you saw, with dates, times and names.
5. Follow the pharmacy's policy, which may include reporting to the regulator or the police.

## What is a record?

A **record** is a written (or electronic) account of something that happened, kept for later use. In a pharmacy, records include prescriptions, stock cards, vouchers, books of account, and registers of controlled drugs. The following lessons describe the main ones.

## Benefits of record keeping

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Accountability | It shows who did what, and where stock and money went |
| Control of stock | It shows what is in stock and what is needed |
| Better ordering | Past use is the basis for the next order |
| Detecting loss and fraud | Differences between records and stock show a problem |
| Patient safety | A dispensing record shows what a patient was given, for follow-up |
| Tracing and recall | Batch records let a faulty batch be found and withdrawn |
| Legal and audit needs | Records prove that rules were followed |
| Planning and budgeting | Totals show costs and use over time |
| Continuity | A new worker can take over, using the records |

## Good record keeping

- Write **clearly**, in ink, with the **date** and **signature**.
- Record **at the time** things happen, not later from memory.
- Never erase or use correction fluid. **Cross out with a single line**, write the correction, and initial it.
- **Keep records safe** and for the **time** required by the rules.
- Keep **copies** of important records in a different place.
