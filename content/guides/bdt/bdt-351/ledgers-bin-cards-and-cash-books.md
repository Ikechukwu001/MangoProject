## The books and what they do

| Book | What it records | Where it is kept |
|---|---|---|
| Bin card (stock card) | Stock received, issued and balance, for one item | With the stock |
| Stock ledger | The same information for each item | In the store office, apart from the stock |
| Cash book | All money received and paid, with the balance | With the person responsible for the accounts |

Both the bin card and the ledger are used because **one checks the other**. If the same person keeps both, an error or a theft can be hidden.

## The bin card and ledger

For each item, one line is written for each movement. The balance is worked out every time.

```
new balance = previous balance + received − issued
```

| Date | Reference | Received | Issued | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Oct | Opening | | | 300 |
| 4 Oct | RV 14 | 200 | | 500 |
| 9 Oct | IV 40 | | 150 | 350 |
| 15 Oct | IV 47 | | 100 | 250 |

The closing balance is **250**.

### Checking

1. Add up the received column, and the issued column.
2. Opening + received − issued should equal the closing balance: 300 + 200 − 250 = **250**.
3. Compare the **ledger**, the **bin card** and the **shelf count**.

**Example.** *The ledger and the bin card each show 450. The shelf count is 440.*

- The records agree with each other but not with the stock: a **deficiency of 10**.
- Investigate it: a wrong count, an issue not recorded, or a loss.

## The cash book

The **cash book** records every naira received and paid. A **two-column cash book** has a **cash** column and a **bank** column.

| | Cash | Bank |
|---|---|---|
| Opening balance | ₦20 000 | ₦100 000 |
| **Receipts** | | |
| Cash sales | ₦45 000 | |
| Cash paid into the bank | | ₦40 000 |
| **Payments** | | |
| Cash paid into the bank | ₦40 000 | |
| Expenses paid in cash | ₦5 000 | |
| Supplier paid by cheque | | ₦30 000 |
| **Closing balance** | **₦20 000** | **₦110 000** |

### Working

**Cash column**

- Money in = 20 000 + 45 000 = ₦65 000
- Money out = 40 000 + 5 000 = ₦45 000
- Closing cash = 65 000 − 45 000 = **₦20 000**

**Bank column**

- Money in = 100 000 + 40 000 = ₦140 000
- Money out = ₦30 000
- Closing bank = 140 000 − 30 000 = **₦110 000**

**Check.** Total of both = 20 000 + 110 000 = ₦130 000. Opening total was 120 000, plus sales 45 000, less expenses 5 000 and the supplier payment 30 000 = **₦130 000**. The money banked simply moved between columns.

### The banking entry

When cash is banked, **two entries** are made:

- a **payment** in the **cash** column (the cash box is lower);
- a **receipt** in the **bank** column (the bank is higher).

Entering it once would make the totals wrong.

## Checking the cash book against the bank statement

Each month, compare the bank column's balance with the **bank statement**. They may differ because of:

- **cheques issued but not yet cashed**;
- **deposits not yet cleared**;
- **bank charges** not yet in the cash book.

**Example.** *The cash book bank balance is ₦110 000. A cheque for ₦10 000 has not yet been cashed.*

- Bank statement should show 110 000 + 10 000 = **₦120 000**.

If a difference cannot be explained, it must be reported and investigated.

## Good practice

- Write up the books **every day**.
- Support every entry with a **voucher or receipt**.
- Never erase. **Cross out, write the correction and initial it.**
- Have the books **checked by another person**.
- Keep stock books and cash books **separate**, and keep them **safe**.

All amounts are made up for practice.
