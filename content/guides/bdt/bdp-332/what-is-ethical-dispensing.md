## A note on this course

BDP 332 in the curriculum lists one topic: **Ethical Dispensing**, and refers to the lecturer's own notes. These lessons give the general principles and good practice that apply in Nigerian pharmacy. If your school has lecture notes or a code of ethics, use their wording in an examination.

## What is ethics?

**Ethics** is the set of **moral principles** that guide what we think is right and wrong, and how we should act. **Professional ethics** is the set of standards that a profession expects of its members, because the public depends on them.

**Ethical dispensing** means supplying medicines in a way that is:

- **safe** for the patient;
- **honest**, with no lying or misleading;
- **lawful**, within the rules for pharmacy practice;
- in the **patient's best interest**, ahead of profit, speed or pressure.

## Why ethics matters in a pharmacy

A patient cannot check a medicine, a label or a dose for themselves. They **trust** the pharmacy to get it right. A mistake, a shortcut or a lie can **harm or kill**. Ethics is how the profession protects that trust.

## The principles

| Principle | Meaning | Dispensing example |
|---|---|---|
| Beneficence (doing good) | Act to benefit the patient | Give clear counselling so the patient takes the medicine correctly |
| Non-maleficence (avoiding harm) | Do not cause harm | Refuse to supply expired or damaged medicine, and check doses |
| Autonomy | Respect the patient's right to make decisions about their own care, with proper information | Explain the options and the risks, and respect an informed choice |
| Justice (fairness) | Treat patients fairly and equally | Serve people in order of need, without favour or discrimination |
| Honesty | Tell the truth | State the true price, and the true strength, source and expiry of the medicine |
| Confidentiality | Keep patient information private | Do not discuss a patient's medicines in public or with those who have no need to know |

Sometimes principles **conflict**. For example, respecting a patient's wish (autonomy) may risk harm. In such cases, ask the pharmacist.

## Legal and ethical are not the same

| | Legal | Ethical |
|---|---|---|
| Question asked | Is it allowed by law? | Is it right? |
| Source | Laws and regulations | Moral principles and professional standards |
| Example | A pharmacy may legally sell a medicine over the counter | It is unethical to sell a large quantity to someone who is clearly misusing it |

The law sets the **minimum**. Ethics asks for **more**. A good technician follows the law **and** asks what is right.

## The technician's role and limits

A pharmacy technician:

- works **under the supervision of a pharmacist**;
- performs tasks **within the technician's training and role**;
- does **not prescribe**, diagnose or change a prescription;
- **refers** questions beyond their role to the pharmacist;
- **keeps learning**, so that skills stay up to date.

Acting **outside** this role, even with good intentions, is an ethical and a legal risk.

## Habits of an ethical dispenser

- **Put the patient first**, every time.
- **Double-check** every prescription and label.
- **Admit mistakes** at once, so they can be fixed.
- **Respect privacy**, and speak quietly at the counter.
- **Be honest** about what the pharmacy has and what things cost.
- **Do not accept** gifts or pressure that could influence what is dispensed.
- **Behave the same** when no one is watching.
