## The dispensing process and the duties in it

Ethics is not only about big dilemmas. It is about **ordinary actions** at each step of the dispensing process.

## 1. Receiving the prescription

- Greet the patient politely, and treat everyone **fairly**, whatever their age, wealth, tribe, religion or illness.
- Check that the prescription is **complete, readable and valid**: patient, prescriber, date, medicine, strength, dose and quantity. See the lesson on prescriptions in BDT 251.
- **Query** anything that is unclear, unusual or unsafe. **Never guess.**
- Watch for signs of **alteration or forgery**, and report them to the pharmacist.
- Protect the patient's **privacy**. Do not ask for private details where others can hear.

### When to query or refuse

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Unclear writing, dose or quantity | Ask the pharmacist, who may contact the prescriber |
| A dose that seems too high or too low | Tell the pharmacist before preparing it |
| The medicine may interact with another the patient takes | Tell the pharmacist |
| The patient may be allergic | Tell the pharmacist |
| An altered or forged prescription | Do not supply. Tell the pharmacist |
| The medicine is not in stock | Tell the patient honestly, and refer them to where it is available |

## 2. Preparing the medicine

- Work in a **clean, orderly** space.
- Read the **label of the stock bottle three times**: when taking it, when measuring, and when putting it back.
- Check the **name, strength, form, batch and expiry date**.
- **Count and measure carefully.** Never rush, and never "estimate".
- **Do not substitute** one medicine for another unless the law and the pharmacist allow it, and the patient is told.
- **Do not change** the dose, the quantity or the directions. Only the prescriber and pharmacist can.
- Do **not** supply expired, damaged or suspect products.

## 3. Labelling and checking

- Make the label **accurate and clear**. See the lessons on labels.
- The **pharmacist checks** the final product before it is handed over.
- Never put the label of one patient on another's medicine.

## 4. Handing out and counselling

- Confirm **who the patient is**, by name, before handing over.
- Explain **how to take** the medicine, **for how long**, and the **main warnings** such as "finish the course" or "may cause drowsiness". Use plain words.
- **Check understanding**: ask the patient to repeat the main points.
- Speak **privately** where possible. Do not discuss the patient's illness or medicines in public.
- Be **honest** about the medicine's purpose and its side effects, and about the price.
- Do not give **advice beyond your role**. Refer it to the pharmacist.

## 5. Controlled and commonly abused medicines

Some medicines, such as strong painkillers, sedatives and stimulants, can be **misused or sold illegally**. Ethical duties include:

- keeping them in a **locked place** with limited access;
- recording **every receipt and issue** exactly, and checking the balance;
- supplying them **only on a valid prescription** or as the law allows;
- being alert to **repeated requests**, large quantities, or several prescribers;
- **reporting** concerns to the pharmacist.

## 6. Honesty in prices and stock

- Charge the **correct, fair price**. Do not overcharge or add hidden charges.
- Never **sell expired or damaged** medicines, or products of **unknown source**.
- Be careful about **fake and substandard** medicines. Buy only from approved suppliers, and report a suspect product.
- Do not push a **more expensive** product when a cheaper, equally good one will do, unless there is a reason.
- Do not **exaggerate** what a medicine can do.

## 7. Records

- Record what was dispensed, to whom, and when.
- Keep records **honest and complete**. Never alter or destroy a record to hide a mistake.
- Keep patient records **confidential and safe**.

## Putting it together

Before handing over any medicine, ask yourself:

1. Is it the **right patient, medicine, strength, dose and quantity**?
2. Is it **in date** and **in good condition**?
3. Has the **pharmacist checked** it?
4. Does the patient **understand** how to use it?
5. Would I be **comfortable explaining** what I did to the patient and the pharmacist?
