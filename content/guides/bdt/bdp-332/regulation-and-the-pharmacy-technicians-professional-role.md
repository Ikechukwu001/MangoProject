## Why regulation exists

Medicines can heal or harm, and patients cannot judge their quality for themselves. So governments set **rules** and appoint **regulators** to protect the public. A pharmacy technician must know **who regulates what**, and follow the rules.

This lesson gives the **main roles** of the bodies in Nigeria. Details of laws and regulations change, so check your school's notes and the current regulations for the exact wording.

## The main bodies

| Body | Main role |
|---|---|
| Pharmacists Council of Nigeria (PCN) | Regulates and controls the education, training and practice of pharmacy, including the registration of pharmacists, pharmacy technicians and pharmacy premises, and the standards of professional conduct |
| National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) | Regulates the registration, importation, manufacture, quality and safety of medicines, foods and other regulated products, and acts against fake and substandard products |
| National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) | Enforces the law on narcotic drugs and illegal substances |
| Federal and State Ministries of Health | Make health policy, and run public health services and supply programmes |
| Professional associations | Represent members and support professional standards and training |

A technician usually deals most with **PCN rules** on practice and **NAFDAC rules** on products.

## What the rules mean at work

- **Only registered or properly supervised persons** may carry out pharmacy tasks, in **registered premises**.
- Medicines must be **registered and in date**, and from **approved suppliers**.
- **Prescription-only medicines** are supplied only on a valid prescription.
- **Controlled drugs** need extra security and records.
- **Fake, substandard or expired** products must not be sold, and must be reported.
- A **pharmacist** is responsible for the pharmacy, and for supervising its staff.

## The technician's professional duties

| Duty | In practice |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Dispense and record correctly, and check twice |
| Honesty | Be truthful about stock, prices and mistakes |
| Confidentiality | Protect patient information |
| Safety | Store, handle and dispose of medicines safely |
| Teamwork | Support the pharmacist and colleagues, and communicate clearly |
| Record keeping | Keep accurate stock, dispensing and cash records |
| Reporting | Tell the pharmacist about errors, suspect products, adverse reactions and unsafe practice |
| Learning | Keep up to date with medicines, rules and practice |

## Limits of the role

A technician **does not**:

- **prescribe** medicines or **diagnose** illness;
- **change** a prescription;
- **supervise** the pharmacy alone, without a pharmacist;
- give **advice beyond their training**, without the pharmacist's guidance;
- **supply** prescription-only medicines without a valid prescription and pharmacist approval.

When something is beyond the role, **refer it to the pharmacist**. This is a mark of a professional, not a weakness.

## Professional conduct

- **Be on time**, reliable and properly dressed, with a **clean** coat and name badge.
- **Respect** patients and colleagues, whatever their background.
- Use **polite, clear language** and avoid slang or jokes about patients.
- Keep **personal phone use** away from the counter.
- Do not **smoke, eat or drink** in the dispensing area.
- Keep the **premises clean** and tidy.
- Do not **spread rumours**, or discuss patients outside work.
- **Do not take** pharmacy stock or money for personal use.
- Do not **use the pharmacy's name** to advertise anything unlawful.

## Reporting concerns

If you notice:

- a **suspect or fake** product,
- an **adverse reaction** to a medicine,
- an **error**,
- **unsafe or unlawful** practice,

tell the **pharmacist** at once. The pharmacist decides whether to report to **NAFDAC**, the **PCN** or another authority, following the proper procedure.

## Keeping up to date

Medicines, guidelines and rules change. A good technician:

- reads **notices** from the regulators and employers;
- attends **training** and **continuing education**;
- asks **questions** when unsure;
- learns from **mistakes**, their own and others'.
