## What is an ethical dilemma?

An **ethical dilemma** is a situation in which the **right action is not obvious**, or where **two good aims conflict**. For example, a patient wants a medicine, but supplying it might not be safe. Dilemmas are common, and a technician needs a **method** for dealing with them.

## A method for dilemmas

1. **Stop** and do not act in a hurry.
2. **Find the facts**: what is being asked, and what is the risk?
3. **Think about who could be harmed**, and who could benefit.
4. **Think of the principles**: safety, honesty, fairness, privacy, the patient's choice.
5. **Check the rules**: the law, the pharmacy's policy, your role.
6. **Ask the pharmacist.** You are not expected to carry the decision alone.
7. **Act**, politely and clearly.
8. **Record** what happened, if it matters.

## Common dilemmas at the counter

| Situation | A sound response |
|---|---|
| A customer asks for a prescription-only medicine without a prescription | Explain politely that it needs a prescription, offer to refer them to a doctor, and involve the pharmacist |
| A customer asks for antibiotics for a cold or fever | Explain that antibiotics may not help and can cause harm. Involve the pharmacist |
| Someone repeatedly buys large amounts of a medicine that can be abused | Tell the pharmacist. Do not make the sale without approval |
| A friend asks you to bend a rule | Treat friend and stranger the same. Rules are for safety |
| A patient cannot afford the full course | Tell the pharmacist. Do not hand out a partial or wrong course unless the pharmacist approves |
| The pharmacy is busy and queues are long | Do not skip checks. Ask for help or ask people to wait |
| A colleague is not following safe practice | Speak to the pharmacist or manager, in line with the pharmacy's policy |
| You find expired stock on the shelf | Remove it, record it, and tell the person in charge |

## Pressure and conflicts of interest

**Pressure** can come from customers, employers, colleagues or the wish to make money. It must **never decide** what is supplied.

A **conflict of interest** is a situation where your own gain, or that of someone close to you, could affect your judgment about the patient's good. Examples:

- accepting **gifts or payments** to recommend a product;
- pushing a product because it gives **a bigger profit**;
- dispensing for **family or friends** in a way that breaks the rules.

When there is a conflict of interest, **decline the gift**, **declare** the situation, or **ask the pharmacist** to deal with it.

## Dispensing errors

A **dispensing error** is a mistake in the process: the wrong patient, medicine, strength, form, dose, quantity, label or directions. Most errors are caught before they reach the patient. Some are not.

### Common causes

- **Rush**, interruptions and heavy workload
- **Tiredness**
- **Look-alike or sound-alike** medicine names and packs
- **Illegible** prescriptions
- **Poor storage**, such as similar items side by side
- **Skipping checks**
- **Poor communication**

### What to do when you find an error

1. **Protect the patient.** If the medicine has already been handed out, tell the pharmacist **at once**, so that the patient can be reached and advised.
2. **Do not hide it** or try to fix it quietly.
3. **Tell the pharmacist** exactly what happened.
4. **Help get the right medicine** to the patient as soon as possible.
5. **Record** what happened, including what was dispensed, what should have been, and the actions taken.
6. **Look for the cause** with the team, so it can be prevented. The aim is to **learn**, not to blame.

Be honest. An error reported **early** can usually be put right. An error that is **hidden** can harm the patient, and it usually costs a person their reputation when it comes out.

### Preventing errors

| Measure | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Read the label three times | Catches wrong stock |
| Independent check by a second person | Catches mistakes the first person misses |
| Store look-alike medicines apart | Reduces mix-ups |
| Clear, legible labels and records | Reduces misreading |
| A calm, uncluttered workspace | Reduces slips |
| Ask when in doubt | Resolves unclear prescriptions |
| Counselling and teach-back at hand-over | The patient can spot an error |
| Keep to one patient at a time | Avoids mixing up labels |

## Whistleblowing

If you see **unsafe or unethical practice**, such as selling expired products or serious misuse of medicines, report it to the pharmacist, the superintendent or the manager. If that fails, report it to the **regulator**. Speaking up protects patients, and doing so honestly and with facts is **not** disloyalty.
