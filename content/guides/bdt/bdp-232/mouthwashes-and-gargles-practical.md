## Definitions

| Preparation | Where it acts | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Mouthwash | The whole mouth: teeth, gums, tongue and cheeks | Rinsed around the mouth and spat out |
| Gargle | The throat and the back of the mouth | Held at the back of the throat with the head tilted back, bubbled with air, then spat out |

Both are **aqueous (water-based) liquids**, and both are **for local action only**. They are **not meant to be swallowed**.

## What they are used for

- **Freshening** the mouth and reducing odour.
- **Cleaning** the mouth after dental treatment, and as part of oral hygiene.
- **Soothing** a sore throat or sore mouth.
- **Antiseptic** action to reduce germs.
- **Delivering fluoride**, in some mouthwashes, to help protect teeth.

A sore mouth or throat that does not improve in a few days should be seen by a doctor or dentist.

## Common ingredients

Typical ingredients, depending on the product, are:

- an **antiseptic** or **astringent**;
- **salt** (sodium chloride) or **sodium bicarbonate**, in simple home-made rinses;
- **flavouring** such as peppermint;
- **colouring** and a **sweetener**;
- **alcohol** in some products, which is not suitable for everyone.

Follow the formula for what you are making, and ask the pharmacist about a preparation you do not recognise.

## Concentrated products and dilution

Many mouthwashes and gargles are supplied **concentrated** and are **diluted with water just before use**. This keeps the bottle small and the product stable, and it protects the mouth, because the concentrate may be too strong to use as it comes.

### What does "1 in 4" mean?

| Wording | Meaning | Concentrate | Water | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 in 4 | 1 part in 4 parts altogether | 1 part | 3 parts | 4 parts |
| 1 to 4 (as 1 + 4) | 1 part with 4 parts of water | 1 part | 4 parts | 5 parts |

People use these wordings loosely. For this reason, **always write the actual volumes on the label** and check with the pharmacist if you are not sure what is meant.

### Worked examples

**Example 1.** *Dilute a concentrated mouthwash 1 in 4 to make 40 mL.*

1. Concentrate = 40 ÷ 4 = **10 mL**
2. Water = 40 − 10 = **30 mL**

**Example 2.** *Dilute 1 in 10 to make 100 mL.*

1. Concentrate = 100 ÷ 10 = **10 mL**
2. Water = 100 − 10 = **90 mL**

**Example 3.** *Directions: add 10 mL of concentrate to 30 mL of water. How much does the patient make each time, and what is the dilution?*

- Total = 10 + 30 = **40 mL**
- Dilution = 10 ÷ 40 = 1 in 4

**Example 4.** *A 200 mL bottle of concentrate is used 10 mL at a time. How many rinses?*

- 200 ÷ 10 = **20 rinses**

## How to make and supply them

1. **Read** the prescription or the order and the formula.
2. **Calculate** the volumes.
3. Measure the concentrate and the water in a clean measuring cylinder, **at eye level**.
4. Mix gently. Use purified or freshly boiled and cooled water where the formula calls for it.
5. Transfer to a **clean bottle**, close it and label it.

A diluted product has a **short life**, because the preservative is weaker. Dilute **only what is needed**, and give a short discard date if you supply it diluted.

## Labelling

A mouthwash or gargle label should show:

- the name of the preparation, the patient's name and the date;
- the directions, for example "Rinse the mouth with 10 mL diluted with 30 mL of water for 1 minute, then spit out. Use twice a day";
- **"Not to be swallowed"** or "For rinsing the mouth only" or "For gargling only";
- a **warning to keep out of the reach of children**;
- the **discard date**, and any storage advice.

## Advice for the patient

**Mouthwash**

- Measure the dose and dilute as directed.
- Rinse for about the time stated, then **spit out**.
- Do not eat or drink for a short while afterwards if the pharmacist advises it, because food and drink wash the product off.

**Gargle**

- Tilt the head back, let the liquid reach the back of the throat, and make a gargling sound for about the time stated.
- **Spit it out.** Do not swallow it.
- Repeat as directed.

**Both**

- Keep out of the reach of children. Young children may swallow them.
- Stop and see a doctor or pharmacist if the mouth becomes more sore, or if there is a rash or swelling.
- Do not use a mouthwash in place of brushing the teeth.

## Safety and records

- Check the name and strength of the concentrate against the order.
- Never put a mouthwash in a bottle that looks like a drink, and always label it.
- Record any dilution made, with the volumes and the discard date.
- Tell the pharmacist if a product has changed colour or become cloudy.
