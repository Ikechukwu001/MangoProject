## What makes an indiffusible mixture different?

An **indiffusible solid** is a heavy powder that sinks quickly and may pack into a hard cake. Common examples are chalk, calamine and zinc oxide. If it is put in water alone, each dose of the shaken bottle would contain a different amount of drug.

A **suspending agent** makes the liquid thicker and coats the particles, so they settle slowly and are easy to shake back. Common suspending agents are:

- **compound tragacanth powder**
- **tragacanth** and **acacia**
- **sodium carboxymethylcellulose**
- **bentonite**

The amount to use comes from the formula. A figure often quoted for compound tragacanth powder is about 2 g in 100 mL, but use the formula from the reference your school follows, and check it with the pharmacist.

## Equipment

The equipment is the same as for a diffusible mixture: balance, mortar and pestle, measuring cylinder, glass rod, funnel and strainer, bottle and labels. Use a **large enough mortar**, because the mixture will need room for stirring.

## Method

1. **Calculate** all the quantities, including the suspending agent, and have them checked.
2. **Weigh** the indiffusible powder and the suspending agent.
3. **Powder** the solid finely in the mortar.
4. **Mix the dry powders together** in the mortar. The suspending agent must be spread through the powder before any liquid is added.
5. **Add the vehicle a little at a time**, stirring to a **smooth cream**, then more liquid to make it thinner.
6. **Dissolve the soluble ingredients** in some vehicle separately, strain if needed, and add them.
7. **Add the flavouring** and any other liquids.
8. **Transfer** to a measuring cylinder, then **rinse the mortar** with vehicle and add the rinsings.
9. **Make up to the final volume** and stir well.
10. **Bottle at once**, close and **label**, with **"Shake the bottle well before use"**.

### Why mix the powders dry first?

A suspending agent such as tragacanth swells when it meets water. If water is poured on it alone, the outside of each grain swells into a sticky skin before the inside is wet, and **lumps** form that are very hard to remove. Spreading it through the other powder keeps the grains apart, so the liquid can wet them evenly.

## Worked example: scaling the formula

*This is an illustrative practice formula, not a pharmacopoeial one. Use the formula your school or pharmacist gives you.*

*Chalk mixture, 100 mL: chalk 4 g, compound tragacanth powder 2 g, cinnamon water to 100 mL. Make 250 mL.*

1. Scale factor = 250 ÷ 100 = **2.5**
2. Chalk = 4 × 2.5 = **10 g**
3. Compound tragacanth powder = 2 × 2.5 = **5 g**
4. Cinnamon water to **250 mL**

**Check.** Chalk strength in the formula = 4 ÷ 100 × 100 = 4% w/v. In the scaled formula, 10 ÷ 250 × 100 = **4% w/v**, which is the same.

If the dose is 10 mL, there are 250 ÷ 10 = 25 doses, so each dose contains 10 ÷ 25 = **0.4 g** of chalk.

## Labelling

The label shows the same details as for any mixture, with:

- **"Shake the bottle well before use"**, which is vital for every suspension;
- the dose, in millilitres or 5 mL spoonfuls;
- the **discard date** and storage advice;
- any **warning** from the pharmacist.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Lumps of suspending agent | Liquid added to it before it was mixed with the powder | Start again, mixing the dry powders first |
| Powder sinks and cakes | Too little suspending agent, or none | Use the formula amount |
| Mixture too thick | Too much suspending agent | Check the weighing and the scaling |
| Air bubbles in the mixture | Vigorous stirring or shaking | Stir gently, and leave to settle before reading the volume |
| Gritty feel | The powder was not fine enough | Powder it more finely before mixing |

## Safety and records

- Chalk and other powders can be dusty. Weigh them carefully and avoid breathing the dust.
- Record the weights, the batch and the discard date.
- Let the pharmacist check the final product before it is given to the patient.
- Tell the patient to **shake the bottle** and to use a measuring spoon or oral syringe.
