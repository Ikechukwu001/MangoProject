## Before you start

A **diffusible solid** is a light powder that spreads easily through the liquid and stays spread long enough for a dose to be measured, so it **needs no suspending agent**. Common examples are light kaolin, light magnesium carbonate and magnesium trisilicate. See the lesson on mixtures for the theory.

Good practice at the bench:

- Wash your hands and wear a clean coat. Tie back long hair.
- Clean the bench, the mortar and pestle and the measuring cylinder.
- Read the **prescription** and the **formula** twice. Ask the pharmacist if anything is unclear.
- Calculate the quantities **before** you weigh anything, and have the pharmacist check them.
- Work calmly. A mistake in weighing cannot be undone after the powder is in the mortar.

## Equipment

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Balance and weights | Weighing the solids |
| Mortar and pestle | Powdering and mixing |
| Measuring cylinder | Measuring liquids and the final volume |
| Glass rod | Stirring and transferring |
| Funnel, strainer or muslin | Transferring and straining |
| Spatula and weighing boats or papers | Handling powders |
| Bottle and closure | Final container (clean, and amber for light-sensitive drugs) |
| Labels | Labelling the bottle |

## Method

1. **Calculate** the quantity of each ingredient for the volume to be made.
2. **Weigh** each solid. Use a clean boat or paper for each, and note down the weights.
3. **Dissolve** the soluble solids, such as sodium bicarbonate, in part of the vehicle in a beaker. Strain if the liquid is not clear.
4. **Powder** the diffusible solid in the mortar until it is fine.
5. **Add a little of the vehicle** to the powder and rub to a **smooth cream**, with no lumps.
6. **Add more vehicle gradually**, stirring all the time.
7. **Add the solution** from step 3, and any liquid ingredients such as flavouring.
8. **Transfer** to a measuring cylinder using the glass rod.
9. **Rinse the mortar** with a little of the vehicle. Add the rinsings to the cylinder.
10. **Make up to the final volume** with the vehicle and stir.
11. **Transfer** to the bottle, close it and **label** it.
12. **Clean up**, return the ingredients to the shelf, and record the preparation.

Check the measuring cylinder at **eye level**, reading the bottom of the curve of the liquid (the meniscus).

## Worked example: scaling a formula

*This is an illustrative practice formula, not a pharmacopoeial one. Use the formula your school or pharmacist gives you.*

*Antacid mixture, 100 mL: magnesium trisilicate 5 g, sodium bicarbonate 5 g, peppermint water to 100 mL. The prescription is 10 mL three times a day for 5 days.*

1. Volume needed = 10 × 3 × 5 = **150 mL**
2. Scale factor = 150 ÷ 100 = **1.5**
3. Magnesium trisilicate = 5 × 1.5 = **7.5 g**
4. Sodium bicarbonate = 5 × 1.5 = **7.5 g**
5. Peppermint water to **150 mL**

**Check.** Each 10 mL dose should contain 7.5 ÷ 15 = **0.5 g** of each solid, because there are 150 ÷ 10 = 15 doses. This agrees with the original formula, which has 5 g in 100 mL, or 0.5 g in 10 mL.

Always multiply **every** ingredient by the same factor, and check one dose at the end.

## Labelling

The label of a diffusible mixture shows:

- the patient's name, the date, and the name of the mixture;
- the directions, for example "Take TWO 5 mL spoonfuls THREE times a day";
- **"Shake the bottle well before use"**;
- the **discard date**, and storage advice;
- the name and address of the pharmacy.

A pharmacist checks the finished mixture, the label and the prescription before it is handed out.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Lumps in the mixture | Liquid added too quickly | Work the powder into a cream with a little vehicle first, then add the rest gradually |
| Powder left in the mortar | Mortar not rinsed | Rinse with the vehicle and add the rinsings |
| Volume too low or too high | Wrong reading of the cylinder | Read at eye level, and make up to the mark |
| Powder cakes at the bottom | The powder was not diffusible, or the wrong ingredient was used | Report to the pharmacist before dispensing |
| Fizzing or gas when ingredients meet | A reaction between ingredients | Stop and ask the pharmacist |

## Safety and records

- Clean every item between ingredients to prevent cross-contamination.
- Weigh carefully, and write the weights down.
- Keep a record of what was prepared, the batch, and the discard date, as the pharmacy requires.
- Never taste an ingredient or the mixture.
