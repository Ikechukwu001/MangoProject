## Three sweet liquids

| Preparation | What it is | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| Linctus | A thick, sweet, often syrupy liquid, taken in small sips | Cough and sore throat |
| Syrup | A concentrated solution of sugar in water, with or without a drug | A sweet, pleasant vehicle for drugs, especially for children |
| Elixir | A clear, sweet, flavoured liquid, with alcohol and water as the vehicle | A pleasant form of drugs that dissolve better with alcohol present |

All three are **oral liquids** and are measured with a **5 mL spoon** or an **oral syringe**.

## Linctuses

A **linctus** is thick and sweet so that it **coats the throat** and soothes it. The patient takes it in **small sips**.

Practical points:

- **Do not dilute** a linctus. Dilution makes it thin and ruins its soothing action.
- **Do not drink water straight after** the dose, as it washes the coating away.
- The usual dose is **5 mL**, taken slowly. The dose for children depends on age, so follow the prescription.
- A linctus is a **symptom reliever**. A cough that lasts more than a few days needs a doctor.
- Some linctuses contain drugs that cause **drowsiness**, so the label may warn the patient not to drive.

## Syrups

A **syrup** is a concentrated solution of **sugar (sucrose)** in water. The sugar:

- makes the preparation **sweet and pleasant**;
- makes it **thick**;
- **resists germs**, because the concentrated sugar holds the water tightly so germs cannot grow.

A plain syrup with no drug is the base for many medicated syrups and is called **simple syrup**. Many commercial syrups use other sweeteners instead of sugar. A **sugar-free** syrup is thickened with other agents, so check the label.

### Why dilution matters

If a syrup is **diluted**, the sugar becomes weaker and **germs can grow**. A diluted syrup should be freshly prepared, kept for a short time, and given a short discard date. A dilution is also a **new product**, so it should be done only on the pharmacist's instruction and with a suitable diluent.

## Elixirs

An **elixir** is a **clear, sweetened and flavoured** liquid. Its vehicle contains **alcohol and water**. The alcohol helps dissolve drugs that dissolve poorly in water alone.

Practical points:

- Elixirs are clear. If one turns cloudy, an ingredient has come out of solution, so tell the pharmacist.
- **Do not dilute** an elixir with too much water without advice, as the drug may come out of solution.
- The alcohol content matters for **children**, for people who avoid alcohol for health or religious reasons, and for people taking medicines that react with alcohol. Always check the label and advise the patient.

## Handling thick liquids

- Pour **slowly**, to avoid air bubbles and spills.
- Allow **extra time** for the liquid to drain from the measure. Otherwise some stays behind and the dose is short.
- Wipe the neck of the bottle so the closure does not stick.
- Use **measuring spoons or oral syringes** supplied with the medicine. Kitchen spoons vary in size.
- Keep the **cap tight**, and store as the label says.

## Worked examples

**Dose volume.** *A syrup contains 125 mg in 5 mL. The dose is 250 mg.*

- Volume = 250 ÷ 125 × 5 = **10 mL**

**Volume to supply.** *The dose is 5 mL four times a day for 5 days.*

- 5 × 4 × 5 = **100 mL**

**Alcohol in a dose.** *An elixir contains 10% v/v alcohol. The dose is 5 mL.*

- 5 × 10 ÷ 100 = **0.5 mL of alcohol** per dose

**Doses in a bottle.** *A bottle holds 100 mL and the dose is 5 mL.*

- 100 ÷ 5 = **20 doses**

## Labelling and advice

Label each preparation with the patient's name, the directions, the date and the discard date, and add advice that suits it:

| Preparation | Useful advice |
|---|---|
| Linctus | Sip slowly. Do not dilute. Do not drink water straight after |
| Syrup | Measure with a spoon or syringe. Brush teeth after a sweet syrup, particularly at night. Diabetic patients should check the sugar content |
| Elixir | Check the alcohol content with the pharmacist for children and others who should avoid alcohol |

Also tell the patient to **keep the bottle tightly closed**, **away from children**, and to **finish the course** if it is an antibiotic.

## Safety and records

- Check the name and strength on the bottle, and compare it with the prescription.
- Check the expiry date and the discard date of any opened bottle.
- Record any dilution, with the diluent, the amount and the discard date.
- Do not give a syrup that has crystals, mould or a changed smell. Tell the pharmacist.
