## What is a simple solution mixture?

A **simple solution mixture** is an oral liquid in which **every ingredient is dissolved** in the vehicle, usually water or an aromatic water. The liquid is **clear**, so the patient does not need to shake the bottle, and every dose has the same amount of drug.

## Solubility

**Solubility** tells you how much liquid is needed to dissolve a solid. It is often given as the **number of millilitres of water needed to dissolve 1 g**.

| Description | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Very soluble | A very small volume dissolves 1 g |
| Soluble | A modest volume dissolves 1 g |
| Slightly soluble | A large volume is needed for 1 g |
| Insoluble | It does not dissolve in a usable volume |

If a drug is **insoluble** it cannot go in a solution mixture. It needs a suspension instead. See the lessons on diffusible and indiffusible mixtures.

### Worked example

*1 g of a drug dissolves in 3 mL of water. How much water is needed for 5 g?*

- 5 × 3 = **15 mL**

If the **volume of the dose** is too small for this much water, the drug will not all dissolve. Ask the pharmacist.

## Equipment

Balance and weights, mortar and pestle, beakers, a glass rod, measuring cylinders, a funnel with filter paper or muslin, a bottle and a label. Everything must be **clean**. Wash with purified water at the end.

## Method

1. **Calculate** the quantity of every ingredient, and have it checked.
2. **Weigh** the solids. **Crush** any large crystals in a mortar, because small particles dissolve faster.
3. **Dissolve** the solids in **part of the vehicle** in a beaker, **starting with the slowest to dissolve**. Stir with a glass rod. Do not heat unless the formula allows it, because heat can break down some drugs.
4. **Strain or filter** if the liquid is not clear.
5. **Add the other liquids**, such as syrups, tinctures and flavourings, **after** the solids have dissolved.
6. **Transfer** to a measuring cylinder.
7. **Rinse** the beaker and mortar with some vehicle and add the rinsings.
8. **Make up** to the final volume with the vehicle, reading at eye level.
9. **Transfer** to the bottle, close it and **label** it.
10. **Clean up** and **record** the preparation.

### Why add syrup late?

Syrup is thick and **slows the dissolving of solids**. It is also measured by volume, and a thick liquid clings to the cylinder, so measure it carefully and rinse the cylinder with some of the vehicle.

## Worked example: scaling the formula

*Illustrative practice formula, not a pharmacopoeial one.*

*Sodium citrate mixture, 100 mL: sodium citrate 3 g, syrup 10 mL, water to 100 mL. Make 150 mL.*

1. Scale factor = 150 ÷ 100 = **1.5**
2. Sodium citrate = 3 × 1.5 = **4.5 g**
3. Syrup = 10 × 1.5 = **15 mL**
4. Water to **150 mL**

**Check.** Strength of sodium citrate = 3 ÷ 100 × 100 = 3% w/v. In the scaled mixture, 4.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = **3% w/v**.

For a 10 mL dose there are 150 ÷ 10 = 15 doses, so each dose holds 4.5 ÷ 15 = **0.3 g** of sodium citrate.

## Labelling

A solution mixture label carries:

- the patient's name, the date, and the name of the mixture;
- the **dose in millilitres** or 5 mL spoonfuls, and how often to take it;
- the **discard date** and storage advice;
- any warning that the pharmacist adds.

Because the liquid is a clear solution, "Shake the bottle" is not needed. Give the patient a **5 mL spoon** or an **oral syringe**.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Solid does not dissolve | Not enough vehicle, or crystals too large | Check the solubility, crush the crystals and stir longer |
| Cloudiness | A solid is not fully dissolved, or fine particles are present | Filter, if the liquid should be clear |
| Precipitate forms | Ingredients are incompatible, or one is too concentrated | Stop and tell the pharmacist |
| Volume wrong | Cylinder read incorrectly | Read at eye level, and make up to the mark |
| Mould or cloudiness later | No preservative, or contaminated equipment | Use clean equipment, a short discard date and proper storage |

## Safety and records

- Check each ingredient against the formula twice.
- Never taste an ingredient or the mixture.
- Record the weights, the volumes and the discard date.
- The pharmacist checks the preparation and the label before it is handed out.
