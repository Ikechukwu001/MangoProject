## Why powders for reconstitution?

Many drugs, including **many antibiotics**, are **unstable in water**. In a ready-made liquid they would lose strength within days. So the manufacturer supplies them as a **dry powder or granules** in a bottle. The pharmacy adds **water just before supply**, which gives a liquid that is good for a limited time. Children and others who cannot swallow tablets benefit most.

Before reconstitution the product has a **long shelf life**, shown by the **expiry date** on the bottle. After reconstitution it has a **short shelf life**, shown by the **discard date** you put on the label.

## Equipment and water

- A clean **measuring cylinder** (or the mark on the bottle)
- A **funnel**, if needed
- **Purified water**, or water that was **boiled and cooled**, as the manufacturer directs. Tap water can carry germs and chemicals.
- A label, a measuring spoon or an oral syringe, and a pen

Wash your hands and clean the bench before you begin.

## Method

1. **Read the label**: the volume of water, the way of mixing, the storage and the shelf life after mixing. Follow it exactly.
2. **Check** the product name, the strength and the **expiry date** of the powder. Do not use it if it has expired.
3. **Tap** the bottle gently on the bench, or shake it, to **loosen the powder**.
4. **Add the water** as directed. Many products say to add the water in **two parts**, shaking after each, which mixes the powder better.
5. **Shake well** until the powder is completely mixed, with no lumps.
6. **Top up** with water to the **mark** on the bottle if the label says so, and shake again.
7. **Label** the bottle, with the date and the discard date.
8. **Record** the reconstitution, and let the pharmacist check it.

Always measure the water with care. **If the volume is wrong, the strength of every dose is wrong.**

## Worked examples

**Dose volume.** *Strength 125 mg in 5 mL. The dose is 250 mg.*

- Volume = 250 ÷ 125 × 5 = **10 mL**

**Quantity to supply.** *The dose is 10 mL three times a day for 5 days.*

- 10 × 3 × 5 = **150 mL**

**Number of bottles.** *Each bottle makes 100 mL.*

- 150 mL needs **2 bottles**, since one bottle gives only 100 mL. The pharmacist decides how to supply a quantity that is not a whole bottle.

**A dose by body weight.** *A child weighs 20 kg. The dose is 10 mg per kg. The strength is 125 mg in 5 mL.*

1. Dose = 10 × 20 = **200 mg**
2. Volume = 200 ÷ 125 × 5 = **8 mL**

Doses by body weight are always set by the prescriber, and checked by the pharmacist. These numbers are for practice.

**Discard date.** *Reconstituted on 4 October. Shelf life 7 days, with the day of reconstitution as day 0.*

- 4 + 7 = **11 October**

The shelf life after reconstitution, and whether to keep the bottle in a refrigerator, come from the manufacturer's label. Do not use a fixed number from memory.

## Labelling and advice

The label of a reconstituted medicine shows:

- the patient's name, the name and strength of the medicine, and the dose;
- the **date of reconstitution** and the **discard date**;
- **"Shake the bottle well before use"**;
- the storage advice, such as "Store in a refrigerator" if the label says so;
- any other advice, such as "Finish the course".

Tell the patient to:

- **shake the bottle** before each dose;
- use the **measuring spoon or oral syringe**, not a kitchen spoon;
- **finish the whole course**, even if the child feels better, unless a doctor says otherwise;
- **throw away** what is left after the discard date;
- keep the bottle **out of the reach of children**.

## Common errors

| Error | Result | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong volume of water | Every dose too weak or too strong | Measure to the mark, and follow the label |
| Powder not loosened first | Lumps and an uneven mixture | Tap the bottle before adding water |
| Tap water used | Germs or chemicals get into the medicine | Use purified water, or boiled and cooled water |
| No discard date | Medicine used after it has lost strength | Always put the discard date on the label |
| Not shaken before use | Uneven doses | Label "Shake the bottle well before use" and tell the patient |

## Safety and records

- Check the product against the prescription before you begin.
- Record the batch number and expiry date of the powder, the date of reconstitution and the discard date.
- Allergies matter, so ask the pharmacist to check, particularly for penicillin-type antibiotics.
