## What are aromatic waters?

An **aromatic water** is **water holding a small amount of a volatile (easily evaporating) oil** or another aromatic substance. It has the **taste and smell** of the oil but is clear or slightly cloudy.

| Aromatic water | Smell and use |
|---|---|
| Peppermint water | Flavouring, and a mild aid for wind |
| Cinnamon water | Flavouring |
| Dill water | Flavouring, and traditionally given for infant wind |
| Chloroform water | A preservative and flavour in mixtures. Only small, controlled amounts are used |

Aromatic waters are used as the **vehicle** in mixtures, so that the medicine tastes and smells pleasant.

## How they are supplied

Making an aromatic water from the oil is slow, and the oil dissolves poorly in water. In most pharmacies the water is made by **diluting a concentrated aromatic water** with **purified water**. The label of the concentrate gives the dilution, and common ones are around **1 in 40**. Always use the dilution stated on the bottle you are using.

## Making an aromatic water from a concentrate

1. **Read** the label for the dilution.
2. **Calculate** the volume of concentrate for the volume wanted.
3. **Measure** the concentrate in a **small** measuring cylinder, because the volume is small.
4. **Transfer** it to a larger measuring cylinder.
5. **Add purified water** and make up to the **final volume**. Stir.
6. **Transfer** to a clean bottle and label it.

### Worked example

*Dilution 1 in 40. Make 200 mL.*

- Concentrate = 200 ÷ 40 = **5 mL**
- Add purified water to **200 mL**, which is 195 mL of water.

Notice that the water is added **to make up to 200 mL**. It is not 200 mL added to the concentrate.

## Dilutions in general

Often a strong solution has to be made weaker. The amount of drug stays the same, but the volume increases.

```
strength wanted × volume wanted = strength of stock × volume of stock
```

### Worked examples

**Example 1.** *Make 100 mL of a 2% solution from a 10% stock.*

1. 10 × V = 2 × 100
2. V = 200 ÷ 10 = **20 mL** of the 10% solution
3. Add water to **100 mL**. Water used = 100 − 20 = 80 mL.

**Example 2.** *Make 250 mL of a 1% solution from a 5% stock.*

1. 5 × V = 1 × 250
2. V = 250 ÷ 5 = **50 mL**
3. Add water to **250 mL**. Water = 200 mL.

**Example 3.** *How many times has Example 1 been diluted?*

- 10% to 2% is a dilution of 10 ÷ 2 = **5 times**, which is a 1 in 5 dilution.

**Check.** The amount of drug in Example 1 is 10 g per 100 mL × 20 mL = 2 g. In the final solution, 2 g per 100 mL = 2%. It agrees.

## Storage and labelling

- Use **clean, dry equipment**, and **purified water**.
- Water supports the growth of germs and moulds, so aromatic waters have a **short shelf life**. Make only the amount needed.
- Store in a **well-closed container**, in a **cool place** and out of strong sunlight.
- Label with the **name**, the **strength or dilution**, the **date** and the **discard date**.
- A bottle that becomes **cloudy**, grows a skin or smells off must be thrown away.

## Safety and records

- Check the label of the concentrate: name, strength and expiry date.
- Record the volumes used and the discard date.
- Chloroform water and similar waters are used only in the amounts the formula allows. Ask the pharmacist if you are unsure.
