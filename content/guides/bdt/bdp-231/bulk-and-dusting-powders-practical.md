## Two kinds of powder

| | Bulk powder for internal use | Dusting powder |
|---|---|---|
| Route | By mouth | On the skin |
| How supplied | One container. The patient measures each dose with a spoon | A container with a sifter top |
| Examples | Antacid powders, such as magnesium trisilicate compound powder | Talc, starch and zinc oxide powders |
| Particle size | Fine enough to mix and measure | Very fine and smooth, so they do not scratch the skin |

For the theory, including divided powders, see the lesson on powders and granules in BDT 152.

## Bulk powders for internal use

A bulk powder is **not divided into doses** by the dispenser. The patient measures each dose, usually with a **5 mL spoon**, and takes it with water or mixes it into water.

Because the patient measures the dose, a bulk powder is **only suitable for drugs that are not potent**, where a small error does no harm, such as **antacids**. A potent drug is given as **divided powders** in individual papers, or as another form.

## Dusting powders

A dusting powder is a **fine, free-flowing powder** applied to the skin to **absorb moisture**, **reduce friction** or **soothe**. The usual bases are talc, starch and zinc oxide.

- A dusting powder is **never swallowed**.
- It should be very fine, and it is **sieved** to remove grit.
- A powder used on **open wounds or broken skin** must be **sterile**. Ordinary dusting powders should not be put on open wounds.
- Do not use a dusting powder on a **baby's** skin near the face, as breathing in the dust can harm the lungs.

## Method

1. **Calculate** the quantities for the amount ordered and have them checked.
2. **Weigh** each ingredient on a clean boat or paper.
3. **Powder** each ingredient in a mortar if it is coarse.
4. **Sieve** each powder through a fine sieve, to remove lumps and grit.
5. **Mix** them, starting with the **smallest quantity** and adding the larger quantities in turn. This is geometric dilution, which gives an even mixture.
6. **Sieve the mixture again** to blend it.
7. **Pack** it into the container.
8. **Label** it, and record the preparation.

Work in a clean place, and avoid creating dust. Wipe the balance and the bench afterwards.

## Worked examples

*Illustrative practice formula, not a pharmacopoeial one.*

**Scaling.** *Dusting powder, 100 g: zinc oxide 10 g, starch 40 g, talc 50 g. Make 30 g.*

1. Scale factor = 30 ÷ 100 = **0.3**
2. Zinc oxide = 10 × 0.3 = **3 g**
3. Starch = 40 × 0.3 = **12 g**
4. Talc = 50 × 0.3 = **15 g**
5. **Check:** 3 + 12 + 15 = **30 g**, so the parts add up to the total.

**Quantity of bulk powder.** *The dose is 3 g three times a day for 7 days.*

- 3 × 3 × 7 = **63 g**

**Number of doses.** *A jar holds 63 g and each dose is 3 g.*

- 63 ÷ 3 = **21 doses**

## Packing and labelling

**Bulk powder**

- Pack in a wide-mouthed, **airtight, dry jar**, because many powders absorb moisture.
- Label with the name, the dose and how to take it, the date and the patient's name. Supply a **5 mL measuring spoon**.

**Dusting powder**

- Pack in a **sifter-top container**, such as a plastic or metal shaker.
- Label **"For external use only"**, and the directions, such as "Dust lightly on the affected area after washing and drying".

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Uneven colour or lumps | Poor mixing, or damp powder | Mix again by geometric dilution and sieve |
| Gritty feel | Coarse powder or no sieving | Powder and sieve it again |
| Powder caked in the jar | Moisture got in | Use a dry jar with a tight lid, and keep it cool and dry |
| Dust in the air | Careless pouring | Handle gently, and keep the face away from the dust |

## Safety

- Powders are **dusty**. Do not inhale them, and keep the area clean.
- Check the ingredients against the formula twice before weighing.
- Never supply a potent drug as a bulk powder. Ask the pharmacist.
