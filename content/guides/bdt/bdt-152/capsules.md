## What is a capsule?

A **capsule** is a solid dosage form in which the drug is enclosed in a small shell that dissolves in the stomach. The shell is usually made of **gelatin**. Once the shell dissolves, the contents are released.

## Types of capsule

| Type | Description | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Hard gelatin capsule | Two pieces: a **body** and a **cap** that slides over it | Dry powders, granules, pellets |
| Soft gelatin capsule | One sealed piece with a flexible, thicker shell | Oils, liquids, suspensions (for example, some vitamin and cod liver oil capsules) |
| Enteric-coated capsule | Capsule or contents coated to resist stomach acid | Drugs that must be released in the intestine |
| Vegetarian capsule | Shell made from plant material (such as cellulose) instead of gelatin | Powders and granules, for patients who avoid animal products |

Gelatin comes from animal sources. Some patients avoid it for religious or personal reasons, so plant-based capsules are useful alternatives to know about.

## Capsule sizes

Hard capsules come in numbered sizes. The numbering can seem backwards:

- **000** is the **largest**
- then **00**, **0**, **1**, **2**, **3**, **4**
- **5** is the **smallest**

The largest holds about 1.4 mL and the smallest about 0.13 mL.

**Choosing a size:** the right size depends on the **volume** the dose takes up, not on its weight. A light, fluffy powder takes up more room than a dense powder of the same weight, so it needs a bigger capsule.

## Advantages and disadvantages

**Advantages**
- They hide the **taste and smell** of the drug.
- The shell is smooth and slippery, so capsules are easy to swallow.
- The shell dissolves quickly, so the drug is released quickly.
- They can hold powders, oils and other liquids.
- Opaque shells protect light-sensitive drugs.
- Small numbers can be filled by hand in the pharmacy.

**Disadvantages**
- Gelatin is damaged by **heat and moisture**.
- A hard capsule cannot hold a water-based liquid, because the water dissolves the shell.
- Very soluble drugs that irritate the stomach can cause problems, because the drug is released all at once in one spot.
- Capsules cannot be divided for a smaller dose.
- More expensive than tablets.

### Capsules compared with tablets

| | Capsule | Tablet |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Drug inside a shell | Compressed solid |
| Taste masking | Very good | Needs coating |
| Can hold liquids | Yes (soft capsules) | No |
| Sensitive to humidity | Very | Less so |
| Can be divided | No | Some can (scored tablets) |

## Filling hard capsules by hand

On a small scale, hard capsules are filled in the pharmacy:

1. Pull the **cap** off each capsule and place the **bodies** in a holder or tray.
2. Weigh the correct amount of **powder** and make sure it is well mixed.
3. Fill each body with the powder. Pressing the body into a layer of powder (the punch method) packs it in. Use a spatula to fill any remaining space.
4. Replace each **cap** and press until it clicks shut.
5. Wipe the capsules clean and check them.
6. Pack and label.

In industry, capsule-filling machines do the same job at high speed.

## Packaging and storage

- Pack capsules in **blister packs** or **tightly closed bottles** with a drying agent if needed.
- Store in a **cool, dry place**. In hot, humid conditions gelatin shells become soft, sticky and misshapen.
- Do not store in a bathroom or a hot vehicle.
- Keep the container tightly closed, and always check the expiry date.

## Helping patients take capsules correctly

- Sit or stand upright.
- Swallow the capsule whole with a **full glass of water**. Do not take it dry, as it can stick in the food pipe.
- Do not open or chew a capsule unless the label says to.
- Keep out of reach of children.
