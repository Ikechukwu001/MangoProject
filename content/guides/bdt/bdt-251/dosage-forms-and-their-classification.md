## What is a dosage form?

A **dosage form** is the physical form in which a medicine is made, supplied and given to the patient. A tablet, a syrup, a cream and an injection are all dosage forms.

The same drug is often available in more than one dosage form. Paracetamol, for example, comes as tablets, syrup, suspension and suppositories. The right one depends on who the patient is and how they need to take it.

## Why not give the drug on its own?

A pure drug is rarely given directly, because:

- the dose is often tiny (a few milligrams) and impossible to measure accurately by hand;
- it may taste bad, irritate the tissues or break down quickly;
- it needs to be in a form that suits the route, such as swallowing, injecting or applying to the skin.

The dosage form solves these problems.

## Components of an ideal dosage form

Every dosage form has two parts:

1. **The active ingredient**, the drug that treats the condition.
2. **The excipients**, all the other ingredients that make the product usable.

| Excipient | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Diluent or filler | Adds bulk | Lactose |
| Binder | Holds a tablet together | Starch paste |
| Vehicle or solvent | Carries the drug in a liquid | Purified water |
| Preservative | Stops microbes growing | Methyl hydroxybenzoate |
| Sweetener and flavour | Improve taste | Sucrose |
| Colour | Appearance and identification | Approved dyes |
| Stabiliser | Keeps the product from breaking down | Antioxidants |

An **ideal dosage form** delivers the correct dose accurately, is stable, safe, easy to use and acceptable to the patient.

## Classification by physical state

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Solid | Powders, granules, tablets, capsules, suppositories, pessaries, lozenges |
| Liquid | Solutions, syrups, elixirs, linctuses, mixtures, suspensions, emulsions, drops, lotions, mouthwashes, injections |
| Semi-solid | Ointments, creams, pastes, gels |
| Gases and aerosols | Inhalers, sprays, medical gases |

### Solid dosage forms

These are shaped solids. Powders, granules, tablets and capsules are swallowed. Suppositories and pessaries are inserted into the rectum or vagina. Lozenges are dissolved slowly in the mouth. The earlier lessons in BDT 152 describe each in detail.

### Liquid dosage forms

| Form | Definition |
|---|---|
| Solution | A clear liquid in which the drug is dissolved |
| Syrup | A concentrated solution of sugar in water, usually with a drug and flavour |
| Elixir | A clear, sweetened solution containing water and alcohol |
| Linctus | A thick, sweet liquid for cough and sore throat, sipped slowly and not washed down with water |
| Mixture | An oral liquid in a bottle with one or more drugs dissolved or suspended in a vehicle |
| Suspension | Fine solid particles spread through a liquid. Shake before use |
| Emulsion | Two liquids that do not mix (such as oil and water), with one spread through the other as tiny droplets |
| Drops | Liquid given by the drop into the eye, ear or nose, or by mouth |
| Lotion | A liquid for application to the skin without rubbing |
| Mouthwash and gargle | Liquids used to rinse the mouth or throat and then spat out |

**Parenteral dosage forms** are liquids (or powders made into liquids) given by **injection** into the body, bypassing the gut. They include injections given into a vein (intravenous), a muscle (intramuscular) and under the skin (subcutaneous). Parenterals must be **sterile** and free from particles.

### Semi-solid dosage forms

| Form | Definition |
|---|---|
| Ointment | A greasy semi-solid for the skin. It stays on the skin and forms a protective layer |
| Cream | A semi-solid emulsion. Less greasy than an ointment, spreads easily and washes off with water |
| Paste | A stiff ointment that contains a large amount of powder, such as zinc oxide paste. It stays where it is put and protects the skin |
| Gel | A clear or translucent semi-solid made with a gelling agent, often water-based |

### Gases and aerosols

An **aerosol** is a product in a pressurised container that releases the drug as a fine mist or spray. Examples are asthma inhalers and throat sprays. **Medical gases**, such as oxygen, are also in this group.

## Classification by route

Dosage forms can also be grouped by how they are given:

| Route | Examples |
|---|---|
| Oral (by mouth) | Tablets, capsules, syrups, mixtures |
| Topical (on the skin) | Creams, ointments, lotions |
| Parenteral (by injection) | Injections |
| Rectal | Suppositories |
| Vaginal | Pessaries |
| Inhalation | Inhalers |
| Ocular and otic | Eye drops, ear drops |

## Choosing a dosage form

The choice depends on:

- the **age** of the patient: syrups for small children, for example;
- whether the patient can **swallow**;
- how **fast** the effect is needed: an injection works faster than a tablet;
- where the drug must act: a cream for a skin infection;
- whether the drug is **stable** in that form;
- what the patient prefers, and what is available and affordable.
