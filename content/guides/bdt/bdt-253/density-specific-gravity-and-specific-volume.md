## Why do we need this?

In the pharmacy some liquids are easier to **measure** than to **weigh** (oils, syrups, glycerol), but a formula may give the quantity in **grams**. Others are written in millilitres when you want to weigh them. **Specific gravity** lets you move between weight and volume.

## Density

**Density** is the mass of a substance in one unit of its volume.

```
density = mass ÷ volume
```

In pharmacy it is usually written in grams per millilitre (g/mL). Water has a density of **1 g/mL**.

**Example.** *50 mL of a liquid weighs 62.5 g.* Density = 62.5 ÷ 50 = **1.25 g/mL**.

## Specific gravity

**Specific gravity (SG)**, also called relative density, compares a substance with water.

```
SG = weight of a substance ÷ weight of an equal volume of water
```

- SG has **no units**, because it is a ratio of two weights.
- Because 1 mL of water weighs 1 g, the **number** for SG is the same as the density in g/mL.
- A liquid with SG **greater than 1** is heavier than water and sinks in it. A liquid with SG **less than 1** is lighter than water and floats on it.
- SG changes slightly with temperature, so it is measured at a stated temperature.

### Some common values

| Liquid | SG (approximate) |
|---|---|
| Water | 1.00 |
| Glycerol | 1.25 |
| Syrup | 1.33 |
| Chloroform | 1.48 |
| Castor oil | 0.96 |
| Liquid paraffin | 0.87 |
| Alcohol (about 95%) | 0.81 |

Use the value given in the question or in your reference book. Values vary a little between sources.

## Converting between weight and volume

```
weight = volume × SG
volume = weight ÷ SG
```

A quick check: for a liquid heavier than water (SG above 1), the weight in grams is **bigger** than the volume in millilitres. For a liquid lighter than water, the weight is **smaller**.

**Example 1.** *What is the weight of 50 mL of glycerol (SG 1.25)?*

- 50 × 1.25 = **62.5 g**

**Example 2.** *What is the weight of 200 mL of a liquid with SG 0.90?*

- 200 × 0.90 = **180 g**

**Example 3.** *What volume does 150 g of syrup (SG 1.33) occupy?*

- 150 ÷ 1.33 = 112.78 = **112.8 mL**

**Example 4.** *A formula needs 100 g of liquid paraffin (SG 0.87). What volume do you measure?*

- 100 ÷ 0.87 = 114.94 = **114.9 mL**

**Example 5.** *A prescription needs 30 mL of glycerol (SG 1.25), but your formula is in grams. How many grams?*

- 30 × 1.25 = **37.5 g**

## Specific volume

**Specific volume** is the volume (in mL) taken up by **1 gram** of the substance.

```
specific volume = 1 ÷ SG
```

- Glycerol, SG 1.25: specific volume = 1 ÷ 1.25 = **0.8 mL/g**
- Alcohol, SG 0.81: specific volume = 1 ÷ 0.81 = 1.23 mL/g

To find a volume: **volume = weight × specific volume**. For 50 g of alcohol: 50 × 1.2346 = 61.7 mL.

## Measuring specific gravity

### Specific gravity bottle (pycnometer)

A **specific gravity bottle** holds an exact volume. You weigh it three times:

1. empty,
2. full of **water**,
3. full of the **liquid**.

```
SG = (weight of liquid) ÷ (weight of water)
```

**Example.** *Empty bottle 25.0 g. Filled with water 75.0 g. Filled with the liquid 87.5 g.*

1. Weight of water = 75.0 − 25.0 = 50.0 g
2. Weight of liquid = 87.5 − 25.0 = 62.5 g
3. SG = 62.5 ÷ 50.0 = **1.25**

Remember to **subtract the empty bottle** each time. Using 87.5 ÷ 75.0 = 1.17 would be a mistake.

### Hydrometers

A **hydrometer** is a weighted glass float that sinks to a depth depending on the density of the liquid. You read the SG from the scale at the liquid surface. Special hydrometers have special names, such as the **alcoholmeter** (for alcohol), the **lactometer** (for milk) and the **urinometer** (for urine).
