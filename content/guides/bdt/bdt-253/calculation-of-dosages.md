## A note before you start

The doses used in this lesson are made-up numbers for practising arithmetic. Real doses come from the prescriber and from approved references. Always confirm a dose with the pharmacist.

## Dose terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Single dose | The amount taken at one time |
| Daily dose | The total amount taken in 24 hours |
| Total dose | The total for the whole course |
| Frequency | How often it is taken |

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Doses per day |
|---|---|---|
| od | once daily | 1 |
| bd | twice daily | 2 |
| tds | three times daily | 3 |
| qds | four times daily | 4 |

## Quantity to supply

```
total quantity = amount per dose × doses per day × number of days
```

**Example 1.** *Paracetamol 120 mg/5 mL, 10 mL qds for 3 days.*

- 10 mL × 4 × 3 = **120 mL**

**Example 2.** *A tablet, one three times a day for 7 days.*

- 1 × 3 × 7 = **21 tablets**

### How long does a supply last?

```
days = total quantity ÷ quantity used per day
```

- A 100 mL bottle at 5 mL twice a day: 5 × 2 = 10 mL a day, and 100 ÷ 10 = **10 days**.
- A 150 mL bottle at 10 mL three times a day: 10 × 3 = 30 mL a day, and 150 ÷ 30 = **5 days**.
- Number of 5 mL doses in a 150 mL bottle: 150 ÷ 5 = **30 doses**.

### Divided doses

*The daily dose is 30 mg per kg, in 3 divided doses. The child weighs 20 kg.*

1. Daily dose = 30 × 20 = 600 mg
2. Each dose = 600 ÷ 3 = **200 mg**

## Using the stock strength

### Tablets and capsules

```
number of tablets = dose wanted ÷ strength of one tablet
```

*The dose is 750 mg and the tablets are 250 mg.* 750 ÷ 250 = **3 tablets**.

### Liquids

```
volume to give = (dose wanted ÷ drug in stock volume) × stock volume
```

**Example 1.** *The dose is 250 mg. The stock suspension is 125 mg in 5 mL.*

- (250 ÷ 125) × 5 = 2 × 5 = **10 mL**

**Example 2.** *The dose is 90 mg. The stock is 120 mg in 5 mL.*

- (90 ÷ 120) × 5 = 0.75 × 5 = **3.75 mL**

Round to a volume your oral syringe can measure, such as 3.8 mL, and check it with the pharmacist.

**Example 3: drug in each dose from the total.** *A mixture contains 600 mg in 120 mL. How much is in each 5 mL dose?*

- Strength = 600 ÷ 120 = 5 mg per mL
- 5 mL dose = 5 × 5 = **25 mg**

## Approximate and exact equivalents

| Item | Exact | Approximate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 grain | 64.8 mg | 65 mg |
| 1 minim | 0.0592 mL | 0.06 mL |
| 1 teaspoonful | | 5 mL |
| 1 kilogram | 2.205 lb | 2.2 lb |

- Use **approximate** equivalents for converting a dose written in older units into the metric dose that is actually given. A dose of 5 grains is taken as 325 mg (5 × 65).
- Use **exact** equivalents when you are calculating quantities to prepare or dispense. The exact value for 5 grains is 5 × 64.8 = 324 mg.

## Children's doses

A child is not a small adult. Children differ in weight, in how their bodies handle medicines, and in how quickly they grow, so a dose is chosen carefully.

The **safest method** is to use a dose written per kilogram of body weight, or a dose banded by age or weight from an approved children's reference. The rules below give an estimate only. Use them when a weight-based dose is not available, and always have the result checked.

| Rule | Formula | For |
|---|---|---|
| Young's rule | age (years) ÷ (age + 12) × adult dose | Children 1 to 12 years |
| Clark's rule | weight (kg) ÷ 70 × adult dose | Children, by weight |
| Fried's rule | age (months) ÷ 150 × adult dose | Infants under 1 year |
| Body surface area | child's BSA (m²) ÷ 1.73 × adult dose | Most reliable of the rules |

**Young's rule.** *Adult dose 500 mg. Child aged 4 years.*

- 4 ÷ (4 + 12) = 4 ÷ 16 = 1/4
- 1/4 × 500 = **125 mg**

**Clark's rule.** *Adult dose 300 mg. Child weighs 21 kg.*

- 21 ÷ 70 = 0.3
- 0.3 × 300 = **90 mg**

**Fried's rule.** *Adult dose 150 mg. Infant aged 10 months.*

- 10 ÷ 150 × 150 = **10 mg**

**Body surface area.** *Adult dose 100 mg. Child's surface area 0.80 m².*

- 0.80 ÷ 1.73 = 0.4624
- 0.4624 × 100 = 46.2 mg, which rounds to **46 mg**

**Dose per kilogram.** *The prescribed dose is 15 mg per kg. The child weighs 12 kg. The suspension is 120 mg in 5 mL.*

1. Dose = 15 × 12 = **180 mg**
2. Volume = (180 ÷ 120) × 5 = 1.5 × 5 = **7.5 mL**

## Miscellaneous dosage problems

**How many tablets for a course?** *One tablet three times a day for 5 days.* 1 × 3 × 5 = **15 tablets**.

**Working back from a total.** *You have 60 mL of a mixture and the dose is 10 mL three times a day. How many days will it last?* 10 × 3 = 30 mL a day, and 60 ÷ 30 = **2 days**.

**Check every answer** by estimating first, and make sure it is a volume or number of tablets that the patient can actually measure and take.
