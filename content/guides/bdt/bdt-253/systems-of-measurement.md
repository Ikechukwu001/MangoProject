## Why systems of measurement matter

Medicines are measured by mass, volume and length. Most of pharmacy now uses the **metric system**, but older systems still appear in some books, old prescriptions and imported labels. You need to read all of them and convert safely.

## The metric system

The metric system is based on powers of ten, which makes conversion simple.

| Quantity | Base unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | gram | g |
| Volume | litre | L |
| Length | metre | m |

### Prefixes

| Prefix | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| kilo | k | 1 000 |
| deci | d | 0.1 |
| centi | c | 0.01 |
| milli | m | 0.001 |
| micro | µ or mc | 0.000 001 |
| nano | n | 0.000 000 001 |

### Mass and volume tables

| Mass | |
|---|---|
| 1 kilogram (kg) | 1 000 grams |
| 1 gram (g) | 1 000 milligrams (mg) |
| 1 milligram (mg) | 1 000 micrograms |
| 1 microgram | 1 000 nanograms |

| Volume | |
|---|---|
| 1 litre (L) | 1 000 millilitres (mL) |
| 1 decilitre (dL) | 100 mL |
| 1 millilitre (mL) | 1 cubic centimetre (cm³) |

### Converting within the metric system

- **Larger unit to smaller unit: multiply** (the number gets bigger).
- **Smaller unit to larger unit: divide** (the number gets smaller).

| Conversion | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25 g to mg | 0.25 × 1 000 | **250 mg** |
| 750 mg to g | 750 ÷ 1 000 | **0.75 g** |
| 500 micrograms to mg | 500 ÷ 1 000 | **0.5 mg** |
| 1.5 L to mL | 1.5 × 1 000 | **1 500 mL** |
| 2.5 kg to g | 2.5 × 1 000 | **2 500 g** |
| 0.06 g to mg | 0.06 × 1 000 | **60 mg** |

## The apothecaries' system

The apothecaries' system was once the standard for dispensing. It is still found in older reference books.

**Weight**

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 20 grains (gr) | 1 scruple |
| 3 scruples | 1 drachm (60 grains) |
| 8 drachms | 1 ounce (480 grains) |
| 12 ounces | 1 pound (5 760 grains) |

**Fluid measure (imperial)**

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 60 minims (min) | 1 fluid drachm |
| 8 fluid drachms | 1 fluid ounce |
| 20 fluid ounces | 1 pint |
| 8 pints | 1 gallon |

The American pint and fluid ounce are different sizes from the imperial ones. Check which system a book or label uses.

## The avoirdupois system

The avoirdupois system is used for everyday goods and is the system found in the shop.

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 437.5 grains | 1 ounce (oz) |
| 16 ounces | 1 pound (lb), 7 000 grains |

The **grain** is the same in both systems, but the **ounce** is not: the apothecaries' ounce is 480 grains and the avoirdupois ounce is 437.5 grains.

## Equivalents

| Item | Exact | Approximate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 grain | 64.8 mg | 65 mg |
| 1 minim | 0.0592 mL | 0.06 mL |
| 1 ounce (avoirdupois) | 28.35 g | 28 g |
| 1 pound | 453.6 g | 454 g |
| 1 kilogram | 2.205 lb | 2.2 lb |
| 1 fluid ounce (imperial) | 28.4 mL | |
| 1 pint (imperial) | 568 mL | |

### Worked conversions

- **5 grains to mg:** 5 × 64.8 = **324 mg** exact. With the approximate value of 65 mg, 5 × 65 = **325 mg**.
- **2 fluid ounces to mL:** 2 × 28.4 = **56.8 mL**.
- **8 ounces (avoirdupois) to g:** 8 × 28.35 = **226.8 g**.
- **A patient weighs 154 lb. Weight in kg?** 154 ÷ 2.2 = **70 kg**.
- **Half an imperial pint in mL:** 568 ÷ 2 = **284 mL**.

## Household measures

| Measure | Volume |
|---|---|
| 1 teaspoonful | 5 mL |
| 1 dessertspoonful | 10 mL |
| 1 tablespoonful | 15 mL |

Ordinary kitchen spoons vary widely in size. For accuracy, give the patient a **5 mL measuring spoon**, an **oral syringe** or a **measuring cup**. A bottle of 100 mL at 5 mL per dose holds 100 ÷ 5 = **20 doses**.

## Temperature

```
°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
```

| Example | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 37°C to °F | 37 × 1.8 = 66.6, + 32 | **98.6°F** |
| 25°C to °F | 25 × 1.8 = 45, + 32 | **77°F** |
| 104°F to °C | (104 − 32) = 72, × 5/9 | **40°C** |
| 2°C to °F | 2 × 1.8 = 3.6, + 32 | **35.6°F** |
| 8°C to °F | 8 × 1.8 = 14.4, + 32 | **46.4°F** |

A refrigerator for medicines is normally kept between 2°C and 8°C, which is 35.6°F to 46.4°F.

## Writing units safely

Unclear units cause serious errors. Follow these habits:

- Write **"microgram"** in full, or "mcg", rather than "µg". The "µ" can be misread as "mg", which is a thousand times larger.
- Write **"units"** in full, never "U", which can be read as a zero.
- Write **"mL"** and not "cc".
- Use a **zero before** a decimal point (0.5 mg) and **no zero after** a whole number (5 mg).
- Leave a **space** between the number and the unit.
