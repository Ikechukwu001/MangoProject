## Ways of writing strength

The **strength** of a preparation is the amount of drug in a stated amount of the product. It is most often written as a **percentage**, but there are different kinds of percentage, and each one means something different.

| Type | Meaning | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **% w/w** (weight in weight) | grams of ingredient in **100 g** of product | Ointments, creams, powders |
| **% w/v** (weight in volume) | grams of ingredient in **100 mL** of product | Solutions and suspensions of solids |
| **% v/v** (volume in volume) | millilitres of ingredient in **100 mL** of product | Mixtures of liquids, such as alcohol in water |
| **% v/w** (volume in weight) | millilitres of ingredient in **100 g** of product | Liquids in semi-solids (not common) |

### When the type is not stated

The usual convention is:

- a **solid in a liquid** is **w/v**;
- a **liquid in a liquid** is **v/v**;
- a **solid in a solid** (or semi-solid) is **w/w**.

So "a 2% solution of sodium bicarbonate" means 2% w/v, and "a 5% ointment" means 5% w/w.

## The basic calculation

```
amount of drug = percentage × quantity of preparation ÷ 100
```

**Example 1.** *How many grams of drug are in 250 mL of a 2% w/v solution?*

- 2 × 250 ÷ 100 = **5 g**

**Example 2.** *Make 500 g of a 5% w/w ointment. How much drug and how much base?*

- Drug = 5 × 500 ÷ 100 = **25 g**
- Base = 500 − 25 = **475 g**

**Example 3: finding the percentage.** *3 g of drug is dissolved in water to make 60 mL. What is the % w/v?*

- 3 ÷ 60 × 100 = **5% w/v**

**Example 4: v/v.** *How much alcohol is in 200 mL of a 70% v/v solution?*

- 70 × 200 ÷ 100 = **140 mL**

**Example 5: amount in a tube.** *A 15 g tube holds a 0.5% w/w cream. How many milligrams of drug?*

- 0.5 × 15 ÷ 100 = 0.075 g = **75 mg**

**Example 6: tablets.** *Make 400 mL of a 0.5% w/v solution from 500 mg tablets. How many tablets?*

1. Drug needed = 0.5 × 400 ÷ 100 = 2 g = 2 000 mg
2. Tablets = 2 000 ÷ 500 = **4 tablets**

## Milligrams per millilitre

A percentage w/v is easy to turn into milligrams per millilitre:

| Strength | Meaning | mg per mL |
|---|---|---|
| 1% w/v | 1 g in 100 mL | **10 mg/mL** |
| 0.1% w/v | 0.1 g in 100 mL | **1 mg/mL** |
| 0.01% w/v | 0.01 g in 100 mL | **0.1 mg/mL** |

To change % w/v to mg/mL, **multiply by 10**.

**Example 7: dose from a strength.** *A 2% w/v solution is given in 5 mL doses. How much drug is in one dose?*

- 2% w/v = 20 mg/mL
- 5 mL × 20 mg/mL = **100 mg**

## Ratio strength

A strength can also be written as a **ratio**, such as **1 in 1000** or 1 : 1000. This means **1 part in 1000 parts**:

- for a solid in a liquid: **1 g in 1000 mL**;
- for a liquid in a liquid: **1 mL in 1000 mL**;
- for a solid in a solid: **1 g in 1000 g**.

### Converting between ratio strength and percentage

```
percentage = 100 ÷ X        (for a ratio of 1 in X)
X = 100 ÷ percentage
```

| Ratio strength | Working | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1 in 1 000 | 100 ÷ 1 000 | **0.1%** |
| 1 in 200 | 100 ÷ 200 | **0.5%** |
| 1 in 2 500 | 100 ÷ 2 500 | **0.04%** |
| 1 in 5 000 | 100 ÷ 5 000 | **0.02%** |

**Percentage to ratio:** *0.25% is 1 in how many?* X = 100 ÷ 0.25 = 400, so **1 in 400**.

**Example 8.** *How many milligrams of drug are in 1 mL of a 1 in 5 000 solution?*

- 1 g in 5 000 mL = 1 000 mg in 5 000 mL = 1 000 ÷ 5 000 = **0.2 mg per mL**

## Weight in weight and weight in volume

A w/w strength is in grams per 100 **grams** of product. A w/v strength is in grams per 100 **millilitres**. They are the same only when the product has the same density as water.

To change a w/w strength to w/v for a liquid, **multiply by the specific gravity**:

```
% w/v = % w/w × SG
```

**Example.** *A solution is 10% w/w and has SG 1.05. What is the % w/v?*

- 10 × 1.05 = **10.5% w/v**

This makes sense: 100 g of this solution is less than 100 mL, so the same 10 g of drug is packed into a smaller volume, giving a higher strength per 100 mL.
