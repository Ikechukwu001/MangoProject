## Tonicity and osmosis

Body cells are surrounded by a thin membrane that lets water pass through but not most dissolved substances. Water moves across this membrane from a **weaker** solution to a **stronger** one. This movement is called **osmosis**.

The strength of a solution in terms of osmosis is its **tonicity**.

| Type | Compared with body fluids | What happens to red blood cells |
|---|---|---|
| **Isotonic** | Same osmotic pressure | Stay the same size |
| **Hypotonic** | Lower osmotic pressure (weaker) | Water enters, so they **swell** and may **burst** (haemolysis) |
| **Hypertonic** | Higher osmotic pressure (stronger) | Water leaves, so they **shrink** (crenation) |

A solution of **0.9% w/v sodium chloride** (normal saline) is isotonic with blood plasma. About 5% glucose is also roughly isotonic. Blood plasma freezes at about −0.52°C, and any solution that freezes at that temperature is isotonic with it.

## Why tonicity matters

Solutions that are put into delicate tissues must be close to isotonic:

- **Injections** that are far from isotonic can damage blood cells, cause pain and harm tissue.
- **Eye drops** that are not isotonic sting and can irritate or damage the eye.
- **Nasal drops** can irritate the lining of the nose and damage the tiny hairs (cilia) that clear it.

Oral medicines and solutions for intact skin do not need to be isotonic.

## Sodium chloride equivalents

Different drugs affect osmotic pressure by different amounts. To compare them, each drug is given a **sodium chloride equivalent**, written **E**.

> The **sodium chloride equivalent (E)** of a drug is the weight of sodium chloride that has the same osmotic effect as **1 g** of the drug.

Some typical values:

| Substance | E |
|---|---|
| Sodium chloride | 1.00 |
| Boric acid | 0.50 |
| Ephedrine hydrochloride | 0.30 |
| Pilocarpine nitrate | 0.23 |
| Atropine sulfate | 0.13 |

Values differ slightly between reference books. In an exam or in practice, use the value from the table you are given.

## Making a solution isotonic

1. Work out the **sodium chloride needed** for the volume to be isotonic:
   `0.9 g × (volume in mL ÷ 100)`
2. Work out the **sodium chloride equivalent of the drug**:
   `weight of drug × E`
3. Subtract:
   `NaCl to add = (step 1) − (step 2)`

Then dissolve the drug and the sodium chloride and make up to volume with water.

**Example 1.** *Make 100 mL of 1% w/v atropine sulfate eye drops isotonic. E = 0.13.*

1. NaCl for 100 mL = 0.9 g
2. Drug = 1 g, and 1 × 0.13 = 0.13 g NaCl equivalent
3. NaCl to add = 0.9 − 0.13 = **0.77 g**

**Example 2.** *Make 30 mL of 2% w/v pilocarpine nitrate eye drops isotonic. E = 0.23.*

1. NaCl for 30 mL = 0.9 × 30 ÷ 100 = 0.27 g
2. Drug = 2 × 30 ÷ 100 = 0.6 g, and 0.6 × 0.23 = 0.138 g NaCl equivalent
3. NaCl to add = 0.27 − 0.138 = **0.132 g**

**Example 3.** *Make 60 mL of 1% w/v ephedrine hydrochloride nasal drops isotonic. E = 0.30.*

1. NaCl for 60 mL = 0.9 × 60 ÷ 100 = 0.54 g
2. Drug = 0.6 g, and 0.6 × 0.30 = 0.18 g NaCl equivalent
3. NaCl to add = 0.54 − 0.18 = **0.36 g**

**Example 4: two drugs.** *Make 100 mL containing 0.5% atropine sulfate (E = 0.13) and 1% pilocarpine nitrate (E = 0.23) isotonic.*

1. NaCl for 100 mL = 0.9 g
2. Atropine sulfate: 0.5 × 0.13 = 0.065 g. Pilocarpine nitrate: 1 × 0.23 = 0.23 g. Total = 0.295 g
3. NaCl to add = 0.9 − 0.295 = **0.605 g**

### If the answer is zero or negative

If the drug alone gives 0.9 g or more of sodium chloride equivalent per 100 mL, the solution is already isotonic or **hypertonic**. No sodium chloride is added. Refer a hypertonic result to the pharmacist.

## The strength that is isotonic by itself

A drug solution is isotonic by itself at:

```
isotonic strength (% w/v) = 0.9 ÷ E
```

**Example.** *Boric acid, E = 0.50.* 0.9 ÷ 0.50 = **1.8% w/v**.

## Using another substance instead of sodium chloride

Sometimes a different substance is used to adjust tonicity. Divide the sodium chloride weight you need by the **E of the new substance**.

**Example.** *In Example 1, 0.77 g of sodium chloride was needed. How much boric acid (E = 0.50) gives the same effect?*

- 0.77 ÷ 0.50 = **1.54 g** of boric acid

## Good practice

- Eye drops, nasal drops and injections must also be **sterile**, with the right preparation method and containers. Follow the pharmacist's instructions.
- Use **purified water** (or water for injections for injections) to make up to volume.
- Check the **E value** against the reference table, and check the working with the pharmacist.
