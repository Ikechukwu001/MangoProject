## What is a cream?

A **cream** is a **semi-solid emulsion** for application to the skin. It contains **water**, **oils or waxes** and an **emulsifying agent**. It is softer and less greasy than an ointment and is usually **easy to wash off**.

| | Cream | Ointment |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Contains water | Usually none |
| Feel | Smooth, less greasy | Greasy |
| Washes off | Yes (aqueous) | Difficult |
| Preservative needed | Yes | Usually not |
| Shelf life | Shorter | Longer |

## Oily and aqueous creams

| | Aqueous cream | Oily cream |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Oil-in-water (O/W) | Water-in-oil (W/O) |
| Feel | Light, not greasy, vanishes into the skin | Heavier, greasy, stays on the skin |
| Washing | Washes off with water | Does not wash off easily |
| Used for | Soothing, moisturising, as a base for other medicines | Very dry skin, protection |
| Example | Aqueous cream | Zinc cream |

## Equipment

Water bath or hot plate, evaporating dish or beaker, thermometer, glass rod or spatula, balance, ointment slab or tile, mortar, ointment jars or tubes, and labels. All must be **clean and dry**.

## Making an aqueous cream by the heat method

*A typical formula: emulsifying ointment 30 g, preservative 1 g, purified water to 100 g.* Use the formula your school provides.

1. **Calculate** the quantities and have them checked.
2. **Weigh** the emulsifying ointment (the oil phase) into a dish and **melt it** on a water bath.
3. **Heat the water** with the dissolved preservative to the **same temperature** as the oil phase (about 60 to 70 °C).
4. **Add the water phase to the oil phase**, stirring all the time.
5. **Stir until the cream is cool** and has thickened, to avoid a grainy texture. Do not stop stirring while it is warm.
6. **Weigh** the cream and make up to the final weight with purified water if some has evaporated.
7. **Pack** into a clean jar and label.

### Worked example: scaling

*Make 200 g of aqueous cream.*

1. Scale factor = 200 ÷ 100 = **2**
2. Emulsifying ointment = 30 × 2 = **60 g**
3. Preservative = 1 × 2 = **2 g**
4. Purified water = 200 − 60 − 2 = **138 g**

**Check.** 60 + 2 + 138 = **200 g**.

### Points to watch

- Use a **gentle heat**. Do not boil.
- Always **add the water to the oil phase**, not the other way, as directed.
- **Keep stirring** until cool.
- Use **purified or boiled water**, because tap water can carry germs.
- Clean equipment is vital. Germs in a cream are hard to see until it has spoiled.

## Oily creams

An oily cream such as **zinc cream** contains a larger proportion of oils, with a smaller part water. It is made by the same idea: the **oils are melted**, and the **water phase** is added at the same temperature and stirred until cool. Follow the formula exactly, because the amount of each ingredient decides which type of emulsion forms.

## Diluting a cream

A prescriber may ask for a weaker cream than the one in stock. Dilute it **only** with the **proper base**, as the formula or the pharmacist says. Using the wrong diluent can break the emulsion or alter the preservative.

```
strength wanted × weight wanted = strength of stock × weight of stock
```

### Worked example

*Make 100 g of a 5% w/w cream from a 10% w/w stock cream.*

1. 5 × 100 = 10 × x
2. x = 500 ÷ 10 = **50 g** of the 10% cream
3. Base = 100 − 50 = **50 g**

Mix on an ointment slab with a spatula until smooth, or in a mortar, by **adding the base to the stock cream in portions**.

Diluted creams are usually given a **short discard date**, as a diluted product has a weaker preservative.

## Packing, labelling and storage

- Use a **wide-mouthed jar**, or a **collapsible tube**, which is cleaner because the cream is squeezed out.
- Fill the jar **without air gaps**, and smooth the top.
- Label: patient's name, name and strength of the cream, directions, **"For external use only"**, the date, and the **discard date**.
- Store in a **cool place**, and out of direct sunlight. Do not freeze.
- Tell the patient: **wash the hands before and after use**; use a clean spatula or clean fingers; do not put used cream back in the jar.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Grainy cream | The phases were at different temperatures, or stirring stopped early | Make again at the right temperature and stir until cool |
| Oily separated layer | Cracked emulsion | Remake it |
| Water drops on the surface | Not enough emulsifier, or the cream was cold when mixed | Remake it |
| Mould or odour | Contamination, or no preservative | Discard, and review hygiene |
