## What is an emulsion?

An **emulsion** is a mixture of **two liquids that do not mix**, such as oil and water. One liquid is spread through the other as **tiny droplets**. A third ingredient, the **emulsifying agent**, forms a film around the droplets and **stops them joining together**.

| Part | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| The droplets | Internal (dispersed) phase | The liquid in small drops |
| The liquid around the droplets | External (continuous) phase | The liquid that surrounds them |

## Types

| Type | Droplets | Outer liquid | Use | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oil-in-water (O/W) | Oil | Water | Oral emulsions, and many creams | Not greasy, washes off with water |
| Water-in-oil (W/O) | Water | Oil | Some external products | Greasy, stays on the skin |

### Simple tests for the type

- **Dilution test:** an emulsion mixes with its outer liquid. An O/W emulsion mixes with water. A W/O emulsion does not.
- **Dye test:** a water-soluble dye colours the outer phase of an O/W emulsion evenly.

## Emulsifying agents

| Agent | Notes |
|---|---|
| Acacia (gum arabic) | The usual agent for oral emulsions. Makes O/W |
| Tragacanth | Thickens and stabilises. Used with acacia |
| Methylcellulose | Thickens the water, and can act as an emulsifier |
| Soaps, such as those from wool fat or fatty acids | Used in external creams and lotions |
| Synthetic agents (Tweens and Spans) | Used in many creams and lotions |

The amount and type of agent are taken from the formula. Do not change them.

## Making an oral emulsion

For a fixed oil such as arachis oil or castor oil, a **primary emulsion** is made first, in the ratio:

```
oil : water : gum  =  4 : 2 : 1
```

The primary emulsion is then **diluted** with the rest of the water to the final volume.

### Worked example

*Make a primary emulsion for 30 mL of fixed oil.*

1. 4 parts = 30 mL, so 1 part = 30 ÷ 4 = **7.5**
2. Gum = 1 part = **7.5 g**
3. Water = 2 parts = 2 × 7.5 = **15 mL**

Mineral oils and volatile oils use slightly different ratios, so follow the formula, for example 3 : 2 : 1 for liquid paraffin.

### Dry gum method (Continental method)

1. Put the **gum** in a **dry mortar**.
2. Add the **oil** and mix lightly with the pestle.
3. Add the **water all at once**, and stir briskly and **in one direction** until a thick, white, creamy mixture forms. You hear a characteristic clicking or crackling sound.
4. Stir for about a minute more to complete the **primary emulsion**.
5. **Dilute** with water, a little at a time, stirring.
6. Add the **soluble ingredients and flavour**, then make up to the volume.
7. Transfer to the bottle and label.

### Wet gum method (English method)

1. In the mortar, make a **thick mucilage** by mixing the **gum with the water**.
2. Add the **oil in small amounts**, stirring constantly, until the primary emulsion forms.
3. **Dilute** and make up to volume as above.

The dry gum method is quicker. The wet gum method is useful when the gum is lumpy or when you need more control.

### Points to watch

- Use a **dry, wide, clean mortar**, not a glass one with a smooth surface, because a rough surface helps.
- Add the water **all at once** in the dry gum method, because it must reach the right concentration to form the primary emulsion.
- Stir **in one direction**, quickly.
- Add alcohol, strong salts or acids only **after the emulsion is made**, since they can break it.
- Add **flavouring and preservative** after dilution.

## Instability

| Problem | What happens | Can it be reversed? |
|---|---|---|
| Creaming | A layer of oil or cream gathers at the top or bottom | Yes, by shaking |
| Cracking (breaking) | The oil and water separate and will not mix again | No, so remake it |
| Phase inversion | O/W changes to W/O or the other way, often with too little emulsifier or too much heat | No |

Causes: too little emulsifying agent, wrong ratio of oil, water and gum, too much heat or cold, strong salts or alcohol, germs, and not enough mixing.

## Labelling and storage

- **"Shake the bottle well before use."**
- A **discard date**, because emulsions contain water and can grow germs. Use a **preservative** where the formula says.
- Store in a **cool place**. Do not freeze, as freezing breaks the emulsion.
- Supply a 5 mL spoon or an oral syringe.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The primary emulsion is thin and oily | The ratio was wrong, or too much water was added | Remake it |
| Oil layer that will not shake in | Cracked | Remake it |
| Lumps of gum | The gum was not dry, or was added slowly | Powder the gum, mix with oil first |
| Air bubbles | Too much beating | Stir more gently, and leave to settle |
