## Definitions

| Preparation | Definition | How it is applied |
|---|---|---|
| Lotion | A liquid for **external application** to the skin, without rubbing | Dabbed, painted or spread on gently, often on a pad of cotton wool |
| Liniment | A liquid for external application **with rubbing** | Massaged in, to relieve muscle and joint pain |

Both are **for external use only**.

## Lotions

Lotions can be:

- **solutions**, for example an antiseptic lotion;
- **suspensions**, for example **calamine lotion**, in which solids are spread through water;
- **emulsions**, for example some moisturising lotions.

They are used to **cool**, **soothe**, **protect**, **clean** or **treat** the skin. Lotions that contain water cool the skin as the water evaporates. Glycerol is added to some lotions to keep the skin from drying.

### Calamine lotion

This is a **suspension lotion**. The powders (calamine and zinc oxide) are **heavy and do not dissolve**, so the lotion needs a **suspending agent**, such as bentonite, and must be shaken.

*Illustrative practice formula, not a pharmacopoeial one. Use the formula your school gives you.*

*Calamine lotion, 100 mL: calamine 15 g, zinc oxide 5 g, bentonite 3 g, glycerol 5 mL, purified water to 100 mL.*

**Method**

1. **Sprinkle the bentonite** on some of the water and **leave** it to swell and form a smooth mucilage.
2. **Mix** the calamine and zinc oxide **in a mortar** with the glycerol to make a smooth paste.
3. **Add the bentonite mucilage** gradually to the paste, stirring until smooth.
4. **Transfer** to a measuring cylinder, rinse the mortar and add the rinsings.
5. **Make up** to the final volume with purified water.
6. Put into a bottle, and label: **"Shake the bottle well before use. For external use only."**

**Scaling example.** *Make 60 mL.*

1. Scale factor = 60 ÷ 100 = **0.6**
2. Calamine = 15 × 0.6 = **9 g**
3. Zinc oxide = 5 × 0.6 = **3 g**
4. Bentonite = 3 × 0.6 = **1.8 g**
5. Glycerol = 5 × 0.6 = **3 mL**
6. Purified water to **60 mL**

### Using a suspension lotion

Shake the bottle. Shake a little onto cotton wool and **dab** onto the skin. Do not rub. Let it dry. Use it on **unbroken** skin such as a rash or insect bites, as advised.

## Liniments

A **liniment** is rubbed in. Most contain:

- an **oil** (such as arachis oil or olive oil), which lets the hand glide, **or** alcohol or water;
- one or more **counter-irritants**, such as **methyl salicylate**, camphor or menthol, which warm the area and ease pain.

### Oily liniment

*Illustrative practice formula.*

*Methyl salicylate liniment: methyl salicylate 25 mL, arachis oil to 100 mL.*

**Method**

1. **Measure** the arachis oil in a **dry** measuring cylinder.
2. Measure the **methyl salicylate** in a **small, dry** measuring cylinder.
3. **Mix** them in a dry bottle or beaker, and make up to the volume with arachis oil.
4. Label.

Everything must be **dry**, because water does not mix with oil.

**Scaling example.** *Make 40 mL.*

- Methyl salicylate = 40 × 25 ÷ 100 = **10 mL**
- Arachis oil to **40 mL**, which is 30 mL of oil

### Safety with liniments

- **For external use only**, and **never swallowed**.
- Do not apply to **broken or sore skin**, the **eyes**, the **mouth** or other mucous membranes.
- Do not cover with a **tight bandage** or **heat pad**, which can cause burns.
- **Wash the hands** after use.
- Methyl salicylate is **poisonous if swallowed**, even in small amounts, especially by children, so use a **child-resistant closure** where possible and keep out of reach.

## Containers and labels

- Use a **fluted (ribbed) bottle** or a coloured bottle for external products, so it cannot be mistaken for medicine to be swallowed.
- Label **"For external use only"** in clear letters.
- Add **"Shake the bottle well before use"** for suspensions and emulsions.
- Give the **date** and **discard date**, **directions**, and the patient's name.
- Warn about **fire** for liniments with alcohol, because they are flammable.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Lumps in calamine lotion | Bentonite was not left to swell, or was added too quickly | Prepare the mucilage properly, and add gradually |
| Powder cakes | Not enough suspending agent | Use the formula amount |
| Liniment cloudy | Water got in | Use dry equipment |
| Wrong volume | Poor reading of the cylinder | Read at eye level |
