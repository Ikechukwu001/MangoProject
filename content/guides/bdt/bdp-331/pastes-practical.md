## What is a paste?

A **paste** is a **stiff semi-solid** that contains a **large proportion of finely powdered solids**, usually **25% or more**, spread in a **greasy base** (or sometimes a water-based one). It is firmer than an ointment.

| | Ointment | Paste |
|---|---|---|
| Powder content | None, or small | Large (about 25% or more) |
| Texture | Soft and greasy | Stiff and less greasy |
| Spreading | Spreads easily | Stays where it is put |
| Absorbs fluid | Little | Absorbs secretions |
| Use | Soften, protect, carry drugs | Protect, absorb, treat small patches |

## Uses

- To **protect** the skin, for example in napkin rash.
- To **absorb** moisture and discharges.
- To **treat small, well-defined areas** of skin disease such as eczema or psoriasis, because the paste stays on the patch and does not spread.
- To act as a **barrier** against irritants.

A paste is **not used on hairy areas**, because it is hard to remove, and not on **large areas** or **weeping wounds** unless directed.

## Example: zinc and salicylic acid paste

*Typical formula: salicylic acid 2%, zinc oxide 24%, starch 24%, white soft paraffin 50%. Use the formula your school gives you.*

### Scaling

*Make 50 g.*

1. Salicylic acid = 50 × 2 ÷ 100 = **1 g**
2. Zinc oxide = 50 × 24 ÷ 100 = **12 g**
3. Starch = 50 × 24 ÷ 100 = **12 g**
4. White soft paraffin = 50 × 50 ÷ 100 = **25 g**

**Check.** 1 + 12 + 12 + 25 = **50 g**. The percentages add to 100%: 2 + 24 + 24 + 50 = 100.

## Method

1. **Weigh** all the ingredients.
2. **Sieve or powder** the solids very finely, so there is no grit.
3. **Mix the powders together** on a tile with a spatula, starting with the smallest quantity (the salicylic acid) and adding the larger ones (geometric dilution).
4. **Soften the base** by working it with the spatula, or by **warming it very gently** if it is too stiff. Do not melt it unless the formula says.
5. **Add the powder to the base in portions**, working each portion in thoroughly until the paste is **smooth and even**.
6. **Pack** into a wide-mouthed jar, pressing out air gaps.
7. **Label** the jar.

### Why add the powder to the base?

Adding the powder to the base **in stages** wets each particle properly and gives a **smooth, uniform** paste. Adding all the base to all the powder at once makes lumps and gritty patches.

## Packing, labelling and storage

- Pack in a **wide-mouthed jar** with a tight lid, because the paste is stiff and hard to remove from a tube.
- Label: patient's name, name and strength of the paste, directions, **"For external use only"**, the date, and a **discard date**.
- Store in a **cool place**.
- Tell the patient: **wash hands** before and after use, apply with a **clean spatula or gloved finger**, and use a **thin layer**. Do not put it near the eyes or on broken skin unless told to do so.
- Salicylic acid can **irritate healthy skin** around the patch, so apply it only to the affected area.

## Faults and what to do

| Fault | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Gritty paste | The powders were too coarse, or not sieved | Powder and sieve them again |
| Lumps | Powder added all at once | Add in portions, mixing well |
| Paste too stiff | Too much powder, or a cold base | Check the weights, and warm the base very gently |
| Paste too soft | Too little powder or too much base | Check the weights |
| Uneven colour | Poor mixing | Continue mixing until smooth |
