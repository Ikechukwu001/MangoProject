## Extemporaneous and small-scale production

| | Extemporaneous dispensing | Small-scale production |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity | One prescription | A **batch** for several patients or a unit |
| When | When the prescription comes | In advance, as a stock |
| Records | Dispensing record | **Batch record** with a batch number |
| Checks | Pharmacist's check | **In-process and final checks** |
| Shelf life | Short | Set by the formula and stability data |

Hospital pharmacies often make **small batches** of products that are not available or are costly, such as mixtures, syrups and mouthwashes.

## Scaling a formula

Every ingredient is multiplied by the **same factor**.

```
scale factor = batch size ÷ master formula size
```

### Worked example

*A syrup has 125 mg of drug in each 5 mL. Make a 10-litre batch.*

1. 125 mg ÷ 5 mL = **25 mg per mL**
2. Batch = 10 litres = 10 000 mL
3. Drug = 25 × 10 000 = 250 000 mg = **250 g**

*The batch is filled into 100 mL bottles.*

- Number of bottles = 10 000 ÷ 100 = **100 bottles** (if there is no loss)

*96 bottles are filled.*

- Yield = 96 ÷ 100 × 100 = **96%**

Some loss is expected (liquid left in the vessel, pipes and filters). A low yield is investigated.

## Making each type

### Solutions

1. **Weigh** the solids, **measure** the liquids.
2. **Dissolve** in part of the vehicle in a **mixing tank**, with a stirrer, **crushing** large crystals first. Use gentle heat only if the formula allows.
3. **Filter** or strain if needed.
4. Add **liquids, flavours and preservatives**.
5. **Make up to volume**, and mix.

### Suspensions

1. Powder and **sieve** the solid.
2. Mix with the **suspending agent**, then wet and disperse in the vehicle.
3. Mix until smooth, and add the other ingredients.
4. **Make up to volume** and **keep stirring** while filling, so that the powder does not settle.

### Emulsions

1. Prepare the **oil phase** and the **water phase**, with the emulsifier in the right phase, and heat if the formula says.
2. Mix to form the **primary emulsion**.
3. Pass through a **homogeniser** or **colloid mill** to break the droplets into small ones.
4. **Cool**, add the **preservative** and flavour, and fill.

## Equipment

| Equipment | Use |
|---|---|
| Mixing tank with stirrer (stainless steel) | Dissolving and mixing |
| Jacketed vessel | Heating or cooling |
| Homogeniser, colloid mill | Emulsions and fine suspensions |
| Filter press, cartridge filter | Clarifying |
| Filling machine | Fills bottles to a set volume |
| Capping and labelling equipment | Closing and labelling |

All equipment must be **clean**, **suitable** (stainless steel, glass) and **dedicated** or properly cleaned between products.

## In-process and final checks

- **Appearance**: colour, clarity, particles, uniformity.
- **pH**, where it matters.
- **Relative density (specific gravity)**.
- **Fill volume**: check bottles at intervals.
- **Closure** and **leaks**.
- **Label**: correct product, strength, batch number and expiry.
- **Samples** are kept, and tests for quality are done as the protocol states.

## Records and hygiene

- Use an approved **master formula** and a **batch record** signed at each step.
- Give every batch a **batch number** and **expiry date**.
- Keep **clean working areas**, wear clean clothes and gloves, and use **purified water**.
- **Label** the equipment with the product being made, and **clear the area** before starting the next one, so that there are no mix-ups.
