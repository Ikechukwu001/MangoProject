## Quality in medicines

A medicine must have the **right drug, in the right amount, free from harmful contamination, in a stable form** that works as it should. Patients cannot judge this by looking, so quality must be **built in** at every step, not just tested at the end.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality assurance (QA) | The **whole system** of arrangements to make sure medicines are of the quality needed for their use. It includes GMP, QC and more |
| Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) | The part of QA that ensures products are **consistently produced and controlled** to the required standards |
| Quality control (QC) | **Sampling, testing and checking** of materials and products, with documents and release procedures |
| Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) | A **written, approved set of steps** for doing a task the same way each time |
| Batch | A quantity of product made in one process, so that it is **uniform** |
| Batch record | The **full record** of how a batch was made and tested |

QA is the umbrella. GMP and QC are parts of it.

## The main areas of GMP

| Area | What it means |
|---|---|
| Personnel | Enough trained, healthy, clean staff. Clear duties. Protective clothing and hygiene. Training records |
| Premises | Designed, built and kept clean to prevent mix-ups and contamination, with separate areas for stores, production, testing and packing |
| Equipment | Suitable, cleaned, calibrated, maintained and recorded |
| Materials | Bought from approved suppliers, tested, labelled, stored properly, and used by FEFO. Quarantine for materials not yet approved |
| Production | Done to written instructions, with checks at key steps, and measures to avoid mix-ups |
| Documentation | SOPs, specifications, batch records and logs |
| Quality control | Testing, release and retained samples |
| Complaints and recalls | A system to receive complaints and withdraw a faulty batch quickly |
| Self-inspection | Regular internal audits to find and correct problems |

## Preventing contamination and mix-ups

- **Separate areas** and **clear flow** of people and materials.
- **Line clearance**: before a new batch, remove all materials, labels and documents of the previous product.
- **Cleaning** of equipment with records, and **status labels** such as "clean" or "in use".
- **Labels** for every container.
- **Protective clothing**, hand washing and **no eating, drinking or smoking** in production areas.
- **Pest control** and **waste control**.
- **Quarantine** of materials until they are approved.

## Documentation

**"If it is not written down, it did not happen."**

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| SOPs | Do each task correctly and the same way each time |
| Master formula and processing instructions | The approved recipe and method |
| Batch record | Shows that each step was done: materials used, weights, who did it, who checked, and results |
| Specifications | The limits that materials and products must meet |
| Logs | Cleaning, equipment use, temperature and training |

Rules for records: write **at the time**, in **ink**, **sign and date**, never erase, and **correct** by a single line with initials and a reason.

## Quality control

QC covers:

- **Raw materials**: identity and quality, checked before use.
- **In-process control**: checks during production, such as weight, pH, fill volume and appearance.
- **Finished products**: tests for strength, purity, appearance, dissolution or disintegration, and microbial quality, as needed.
- **Packaging materials and labels**: checked for correctness.
- **Retained samples** kept for use if a problem appears later.
- **Stability testing** to confirm the shelf life.

A batch is **released** for sale **only** when QC confirms that it meets the specifications, and the **batch record** has been reviewed.

### Worked examples

**Yield.** *A batch should give 100 kg. 96 kg is obtained.*

- Yield = 96 ÷ 100 × 100 = **96%**

Each product has limits for acceptable yield. A yield that is **too low or too high** is investigated, as it may mean loss, error or a mix-up.

**Label claim.** *A tablet is labelled 500 mg. A test finds 485 mg.*

- Percentage of label claim = 485 ÷ 500 × 100 = **97%**

Pharmacopoeias set limits, for example a range around 100%.

## Complaints and recalls

- Every **complaint** about quality is **recorded and investigated**.
- If a product is **faulty**, the batch is **recalled**: stopped from sale and **withdrawn** from wholesalers, pharmacies and patients.
- Batch numbers and distribution records make a **quick recall** possible.
- The cause is found, and **corrective action** is taken to stop it happening again.

## Self-inspection and regulation

Companies carry out **self-inspections** and external **audits**. In Nigeria, **NAFDAC** inspects manufacturing sites and requires **GMP** as part of registration and licensing. A technician working in manufacturing or in a hospital pharmacy that makes products should know and follow the site's GMP and SOPs.
