## Industrial processing

A factory makes medicines in large batches. To control quality, manufacture is divided into basic steps called **unit operations**. Examples are **milling, mixing, sieving, extraction, distillation, granulation, drying, evaporation, filtration and clarification**. Each is studied on its own, and the same operation is used for many products.

The small-scale methods you met in the dispensing practicals are the same operations, done in a mortar, beaker or sieve. In industry the equipment is bigger, and it is controlled and recorded.

## Fluid flow

**Fluid flow** is the movement of **liquids and gases** through **pipes, pumps and valves**.

- **Pumps** move liquids from tanks to mixers, fillers and storage.
- **Viscosity** is how thick a liquid is. Thick liquids such as syrups and creams flow slowly and need **stronger pumps** and **wider pipes**.
- Pipes and pumps must be of **suitable material**, such as stainless steel, and **easy to clean**, so that one product does not contaminate the next.
- **Air bubbles** and leaks must be avoided.

## Material flow

**Material flow** is the movement of **solids**, such as powders, granules and tablets, and of containers and packs.

- **Gravity** feeds material from a **hopper** down to the next machine.
- **Conveyors**, **chutes** and **scoops** carry material between machines and rooms.
- Powders can **cake**, form **bridges** in hoppers, or **separate** by size and weight, so the flow must be controlled.
- Good layout keeps raw materials, in-process material and finished goods **apart** and prevents mix-ups.

## Size reduction

**Size reduction** (milling, comminution) makes particles **smaller**.

### Reasons

- Small particles **dissolve faster**, because they have **more surface area** for their weight.
- They are **absorbed faster** from the gut.
- They **mix more evenly**, and flow and pack better.
- They give **smoother** creams, ointments and suspensions.
- They help **extraction** of drugs from plant material.

### Methods

| Method | How it works | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting | Blades cut the material | Fibrous plant material |
| Compression | Material is crushed between surfaces | Hard, brittle solids |
| Impact | Material is struck by hammers or balls | Many brittle materials |
| Attrition | Particles are rubbed against each other or a surface | Fine grinding |

### Mills and equipment

| Equipment | Main principle |
|---|---|
| Mortar and pestle | Impact and attrition. Small scale |
| Cutter mill | Cutting |
| Roller mill | Compression |
| Hammer mill | Impact |
| Ball mill | Impact and attrition |
| Fluid energy mill | Particles collide in a jet of air, giving very fine powders (micronisation) |

**Cautions:** some drugs are damaged by **heat** produced in milling, and dust can be a **health and explosion risk**. Operators wear masks, and the equipment has dust control.

## Size separation

**Size separation** sorts particles into groups by size. The usual method is **sieving**.

- A **sieve** is a mesh. Particles **smaller than the openings** pass through, and larger ones stay on top.
- Sieves are described by the **size of the openings** or by the **mesh number**, which is the number of openings per unit length. A **higher mesh number means smaller openings**, and a **finer** sieve.
- Sieves are **stacked**, with the coarsest on top, and **shaken** to separate a powder into several size groups.
- Sieving also **removes lumps and foreign material** and **mixes** powders.

### Worked example

*100 g of a powder is passed through a sieve. 82 g pass through and 18 g stay on the sieve.*

- Percentage passed = 82 ÷ 100 × 100 = **82%**
- Percentage retained = 100 − 82 = **18%**

The retained powder may be **milled again** and **re-sieved**.

## Powder grades

Pharmacopoeias describe powders by the sieves they pass, for example **coarse**, **moderately coarse**, **fine** and **very fine**. The more particles that pass a fine sieve, the finer the powder. Use the grades in your textbook.
