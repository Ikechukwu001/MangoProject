## What is an aerosol?

An **aerosol** is a product **packed under pressure** in a container with a **propellant**. When the valve is opened, the propellant forces the contents out as a **fine spray, mist, foam or stream**. The term is also used for the container itself, the **pressurised pack**.

## Parts of an aerosol

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Container | A strong metal (or sometimes plastic-coated glass) can that holds the pressure |
| Valve | Opens and closes to release the contents. A **metering valve** releases a fixed dose |
| Actuator | The button or nozzle that is pressed, and shapes the spray |
| Dip tube | Carries the product from the bottom of the can to the valve |
| Product concentrate | The drug, solvent and other ingredients |
| Propellant | Gas that creates the pressure and pushes the product out |

## Propellants

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Liquefied gases | Stored as a liquid under pressure, and turn to gas when released. Hydrocarbons (such as propane and butane) are common, but they are **flammable**. Hydrofluoroalkanes are used in many inhalers |
| Compressed gases | Gases such as nitrogen or carbon dioxide. The pressure falls as the can empties |
| Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) | Used in the past. **Phased out** because they harm the **ozone layer** |

## Types of aerosol

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Space sprays | Fine mist in the air, such as air fresheners and insecticides |
| Surface sprays | Applied to a surface, such as skin sprays, wound sprays and antiseptic sprays |
| Foams | Product comes out as a foam, such as some creams and shaving products |
| Metered-dose inhalers (MDIs) | Deliver a **measured dose** of drug to the lungs with each puff, such as asthma inhalers |

## Small-scale production

Aerosols are made in **specially controlled** conditions, because of the pressure and the fire risk.

### Cold filling

1. The **concentrate** and the propellant are **cooled** so that the propellant is liquid at normal pressure.
2. The cooled concentrate is put into the container.
3. The cooled propellant is added.
4. The **valve is fitted and crimped on** straight away.

### Pressure filling

1. The **concentrate** is placed in the container.
2. The **valve is fitted and crimped on**.
3. The **propellant is forced in through the valve** under pressure.

Both methods need the right **equipment and training**, with **ventilation**, **no ignition sources** and **checks for leaks**. Finished cans are checked, often by passing them through a **warm water bath** to find leaks.

Other **non-pressurised sprays**, such as pump sprays, are also used. They do not need a propellant and are simpler to make.

## Safety

- **Do not heat**, burn or **puncture** an aerosol, even when it looks empty.
- **Keep away from flames and heat**, and do not use near a fire.
- **Store in a cool place**, out of direct sunlight, below the temperature given on the label (often 50 °C).
- **Keep out of the reach of children.**
- **Do not spray** near the eyes or face unless the label says so.
- **Use in a ventilated place.**
- **Do not inhale** deliberately, which is dangerous.

## Patient advice for inhalers

- **Shake** the inhaler, if directed.
- Breathe out, put the mouthpiece in the mouth, **press and breathe in slowly**, and **hold the breath** for about 10 seconds.
- Wait **about a minute** between puffs.
- A **spacer** helps, especially for children.
- **Rinse the mouth** after using a steroid inhaler.
- Check the **dose counter**, and replace the inhaler before it runs out.

**Example.** *The inhaler has 200 puffs. The dose is 2 puffs twice daily.*

- Daily puffs = 2 × 2 = 4
- Days of supply = 200 ÷ 4 = **50 days**
