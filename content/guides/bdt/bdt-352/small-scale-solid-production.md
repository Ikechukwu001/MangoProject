## Solid dosage forms in batches

The theory of tablets and capsules, including the types, excipients and coating, is in BDT 152. This lesson looks at **making batches** and **checking their quality**.

## Making tablets

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Weighing | Each ingredient is weighed by one person and checked by another |
| 2. Milling and sieving | Powders are made fine and even |
| 3. Mixing | The drug and excipients are mixed. Small amounts are mixed first (geometric dilution) |
| 4. Granulation | Wet or dry granulation, or **direct compression** if the powder flows and compresses well without it |
| 5. Drying and screening | Wet granules are dried and screened |
| 6. Lubrication | A lubricant is added in the last mix |
| 7. Compression | A tablet press compresses the granules in dies, using punches |
| 8. Coating (if needed) | The tablets are coated for taste, protection or release |
| 9. Checking | In-process and final tests |
| 10. Packing and labelling | Tablets are packed with batch number and expiry date |

### Batch quantity

**Example.** *10 000 tablets, each with 500 mg of paracetamol.*

- Drug = 10 000 × 500 mg = 5 000 000 mg = 5 000 g = **5 kg**

Excipients are added to the formula amounts, and the tablet weight is set so that each tablet has exactly the dose.

### Small presses

In a small pharmacy or school, a **single-punch tablet press** or a **hand-operated press** may be used. Industrial presses are rotary machines making many thousands of tablets an hour.

## Making capsules

1. **Weigh** the ingredients, and **sieve** and **mix** them.
2. **Fill** the powder into the lower part (body) of each shell, by hand, with a **capsule-filling device** or a machine.
3. **Close** the capsules by fitting on the caps.
4. **Clean** the outside to remove dust.
5. **Check**: weight, appearance and closure.
6. **Pack** and label.

The **size** of the capsule is chosen to fit the dose. See the lesson on capsules in BDT 152.

## In-process and final tests

| Test | What it shows | How |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Colour, shape, cracks, chips, spots | Looking at a sample |
| Weight variation | That each tablet or capsule has about the same amount of drug | Weigh a sample of units, find the average, and compare each one with it |
| Hardness | The strength of a tablet | A hardness tester measures the force needed to crush a tablet |
| Friability | How well the tablet resists chipping and crumbling | Tumble in a drum, and weigh before and after |
| Disintegration | That the tablet breaks up in fluid in a set time | A basket is raised and lowered in fluid at body temperature |
| Dissolution | How fast and how much drug dissolves | Tablet is stirred in fluid, and samples are tested |
| Content of drug | That the right amount of drug is present | A chemical assay |

### Weight variation

The limits depend on the average weight. A commonly quoted set for uncoated tablets is:

| Average weight | Allowed deviation |
|---|---|
| 80 mg or less | ±10% |
| More than 80 mg and less than 250 mg | ±7.5% |
| 250 mg or more | ±5% |

**Example.** *The average weight is 200 mg and the limit is ±7.5%.*

- 7.5% of 200 = 15 mg
- Acceptable range = **185 mg to 215 mg**

Use the limits in the pharmacopoeia or textbook that your school follows.

### Friability

```
friability (%) = (initial weight − final weight) ÷ initial weight × 100
```

**Example.** *6.50 g of tablets weighs 6.45 g after the test.*

- Loss = 6.50 − 6.45 = 0.05 g
- Friability = 0.05 ÷ 6.50 × 100 = **0.77%**

A loss of **not more than about 1%** is usually accepted.

### Disintegration

Uncoated tablets are generally expected to break up within a short set time, often **15 minutes**, and coated tablets longer. Check the limit for each type in your textbook.

## Precautions

- **Control dust**, with masks, extraction and cleaning.
- **Prevent mix-ups**: one product at a time, **line clearance** between batches, clear labels.
- **Keep dry**, because moisture ruins tablets and capsules.
- **Record** each step, and keep **retained samples**.
- Check that **punches and dies** are clean and undamaged.
