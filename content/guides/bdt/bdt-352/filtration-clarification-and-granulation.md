## Filtration

**Filtration** separates **solid particles from a liquid** by passing the liquid through a **porous filter medium**. The clear liquid that passes through is the **filtrate**. The solid left behind is the **residue**.

### Filter media

| Medium | Notes |
|---|---|
| Filter paper | Common for small amounts. Different grades have different pore sizes |
| Cloth or muslin | Coarse filtering, such as straining mixtures |
| Cotton wool and glass wool | Plugs in funnels for rough clearing |
| Sintered glass | Fine, reusable, resists chemicals |
| Membrane filters | Thin sheets with very small, exact pore sizes. Used for sterile filtration |

A **filter aid**, such as kieselguhr, is sometimes added to stop slimy particles blocking the filter.

### Rate of filtration

Filtration is **faster** when:

- the **filter area** is larger;
- the liquid is **thinner** (less viscous), for example when it is warm;
- the **pressure difference** across the filter is greater, for example by using **vacuum** or **pressure**;
- the filter is **more open**, with larger pores;
- the **layer of residue** (cake) on the filter is thin.

**Example.** *500 mL is filtered in 25 minutes.*

- Rate = 500 ÷ 25 = **20 mL per minute**

### Sterile filtration

Germs are very small. To remove them, a **membrane filter** with pores of about **0.22 micrometre** is used, with **aseptic technique**. It is used for heat-sensitive solutions, such as some eye drops and injections, that cannot be sterilised by heat.

## Clarification

**Clarification** makes a liquid **clear** by removing **small amounts of fine suspended matter**.

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| Settling and decanting | The solid sinks, and the clear liquid is poured off |
| Centrifugation | Spinning forces the solids to the bottom |
| Filtration | Passing through a filter |
| Using a fining agent | Something is added that carries the particles down, such as in clarifying juices |

Clarified liquids look clear but are not necessarily sterile.

## Granulation

**Granulation** turns **powders into larger granules**. Granules **flow better**, **do not separate** into their separate ingredients, are **less dusty**, and **compress well** into tablets. See the lesson on powders and granules in BDT 152.

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| Wet granulation | The powders are mixed with a **liquid binder** to a damp mass, which is **passed through a screen**, **dried** and **screened again** |
| Dry granulation | The powder is **compressed** into large tablets (slugs) or sheets, then **broken up** and screened. No liquid is used. Suits drugs that are damaged by moisture or heat |
| Fluid bed granulation | Powder is kept suspended in a stream of warm air and **sprayed** with a binder, so it granulates and dries in one machine |

### Yield

```
yield (%) = actual amount obtained ÷ theoretical amount × 100
```

**Example.** *A batch should give 50 kg of granules. 47 kg is obtained.*

- Yield = 47 ÷ 50 × 100 = **94%**

Some loss is normal (dust, material on equipment). A low yield is **investigated**.
