## Creams, ointments and pastes in batches

The small-scale methods are in BDP 331. In a batch:

- **Jacketed mixing vessels** (steam or hot water in the jacket) **melt** the bases and **mix** with a stirrer or **paddle**.
- For **ointments**, the base is melted, the drug is added, and the mixture is stirred while it cools.
- For **creams**, the oil phase and water phase are heated to the same temperature and mixed, and then stirred as they cool.
- For **pastes**, the powders are mixed into the softened base in a mixer that can handle stiff material.
- An **ointment mill** (roller mill) passes the product between rollers to make it **smooth** and **free from grit**.
- The product is **filled into jars or tubes** by a filling machine, which weighs or measures each one.

Quality checks include **appearance**, **smoothness**, **consistency**, **fill weight**, **type of emulsion** for creams, and **preservative** content for creams.

## Gels

A **gel** is a **semi-solid** in which a **gelling agent** forms a **network** that holds a **liquid**. Gels are often clear and not greasy, and they are used on the skin and in the mouth, eyes and nose.

| Gelling agent | Notes |
|---|---|
| Tragacanth | Natural gum |
| Sodium alginate | Seaweed-based, forms smooth gels |
| Methylcellulose and similar cellulose derivatives | Synthetic, used widely |
| Carbomers | Synthetic polymers that gel when neutralised |
| Gelatin | Used for jellies, and for some suppository bases |

**Method (general):** the gelling agent is **sprinkled slowly** on the water, **stirred** and **left to swell**, so it does not form lumps. Other ingredients and a **preservative** are added. Gels are **water-based**, so germs can grow, and they need a preservative.

## Jellies

A **jelly** is a gel, usually **water-based and clear**, that is used for **lubrication** (for example for catheters or examinations) or to apply drugs. It is made with a gelling agent, **glycerol** and a preservative. A jelly used for medical procedures may need to be **sterile**.

## Suppositories

The theory is in BDT 152. This lesson covers **moulding**.

### Bases

| Base | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cocoa butter (theobroma oil) | Melts at about body temperature. Must be heated gently to avoid changing its form |
| Glycerol-gelatin | Dissolves slowly. Used for pessaries |
| Macrogols (polyethylene glycols) | Dissolve in body fluids. Do not melt at body temperature |

### Making suppositories by moulding

1. **Calculate** the base needed (see below).
2. **Prepare the moulds**: clean, dry and, if needed, **lightly lubricated**. Cocoa butter usually needs none.
3. **Melt the base** gently on a water bath. For cocoa butter, melt only part and let the rest dissolve in the heat, so it does not overheat.
4. **Add the drug**, and mix. A powder is first made fine, and is mixed in smoothly.
5. **Pour** into the moulds, slightly **overfilling**, **while stirring** to keep the drug evenly spread.
6. **Cool** until set. Do not freeze.
7. **Trim** the extra from the top, **remove** and **inspect** them.
8. **Wrap or pack** and label. Store in a **cool place**.

### Displacement value

The drug takes up space in the mould, and therefore **displaces some base**. The **displacement value (DV)** is the weight of drug that displaces **1 g of base**.

```
base displaced = total weight of drug ÷ DV
base needed = (number × mould capacity) − base displaced
```

### Worked example

*Make 12 suppositories, each with 200 mg of a drug. The mould holds 2 g of base. The DV is 1.5.*

1. Total drug = 12 × 0.2 = **2.4 g**
2. Base displaced = 2.4 ÷ 1.5 = **1.6 g**
3. Base needed = (12 × 2) − 1.6 = 24 − 1.6 = **22.4 g**

In practice, extra is made to allow for loss (for example, enough for 14 suppositories).

## Precautions

- **Hygiene** and **clean equipment**, especially for water-based gels and creams.
- **Gentle heat**, because high heat can damage the base and the drug.
- **Even mixing**, so each unit has the right dose.
- **Correct moulds** and **careful pouring**.
- **Cool storage**, because suppositories and ointments soften in heat.
- **Labelling**: "For external use only" or "Insert into the rectum or vagina as directed", with the date and the discard date.
