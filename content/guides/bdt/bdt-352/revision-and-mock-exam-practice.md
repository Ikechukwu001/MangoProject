## Topic summary

### 1. Industrial processing: unit operations

- **Unit operations** are the basic steps of manufacture.
- **Fluid flow**: liquids and gases moved by pumps and pipes. **Material flow**: solids moved by gravity, conveyors and hoppers.
- **Size reduction**: cutting, compression, impact, attrition. Mills: cutter, roller, hammer, ball, fluid energy.
- **Size separation**: sieving. A higher mesh number means a finer sieve.
- **Extraction**: maceration, percolation, infusion, decoction, digestion, Soxhlet.
- **Distillation**: simple, fractional, steam, vacuum. **Evaporation**: vapour not collected.
- **Drying**: tray, fluid bed, spray, vacuum, freeze. Check by **loss on drying**.
- **Filtration**, **clarification** and **granulation** (wet, dry, fluid bed).

### 2. Small-scale production

- **Liquids**: solutions, suspensions, emulsions. Scale every ingredient by the same factor. Check appearance, volume, pH and relative density.
- **Solids**: tablet steps from weighing to packing, and capsule filling. Tests: weight variation, hardness, friability, disintegration, dissolution.
- **Semi-solids**: creams, ointments, pastes, gels, jellies and suppositories by moulding, with displacement value.
- **Aerosols**: container, valve, actuator, dip tube, concentrate and propellant. Safety rules.

### 3. Packaging, stability and storage

- Packaging protects, contains, identifies, informs and is convenient. **Primary**, **secondary** and **tertiary** packs.
- Materials: glass (amber for light), plastics, metals, paper, rubber. Closures: child-resistant and tamper-evident.
- **Stability**: chemical, physical, microbiological. Factors: temperature, moisture, light, oxygen, pH, germs, container, time.
- **Shelf life** and expiry. **Storage**: cool, dry, away from light. **Cold chain** at 2 to 8 °C.

### 4. QA, QC and GMP

- **QA** is the whole system. **GMP** is making products to the standard. **QC** is testing.
- GMP areas: personnel, premises, equipment, materials, production, documentation, quality control, complaints and recalls, self-inspection.
- **Batch records** and **batch numbers** allow tracing and recall.

## Formulas to know

```
scale factor = batch size ÷ master formula size
yield (%) = actual ÷ theoretical × 100
loss on drying (%) = weight lost ÷ original weight × 100
friability (%) = (initial − final) ÷ initial × 100
base for suppositories = (number × mould capacity) − (total drug ÷ displacement value)
tincture 1 in n: drug (g) = volume (mL) ÷ n
concentration factor = starting volume ÷ final volume
percentage of label claim = amount found ÷ labelled amount × 100
```

## Worked revision examples

**Scaling.** *Master formula for 500 mL contains 25 g of drug. Make 20 litres.*

1. Scale factor = 20 000 ÷ 500 = **40**
2. Drug = 25 × 40 = **1 000 g = 1 kg**

**Displacement value.** *10 suppositories, each with 300 mg of drug (DV 1.5), in 2 g moulds.*

1. Drug = 10 × 0.3 = 3 g
2. Base displaced = 3 ÷ 1.5 = 2 g
3. Base needed = (10 × 2) − 2 = **18 g**

**Friability.** *10.00 g of tablets weighs 9.95 g after the test.*

- (10.00 − 9.95) ÷ 10.00 × 100 = **0.5%**

## Common mistakes in the examination

- Mixing up **QA**, **QC** and **GMP**.
- Mixing up **distillation** and **evaporation**.
- Forgetting **units**, or giving **g** when the answer is **kg**.
- Not scaling **all** ingredients.
- Forgetting that the drug displaces some base in suppositories.
- Writing one word for a **list** question. Give the required number of points.
- Leaving out **working**. Show each step.

## Planning a long answer

For a question such as "Describe the production of tablets":

1. **Define** the product briefly.
2. **List the steps in order**, with one line for each.
3. **Name the equipment** at each step.
4. **State the quality checks** and the tests.
5. **Finish** with packing, labelling and storage.
