## Extraction

**Extraction** removes the **useful constituents** from a drug, most often a plant or animal material, by using a **solvent**. The solvent is called the **menstruum**. The part left behind is the **marc**. The liquid obtained is the **extract**, such as a **tincture** or **liquid extract**.

The choice of menstruum depends on what dissolves the wanted substance: **water**, **alcohol**, **mixtures of alcohol and water**, or others.

### Methods

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Maceration | The coarse drug is soaked in the menstruum in a closed container for several days, with stirring, then pressed and filtered | Simple, suits small scale |
| Percolation | The menstruum is allowed to trickle slowly through the drug packed in a cone-shaped vessel (percolator) | Gives a more complete extraction |
| Infusion | Hot water is poured on the drug and left for a short time | Fresh infusions for soft parts, used quickly |
| Decoction | The drug is boiled in water for a period | For hard parts such as bark and roots. Not for heat-sensitive substances |
| Digestion | Maceration at a gentle heat | Speeds up extraction |
| Soxhlet extraction | Solvent is boiled, condensed, and repeatedly passed over the drug | Efficient, used in laboratories |

### Strength of an extract

**Example.** *A tincture is made as 1 in 5. How much drug is needed for 500 mL?*

- 500 ÷ 5 = **100 g** of drug
- Strength = 100 ÷ 500 × 100 = **20% w/v**

## Distillation

**Distillation** separates liquids by **boiling** and **condensing** the vapour. The substance with the **lowest boiling point** turns to vapour first, then is cooled in a **condenser** and collected as the **distillate**.

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Simple distillation | Purified water from tap water, and liquids with very different boiling points |
| Fractional distillation | Separating liquids with close boiling points, using a column |
| Steam distillation | Volatile oils from plants, such as peppermint oil. Steam carries the oil over at a temperature below its boiling point, so it is not damaged |
| Vacuum distillation | Heat-sensitive substances, because lowering the pressure lowers the boiling point |

Products of distillation include **purified water**, **aromatic waters**, **volatile oils** and **alcohol**.

## Evaporation

**Evaporation** removes **solvent** from a solution, usually water, by heating, so that the solution becomes **more concentrated** or a solid is left. Unlike distillation, the vapour is **not collected**.

- On a small scale, a **dish on a water bath** is used.
- On a large scale, **evaporators** with steam jackets, often under **vacuum**, are used.
- **Vacuum evaporation** works at a lower temperature, which protects heat-sensitive drugs.

**Example.** *200 mL of a solution is evaporated to 50 mL.*

- Concentration factor = 200 ÷ 50 = **4 times**, if no solute is lost.

## Drying

**Drying** removes **moisture** from solids, granules, powders and extracts. It prevents decay, helps storage, and is needed before tabletting or filling.

| Method | How it works | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Tray (oven) drying | Material on trays in warm air | Many powders, granules and plant materials |
| Fluid bed drying | Warm air blown up through granules, keeping them suspended | Granules, quick and even |
| Spray drying | A liquid is sprayed into hot air and dries to fine particles | Extracts, instant powders |
| Vacuum drying | Drying under reduced pressure at low temperature | Heat-sensitive materials |
| Freeze drying (lyophilisation) | Frozen material loses ice by sublimation under vacuum | Vaccines, antibiotics, proteins |
| Air or sun drying | Natural warmth and air | Plant material. Needs protection from dust, rain and sunlight damage |

### Loss on drying

**Loss on drying (LOD)** is the percentage of weight lost when a sample is dried.

```
LOD (%) = weight lost ÷ original weight × 100
```

**Example.** *A 10 g sample loses 0.5 g.*

- LOD = 0.5 ÷ 10 × 100 = **5%**

If the moisture is too high, the product may mould, cake or break down. Pharmacopoeias set a limit for each product.

## Protecting the drug

- **Low temperature**, **vacuum** and **freeze drying** protect heat-sensitive drugs.
- Dry in **clean air**, and avoid contamination.
- Store dried products in **tight containers** away from moisture.
