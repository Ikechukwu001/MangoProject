## What is a mixture?

A **mixture** is a **liquid for taking by mouth**, in which one or more drugs are **dissolved or suspended** in a **vehicle**. The vehicle is usually **water**. A mixture may also contain a **sweetener** such as syrup, a **flavour**, a **colour** and a **preservative**.

Mixtures are measured with a **5 mL medicine spoon**, an **oral syringe** or a **measuring cup**. Common doses are 5 mL and 10 mL. A mixture is supplied in a bottle, usually glass or plastic, with a tight closure.

## Types of mixture

| Type | What it contains | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Solution mixture | Every ingredient dissolves. The liquid is clear | A simple antacid or expectorant mixture in which everything dissolves |
| Suspension mixture | An insoluble powder is spread through the liquid. The liquid is cloudy | A chalk or kaolin mixture |

Suspension mixtures are divided again according to how the powder behaves in the liquid.

### Diffusible and indiffusible solids

| | Diffusible solids | Indiffusible solids |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of the powder | Light and fine | Heavy, or very fine and hard to wet |
| In the liquid | Spreads easily and settles slowly | Sinks quickly and may form a hard cake |
| After shaking | Spreads evenly again, and stays spread long enough to measure a dose | Does not stay spread unless something holds it |
| Suspending agent | **Not needed** | **Needed** |
| Examples | Light kaolin, light magnesium carbonate, magnesium trisilicate | Chalk, calamine, zinc oxide |

Whether a powder counts as diffusible can depend on its **particle size and weight**, so use the classification in the textbook your school follows.

### Suspending agents

A **suspending agent** makes the liquid **thicker** and coats the particles, so they **settle slowly** and are easy to shake back into the liquid. Common suspending agents are:

- **compound tragacanth powder**
- **tragacanth** and **acacia**
- **sodium carboxymethylcellulose**
- **bentonite**

The amount of suspending agent is taken from the formula in the pharmacopoeia or reference, and it should never be guessed.

## How mixtures are prepared

The practical steps are covered in the dispensing practicals. In outline:

**Solution mixture**

1. Weigh or measure each ingredient.
2. Dissolve the solids in part of the vehicle, using the ingredient that dissolves most slowly first.
3. Strain or filter if the liquid is not clear.
4. Add the liquid ingredients and flavour.
5. Make up to the full volume with the vehicle and transfer to the bottle.

**Suspension mixture**

1. Powder the insoluble solid finely in a mortar.
2. For an indiffusible solid, mix the powder with the **suspending agent** first.
3. Add the vehicle a little at a time, stirring to a smooth cream, then add the rest.
4. Add the other ingredients, then transfer to a measuring cylinder.
5. Rinse the mortar with some of the vehicle, add the rinsings and make up to volume.
6. Transfer to the bottle and label it **"Shake the bottle well before use"**.

## Problems to watch for

| Problem | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Precipitate forms | Two ingredients react or one is not soluble enough | Tell the pharmacist. Do not dispense it |
| Powder settles and cakes | Indiffusible solid without enough suspending agent | Use the right suspending agent at the stated amount |
| Germs grow | Water-based mixture with no or weak preservative | Use clean equipment, a preservative if suitable, and a short discard date |
| Colour or smell changes | The drug is breaking down | Do not dispense it |

## Shelf life, labelling and storage

Water supports the growth of germs and the breakdown of drugs, so **water-based mixtures have a short shelf life**. The pharmacist sets the **discard date**, and the label states it. See the lesson on extemporaneous terms.

A mixture label carries:

- the name and strength of the mixture, and the patient's name;
- the directions for use, in plain words, and the dose volume;
- the date of preparation and the **discard date**;
- **"Shake the bottle well before use"** for every suspension;
- the storage advice, such as "Keep in a cool place" or "Store in a refrigerator".

Give the patient a **5 mL measuring spoon** or **oral syringe**, because kitchen spoons vary in size.

## Calculations

**Example 1: volume to supply.** *Mixture, 10 mL three times a day for 5 days.*

- 10 × 3 × 5 = **150 mL**

**Example 2: drug needed.** *Each 10 mL dose contains 500 mg of drug. Make 150 mL.*

1. Number of doses = 150 ÷ 10 = 15
2. Drug needed = 15 × 500 mg = 7 500 mg = **7.5 g**

**Example 3: number of spoonfuls.** *How many 5 mL spoonfuls are in 150 mL?*

- 150 ÷ 5 = **30 spoonfuls**

**Example 4: strength of the mixture.** *7.5 g of drug in 150 mL.*

- 7.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = **5% w/v**
